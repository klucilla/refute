## 4. The frozen design

### 4.1 The claim

**What it says:** among the TOIs of the confirmation half, the v0.5 battery refutes, on the
TOI's own signal, a larger share of TOIs dispositioned FP by the TFOPWG than of TOIs matched to
TESS-discovered confirmed planets, while its rate on those planets stays low.

**What it measures:** **agreement** of the battery (with its limits label) with the TFOPWG
dispositions, on photometry that overlaps theirs: the dispositions rest partly on the same TESS
data and similar diagnostics. **It never measures that the battery "detects false positives".**
A SURVIVED verdict is not independent validation; most FPs are expected not to be refuted (in
part A, 14 of 21 ended INCONCLUSIVE).

The claim is `calibration/v0.5.6/claim.yaml` (`tess-v0.5.6-part-b-fp-agreement`). Its verdict is
computed only by `calibration/v0.5.6/part_b_evaluate.py`.

### 4.2 Strata and pool

From the **confirmation half** of the frame, by its TIC split (never used in part A):
- **`fp`:** the open arm, disposition FP;
- **`planet`:** the planet arm (exactly one PSCompPars planet discovered by TESS, period within
  1 %). Its rows are mostly CP; "confirmed" means the PSCompPars match of the frame's date.
- **Not drawn:** FA (excluded), PC, APC, and the open arm's KP and CP. KP and CP are described from
  part A's records only (`part-b-prep.md`, section 2), never compared.
- **Exposed TICs are left out** (section 4.4).

The pool after the exclusion is **`planet` 197, `fp` 411** (frame counts). It is written as the
claim's targets attachment `pool-targets.yaml` before the lock.

**A bias, stated.** Most of the exclusion comes from one file: v0.2.1's `geometry-list.json`
(902 frame TICs), a per-TIC record of geometry checks that v0.2.1 made from MAST metadata on
planets and false positives. The excluded TICs are therefore not a random part of either stratum,
and the claim's scope is the strata **after** the exclusion.

### 4.3 Sample size and draw

- **150 per stratum, at least 120.** In each stratum, the TICs sorted by TIC are shuffled with
  `random.Random(seed_s)`, where `seed_s = int(sha256(randomness_hex + "|v0.5.6|part-b|" + stratum), 16)`.
  The TICs are walked in that order and checked from MAST **metadata only**, with part A's listing
  and filters (SPOC, exactly 120 s, `*lc.fits`, the TIC itself, sectors up to 107, at least two
  predicted transits with a 0.01-day edge margin), until 150 are eligible.
  - The draw is `complete` at 150, and `short` at 120 to 149 with the pool exhausted.
  - Below 120 it is `incomplete`, and the claim is **INCONCLUSIVE** without a run.
  - For scale, part A found 120 eligible of 123 checked in the planet arm and 120 of 140 in the
    open arm.
- **The randomness:** drand mainnet, its default chain (`refute.drand`), with the BLS signature
  verified. The round is the first one published **at least 10 minutes after the lock commit is on
  GitHub**. "On GitHub" is read as the creation time of the lock commit's CI push run: workflow
  `ci.yml`, event `push`, `head_sha` the lock commit, run attempt 1, from the GitHub API, as in
  v0.5. So the lock commit is pushed as the head of its own push. No such run, or more than one,
  stops the step for the maintainer.
- **The round is bound to the lock.**
  - The round record keeps the lock commit, the SHA-256 of its lock file (equal to the lock on
    disk), and the CI run's id, URL and creation time, with the SHA-256 of GitHub's answer.
  - The draw **recomputes** the round from the commit and the CI run before fetching the beacon,
    and stops if anything differs.
- **The trail is kept.**
  - The MAST listings are cached in a folder of this round only.
  - Every listing the walk used is kept, whole, in the draw record, with the interpreter's
    version.
- **The draw is validated offline** (`part_b_draw.validate_draw`), before it is written, by the
  runner before any download, and by the evaluation. The runner and the evaluation also require
  the draw to be **published**: committed, equal to `HEAD`, and on `origin/main`. The checks:
  - the lock commit holds this lock's bytes;
  - the round is the one the lock commit's CI push run gives (its creation time plus 10 minutes),
    and that run is **read again from GitHub** each time, so the draw's own copy of the CI
    evidence is never taken as given;
  - the beacon is that round, and its BLS signature verifies;
  - the shuffle is replayed from the beacon's randomness and the pool, and the trail must be the
    prefix of that order;
  - every kept listing must be this TIC's and within the filters: a finite 120-s cadence, an
    integer sector from 1 to 107, a valid time range, and this TIC's light curves. Each reason is
    recounted from it; a listing outside the filters is never recounted;
  - every trail entry has exactly the trail's keys. The chosen entries the run uses are rebuilt
    from the trail's own keys (sectors, products) and, for every other field (the ephemerides
    included), from the frozen frame, and they must be equal;
  - the counts must give the recorded status;
  - **Disjointness:** no drawn TIC and no listed product may appear in part A's draw (the TICs and
    products of every TIC part A checked; part A's draw is checked against its SHA-256).
- **Products used:** each record carries the light curves the adapter's manifest lists when the
  record is written, with their SHA-256, and its pixel files' manifest. The evaluation lists them
  against the draw's listing, as a report.

### 4.4 Prior exposure

A TIC is excluded when either of these holds:
- a file of commit `c4d6fa3` (the last before part A) names it, and that file is not a catalogue
  copy;
- a file or folder name under `.cache/` outside part A's folder carries it (TESS light curves,
  pixel files and their download folders).

**Naming** means any of:
- part A's patterns;
- `tic`, `tic_id` or `ticid` before `:` or `=`, in any case;
- a 16-digit TIC between hyphens;
- a JSON value under a key `tic`, `tic_id`, `ticid`, `tid`, `tic_ids` or `tics`;
- a CSV TIC column;
- the file's own path.

**Catalogue copies** are a written list of 11 files: the saved answers of catalogue queries made
before any selection (the TOI, CTOI and PSCompPars copies under `calibration/pool_snapshot/`,
`calibration/v0.2/pool_snapshot/`, `calibration/v0.4/inputs/` and `calibration/v0.5/inputs/`).
**Every other file counts**: records, results, selections, the `inputs-references` of selected
controls, EB extracts, reviews, plans, tests and archive manifests of v0.1 to v0.5.5. This class
and the loose patterns are wide on purpose, so the exclusion can only shrink the strata.

The list is `exposed_tics` in
[part-b-exposure.json](../validation/v0.5.6/part-b-exposure.json), committed before the lock.
Its SHA-256 is in the claim.

### 4.5 Per target, and the missing data

The run is part A's runner, with its battery unchanged (section 4.7). For each drawn target, the
record is classed as follows.
- **Event:** status OK, nonzero coverage (a decisive test examined data), outcome REFUTED **and**
  `identity.matched`. Identity is the same as in part A: the battery's found period at factor 1
  of the TOI's within 0.1 %, and its epoch within half the TOI's duration.
- **Non-event:** status OK with nonzero coverage and no event; or **status ERROR**, which counts as
  a non-event in the denominator, by the decision's ERROR rule.
- **Loss:** ERROR, MISSING_PRODUCTS, OK with zero coverage, or no record (a run stopped by a cap).
  ERROR is both a non-event and a loss.
- **Denominator:** events plus non-events.
- **Cap on the loss:** in each stratum, at most `floor(0.10 x drawn)` losses (15 of 150).
  Otherwise the claim is **INCONCLUSIVE**. A stratum with an empty denominator also makes it
  INCONCLUSIVE.
- **When a missing record is a loss:** only after the run's **terminal state**. The runner
  writes it to the ledger after its last batch has drained:
  - `complete`: every drawn target has a record;
  - or a stop of kind `cap_cpu` or `cap_disk` (a cap reached, or no target fitting under it);
  - or `window`: every remaining target's estimated duration exceeds the 28-minute batch
    window, so it can never be admitted.
  The missing records must be exactly the terminal state's unrecorded targets. **Any failure**
  recorded in a batch (a worker, a record, a light-curve manifest, a deletion) makes the kind
  `other`, even when a cap was also reached, so an admitted target without a record is never
  counted as a target never admitted. A stop of kind `other` goes to the maintainer, and the
  evaluation refuses. A
  ledger with a terminal state is never run again without the maintainer's approval.
- **Records are bound before counting.** Each record and each batch carries the SHA-256 of the
  lock and of the draw. Each record must also name the claim and `mode: confirmatory`, carry its
  file's name as its `target_key`, and be a drawn target; there may be no duplicate. Each batch
  must name the claim's and the runner's SHA-256. The lock must verify when the evaluation runs.

### 4.6 Test and verdict

- **Primary test:** Fisher's exact test, **one-sided** (the event rate of `fp` above that of
  `planet`), **alpha 0.05**. The p-value is the hypergeometric upper tail, computed with integers.
- **The guard on the planet stratum** (in D2's style): the event rate is at most 0.05 and its
  upper Wilson 95 % bound is at most 0.10.
- **The claim's verdict:**

  | Verdict | When |
  |---|---|
  | INCONCLUSIVE | the draw is incomplete, or a stratum's losses are above the cap |
  | SURVIVED | p at most 0.05 and the guard holds |
  | WEAKENED | p at most 0.05 and the guard fails |
  | REFUTED | p above 0.05 |

- **Secondary endpoints**, reported but never part of the verdict:
  - plain REFUTED (one-sided, `fp` above `planet`);
  - SURVIVED (one-sided, `planet` above `fp`);
  - the Wilson 95 % intervals;
  - the first decisive fatal FAIL of each event;
  - the outcomes per stratum, with signal identity.
- **Power** (the review's simulations, section 2, not estimates of the true rates): at 150 per
  stratum, about 0.97 for 12 % against 2 % and 0.99 for 24 % against 7.5 %. Part A's matched
  rates were 24 % against 0.8 %.

### 4.7 The run, the caps and the known defects

- **Runner:** `calibration/v0.5.6/part_b_run.py`. It is part A's runner with only the changes
  listed in its docstring (paths, the cap, `mode: confirmatory`, the lock check); the unified diff
  is `part_b_run.diff`.
  - The battery is v0.5's at stage 2, without the catalog test.
  - Pixel files are deleted per target after the record and its manifest are written and read
    back.
  - Workers and network as in the decision of 2026-11-22 (09:00): Monday to Friday, 08:00 to
    18:00, at most 10 workers and 2 simultaneous downloads; otherwise up to 20 and 6.
  - Batches of up to 30 minutes, every run resumable target by target.
- **Caps:**
  - **CPU: 15 CPU-h** for everything of part B. Part A's rate gives about 7 CPU-h for 300 targets.
  - **Disk: a 20 GB peak.**
  - **Download: about 100 GB**, an estimate from part A's 0.34 GB per target. It is not a cap.
  - **How the caps are enforced** (part A's mechanism):
    - A target is admitted only while the CPU charged, plus the estimates of the targets in
      flight and its own, stays under 15 CPU-h.
    - A target is admitted only while the measured disk plus the reserves stays under 18 GB.
    - The CPU cap reached, or a disk sample over 20 GB, **STOPS** admission. The targets in flight
      finish and are recorded, and the run ends.
    - So the charged CPU and the peak can pass a cap by the work in flight. The ledger records
      such an overrun, and the result reports it. It does not change the verdict.
    - Targets never admitted have no record and are losses, after the terminal state
      (section 4.5). With part A's rates,
      the expected use is about half the CPU cap and under half the disk cap.
- **Run once.** The verdict is reported as it comes, with the limits label. A rerun needs the
  maintainer's approval, recorded first on an issue.
- **Known defects, locked in, with no change to `src/`:**
  - `OverflowError` in `gaussian_sigma_from_chi2`, reached from `centroid_v5.py`. It was seen on
    TIC 219108677 in part A; the traceback and the likely cause are on #59. A target that hits it
    is an ERROR, handled by the ERROR rule.
  - The half-period search defect (#59). Such a record is not an event, because its identity does
    not match.
