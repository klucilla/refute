- **Cheapest decisive steps:** a D2 joint-floor census (about 0–8 CPU-h), a one-realization population version of I3 (about 9 CPU-h instead of 860), and a staged I2 on the 102 class-(i) cases first.

## 2. Per-target path

### D1 (EB1, EB2)

- **Evidence.**
  - The oracle with the catalog star leaves 29.2 and 35.5 binaries non-rejected against an allowance of 10 at level 0.0027, and 14.1 and 12.5 at 0.05 (`stageB/README.md:63-68`).
  - With the star known exactly it still leaves 11.9 and 11.2 at 0.0027 (`:67`); B0b gives 9.3 and 12.9 (`b0b-record.md:31`).
  - So perfect stellar knowledge only brings D1 to the edge.
- **Structural fact not stated in the records.**
  - EB1's secondary ratio is uniform on [0.2, 1.0] with the primary's duration and shape (`calib05/c1.py:263-265`; `v0.5-acceptance.md:375`). The family therefore contains members arbitrarily close to a planet at half the period in the odd/even and secondary channels. About 5 % have a ratio above 0.96, the same order as the allowance (10 of about 190).
  - `odd_even`'s declared alternative is a difference of 1.0 times the depth (`calibration/v0.5/claim.yaml:246`). I1's messages agree: the alternative equals the depth, for example 1001.75 ppm for both `odd_even` and the sinusoid (`i1-diagnosis.json:72, 101`).
  - Every EB2 binary (r in [0.5, 0.9], `c1.py:289`) has a relative odd/even difference of 0.105 to 0.667, below that alternative. A sensitivity-qualified `odd_even` PASS is therefore coherent with missing all of EB2.
  - Consequence: D1 rests on the density channel alone for a share of each family above the allowance. This is evidence toward an infeasibility record for the fatal class, not a reason to touch the target.
- **B0's bound is not the right bound for D1.**
  - It assumes a per-planet level (`stageB/README.md:46-47`).
  - The frozen rules bound family averages: P REFUTED ≤ 0.05 and PG/PV/PA ≤ 0.10 (`c1.py:664-682`).
  - A test may spend its whole allowance on the most binary-like planets, so the correct object is the population ROC: the largest fraction of binaries rejected subject to those family-average rates.
  - B0 can therefore overstate the non-rejected binaries, which is a risk of false infeasibility.
- **Cheapest decisive step: a population version of I3, about 9 CPU-h.**
  - Compute T once per curve on fresh development curves (about 800 binaries and 2,000 planets, one fit pair each).
  - D1, D2 and D3 are population fractions, so per-curve Monte Carlo probabilities (200 realizations, the source of the 860 CPU-h) are unnecessary.
  - The envelope becomes simple: a binomial or DKW band on the calibration quantile and on the evaluation counts, which are independent.
  - Use separate budgets for P and PG, and a continuous range of levels in place of four grid points.
  - It decides between a route for the fatal class and a draft "fatal-class infeasible under C1's information".
- **Also cheap (about 2.5 CPU-h per setting):** B0's oracle at intermediate stellar errors (1, 2 and 3 %), to state what stellar precision D1 would need.

## 3. I4 interpretation

- **What I4 computed** (`i4_bound.py:167-209`):
  - a Fisher bound with the true ephemeris;
  - the generator's covariance taken as known (`:241-242`);
  - the stellar trend taken as known (`trend_multiplier(t, truth)`, `:170`; `stageA/d2_bounds.py:113-118`);
  - sector levels plus four shape parameters shared by all transits.
- **Why the ratio is 1.** Shared shape parameters move odd and even transits identically, so their columns are orthogonal to the contrast (1, −1) up to the imbalance of odd and even coverage. Hence a median ratio of 1.00004 and a maximum of 1.002.
  - L = 2 equals `below_at_known_template` = 2 (`i4-bound.json:166, 171`).
  - I4's only new information is stage A's bound recomputed on the fresh 178, which closes the review's item 5.
  - The symmetry argument was available at design time.
- **Implication for D2.** `odd_even` is not information-limited under the generator's model for 160 or more of 177 planets. The 25 class-(i) losses are the estimator's or the error estimate's.
- **Implication for E2's gap of 1.1 to 2.1 times.**
  - Not knowing the shape does not cause it, so the review's hypothesis 4 is refuted as an explanation.
  - Candidates I4 did not vary:
    - the trend: the bound knows it, while the pipeline detrends;
    - the box in place of the trapezoid, a matched-filter mismatch;
    - local windows that discard baseline information;
    - an estimated covariance;
    - noise in the error estimate itself. Ratios below 1 (0.82) show that the reported error scatters around the truth, so planets near the threshold fall on either side at random.
- **How much efficiency is needed.** With a 3-sigma threshold and power 0.95, moving the median class-(i) power of 0.851 to 0.95 needs about 13 % less error. The worst case (0.625) needs about 29 %. E2 sits at a median of 1.44 times the bound, so it must close nearly the whole gap. This is my arithmetic from `i1-diagnosis.json:3-9`, assuming the Gaussian power form.
- **Direction.**
  - First, a gap decomposition (about 1–2 CPU-h, reusing `i4_bound.py`): recompute the bound adding, one at a time, a free trend (the generator's sinusoid family or the detrender's basis), local windows only, and a box template.
  - It names which piece holds the 1.44.
  - The likely estimator is then a joint GLS or maximum-likelihood fit of two depths, a fitted trapezoid, the trend and a fitted noise model on undetrended flux.
- **Risks.**
  - It may be fitted to the synthetic generator and fail on real noise. T2a's real hosts show the same `odd_even` losses.
  - Maximum-likelihood availability may fail as E3's did (15 %).
  - The error estimate needs a calibrated mean and tail; E2's are UNRESOLVED.
  - The joint floor may leave D2 without margin even at the bound.

## 4. Errors and blind spots

1. **I3: trivial false infeasibility.** `v0.5.4-I2-I3-designs.md:211-220` fixes the other outcomes at version 5's records and checks D2's kept fraction "at once". Version 5 already fails that rule, and a fatal test can only lower the kept count. The median joint probability is therefore about 0 at every level, and the rule at `:225-226` returns "fatal-class infeasible" for reasons unrelated to D1. The design notes that D4 becomes trivial (`:221`) but not this. I read the design text only; no code exists.
2. **I3: the level grid.** The rule uses four levels (`:224`), while the joint probability is non-monotone, as `:198-200` admits. A maximum between 0.01 and 0.05 would be missed, giving a false finding.
3. **I3: one mixture quantile.** It uses a single quantile over 1,000 P and 1,000 PG (`:184`). D2 (P, 0.05) and D3 (PG, 0.10) have different allowances and are checked per family, so this is not the best allocation. A failure bounds only "this test"; the design says so at `:234-236`.
4. **I3: a two-outcome model.** The real tests have three outcomes under the sensitivity rule. A density test that lacks power turns planets INCONCLUSIVE, which costs D2 and D4. That is not modelled.
5. **I3: the per-curve Monte Carlo (`:188-190`) is unnecessary** for population rules. It is the whole 860 CPU-h.
6. **I2: adequacy by Ljung-Box at lag 20 on 2-minute cadences (`:71-76`).**
   - It tests 40 minutes of lag, while the rise error is set by variability over hours.
   - With about 13,000 cadences it will reject for tiny misfits, so K is likely large and the outcome "unresolved" for an uninformative reason.
   - Where it passes, BIC-selected simple models can understate low-frequency power, giving an optimistic σ_GLS and a false route.
   - Scattered-light ramps are non-stationary; a constant plus a linear trend (`:70`) will not absorb them.
7. **I2: the route threshold of 63 (`:128`) counts every other blocker as recoverable.** That includes the 56 above 20 sectors lost by rule, the 5 REFUTED, the 66 class-(ii) losses, and the sinusoid and `period_alias` classes. A "route" there is a route for one sub-check, not for T2a. It should be reported against the margins of 7 (B) and about 49 (C) as well.
8. **I2: its population is all 615, although only the 102 can change the answer cheaply.** Its cost estimate exceeds the cap by 4 to 15 times (`:147-149`).
9. **I2: σ_GLS bounds only estimators that remove a per-window quadratic.** Plugging in the covariance ignores its estimation error. The design states the first limit (`:102-104`); the second is implicit.
10. **I4: the nuisance set is near-vacuous by symmetry** (section 3). The trend and covariance are taken as known without being listed as nuisances in the "Conditions" (`v0.5.4-h1-design.md:206-207` names the covariance and shape family, not the known trend).
11. **I4: E = 15 in both sets, almost all "the convergence check failed".** That is 8.5 % and 13 % of the planets. It is probably an artefact of central differences across the trapezoid's kinks, not a property of the planets (unconfirmed). Those planets' power is not summarized. It is harmless to the outcome (17 < 23) but is a design defect.
12. **I1: class (ii) is labelled "route (a procedure or a domain to fix)" (`v0.5.4-h1-design.md:101`).** The 8 ambiguous-sinusoid D2 losses and the 1 with too few transits are outcomes of a rule or of the data. No estimator fixes them without a rule change.
13. **A label that inverts the diagnosis.** `battery_v52.py:422` writes `inconclusive_class = "insufficient_sensitivity"` for the ambiguous-sinusoid branch.
    - The closing record's "8 insufficient sensitivity, 17 with no class recorded" (`closing-record.md:134-136`; addendum `:124`) therefore reads backwards.
    - The 8 are not sensitivity cases. The unlabelled ones are: I1 has 18 class (i) and 8 class (ii).
14. **A count mismatch.** I1 classifies 27 `odd_even` and 26 sinusoid cases (`i1-diagnosis.json:47-56`), against 26 and 25 in the addendum (`:123-124`). The likely cause is tests hidden behind a fatal FAIL in REFUTED losses (addendum `:73-75`). It is not reconciled in the files I read.
15. **The declared alternative and D1.** `odd_even`'s alternative of 1.0 times the depth, set against EB2's differences of 0.105 to 0.667, is nowhere set beside D1 in the records I read.
16. **B0's per-planet level assumption** is stated as a requirement (`stageB/README.md:44-50`), but its mismatch with the family-average rules is not drawn out.
17. **Decision page against design.** The decision page prices I2 at 3–12 CPU-h and I3 at 5–8 (`v0.5.3-after-round3-decision.md:100, 135`). The designs say 95–380 and about 860. The phase caps (`ROADMAP.md:800-805`) were set on the former.
18. **I4's ledger.** 5.08 CPU-h were charged as a crash charge for 0.19 measured (`i4-bound.json:5-10, 57`). I4 is booked at 10.77 CPU-h for about 5.9 of work.

### Question 4: D1 routes not yet considered, and what each requires

- **Warning-severity density test.**
  - It is legal under the frozen rules: D1 counts SURVIVED only (`c1.py:641-649`), and WEAKENED planets are kept (`:654`).
  - At 0.05 the oracle still leaves 12 to 14 against 10 or 11, so it would need a level of roughly 0.1 to 0.2 (my extrapolation, unconfirmed).
  - Requires the maintainer's ruling. Its credibility cost is that SURVIVED loses meaning for a tenth or more of planets.
- **Ellipsoidal, beaming and reflection signals** (SWEET- or BEER-type tests).
  - Physically real for stellar companions, but C1 does not generate them (`stageB/README.md:34-39`).
  - Requires a new generator version argued from physics before any result, a check that detrending and pre-whitening do not remove the signal, and evidence on real binaries, which is T3b's population.
  - Deciding it now carries the cost of changing the population after failures.
- **Better stellar density** (Gaia radii, spectroscopic log g).
  - In C1 the "catalog" is a 5 % assumption.
  - Requires a sourced error model per star (not invented), a new generator version, and step 4 to know the target precision.
  - Even a perfectly known star is marginal at 0.0027.
- **Gaia flags not in the light curve** (RUWE, radial-velocity scatter, non-single-star solutions).
  - They have no meaning on synthetic curves unless the generator simulates catalog fields.
  - Licence limits apply (CLAUDE.md, data publication).
- **Secondary-eclipse and odd/even tests.** They are already inside the oracle's likelihood and add nothing new.

## 6. When to pause

- **Pausing is rational if steps 1, 3 and 6 return:**
  - a D2 joint floor of about 18 or more of 22;
  - T2a non-fixable losses above its margin under B;
  - no route for D1's fatal class.
- **Why.** The remaining path would then cost more than 3,500 CPU-h and need four simultaneous near-bound estimators, for a pass probability that the evidence puts well below one half. D1 would still depend on a ruling.
- **The three open design questions, as principle only.** Most T2a losses are INCONCLUSIVE results of warning tests (169 `systematics`, 95 sinusoid). Deciding those questions now is contaminated by known results; `v0.5.3-design-questions.md:6-11` already excludes the 98-against-97 fact as a reason. Any decision would have to be prospective, for a new battery version, on fresh data, and disclosed as taken with the results known. That is a real credibility cost.
- **A gap in the advance rule.** A target can be neither passable nor "shown infeasible" when its losses are procedural or rule-made. Pause is then the rule's only honest exit.
- **Where the process wastes most effort.**
  - **Part caps at 1.5 times a 10-curve checkpoint**, with a new script and a three-round Codex review per resume. Examples: `b1_d2d4_rest.py` for 0.7 CPU-h (114,130 tokens, addendum `:213`), and `i4_rest` plus `i4_rest2` (six Codex rounds) for about 6 CPU-h. CPU is the cheap resource here; review tokens and the maintainer's attention are not.
  - **Three-round reviews that end in a tie-break and freeze designs that cannot run** (I2 and I3).
  - **Records that do not keep messages and inputs**, which forced I1's 6.4 CPU-h rerun. This is v0.5.2's lesson 2 recurring.
  - **Bounds and failures computed on different samples.**
