# v0.5.3 stage A: literature read on 2026-11-11

Sources consulted for stage A's report (#217): detection limits, injection-recovery completeness,
and background tests in TESS photometry. Each entry gives the URL, the date read and a short excerpt
as returned by the search or page. They inform the discussion only; no number in stage A's results
comes from them.

1. **Kepler DR25 Robovetter minor flags.** NASA Exoplanet Archive, "Robovetter Minor Flags in the
   Kepler Objects of Interest Table", https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/koi_comment_flags.html,
   read 2026-11-11. Excerpt: "some minor flags are informational only and do not necessarily
   indicate the KOI is a FP". Used in `docs/plans/v0.5.3-design-questions.md` (question 1) as a
   precedent for reporting a flag without letting it decide.
2. **Injection-recovery in a transit pipeline.** Christiansen et al., "Measuring Transit Signal
   Recovery in the Kepler Pipeline I: Individual Events", https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.0255 (HTML at
   https://arxiv.org/html/1303.0255v1), read 2026-11-11. Excerpt: "simulated transit signals are
   typically recovered to within 2–4% of their expected SNR". Context for injection-based
   completeness: a pipeline's recovery is measured, not assumed.
3. **Per-target detection efficiency.** C. J. Burke (2017; the search listed this first author,
   the co-authors were not checked), "Per-Target Flux-Level Transit Injection Tests of TPS ...",
   KSCI-19109-002, https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/KSCI-19109-002.pdf,
   read 2026-11-11. Excerpt (search summary): "The detection efficiency represents the component of
   the pipeline completeness model that describes the fall-off in transit signal recovery".
   Context: completeness depends on the target, as T2a's per-host results do.
4. **Scattered light in TESS.** Battley et al. 2022, "YOUNG Star detrending for Transiting
   Exoplanet Recovery" (MNRAS 511, 4285), https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/511/3/4285/6517697,
   read 2026-11-11. Excerpt (search summary): "According to the TESS data release notes for S17,
   this was typically caused by scattered light from the Earth and Moon glinting across the detector
   at these". Context for (c1)'s finding that the background series is dominated by slow
   variability rather than white noise; it is not evidence about any of the 23 losses.
