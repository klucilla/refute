# Odd/even transit-depth estimators in the literature (read 2026-11-10)

For path (i) of the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-10 (#216): options for a calibrated and efficient `odd_even`
estimator. Each entry gives the URL, the date read, and what the source says, in a short paraphrase; nothing here
was run or validated in Refute.

1. **Kepler Data Validation, odd/even transit depth test.** Twicken et al. 2018, PASP 130, 064502,
   <https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180001990/downloads/20180001990.pdf> (read 2026-11-10). A transit model
   is fitted separately to the odd and the even transits; the depths' uncertainties are the fit uncertainties;
   the comparison is a chi-square test of equal depths. Model fitting of the odd and even transits is described in
   Li et al. 2019, PASP 131, 024506, <https://www.jstor.org/stable/26660723> (abstract read 2026-11-10, section 6.3
   per the abstract page; not read in full).
2. **LEO-Vetter** (Kunimoto et al. 2025, AJ 170, 280), <https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10619v1> (read 2026-11-10).
   Three odd/even depth comparisons: a box (mean depths of odd and even transits, with the noise over those
   transits), a trapezoid and a transit model fitted to odd and even transits separately with only the depth (or
   R_p/R_star) and T_0 free and the other parameters fixed at the overall best fit; each fails above 3 sigma.
   Correlated noise enters its SNR through a white plus red ("pink") noise estimate.
3. **Kepler Robovetter, model-shift uniqueness test** (Thompson et al. 2018, ApJS 235, 38;
   <https://archive.stsci.edu/files/live/sites/mast/files/home/missions-and-data/kepler/_documents/Thompson_2018_ApJS_235_38(1).pdf>;
   Coughlin 2017, KSCI-19105-002, <https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/KSCI-19105-002.pdf>; search
   excerpts read 2026-11-10, not read in full). Fits the transit model at every phase and compares the event's
   significance with the light curve's own red-noise level; used to flag odd/even and secondary events.
4. **Correlated noise in transit fits.** Carter & Winn 2009, ApJ 704, 51, <https://arxiv.org/html/0909.0747v1>
   (abstract read 2026-11-10): a wavelet likelihood for 1/f-type correlated noise in transit parameter estimation.
   Aigrain & Foreman-Mackey 2023, ARA&A 61, 329, <https://arxiv.org/html/2209.08940v2> (search excerpt read
   2026-11-10): Gaussian-process regression as the general framework, Carter & Winn's method a special case.

5. **Two checks after Codex's forecast round 2** (read by Claude 2026-11-10).
   - LEO-Vetter, section IV.4, <https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10619v1>: the trapezoid and transit-model fits with
     lmfit sometimes produced no uncertainties, and then the vetter could not judge the odd/even difference. The
     authors run all three comparisons together to cover those cases.
   - Carter & Winn 2009, abstract, <https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.0747>: the method is tested on simulated transit
     photometry, mainly for the mid-transit time. For noise that follows its assumptions (white plus 1/f^gamma) it
     gives more accurate mid-transit times and truer uncertainty estimates than time-averaging or residual
     permutation. The abstract does not cover odd/even depths.

**Not confirmed:** the exact error treatment of items 1 and 3 beyond the excerpts above; whether any of them was
shown to be calibrated (pull standard deviation near 1) on correlated noise is not established by these readings.
