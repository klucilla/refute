## Thompson et al. 2018, ApJS 235, 38: the Kepler DR25 Robovetter

https://arxiv.org/html/1710.06758v2, read 2026-11-11. Table 3, "stellar eclipse" (SS) and
"centroid offset" (CO) tests, the brief descriptions as printed:

- Odd Even (A.4.1.4): "The depths of odd and even transits are different."
- MS Secondary (A.4.1.2): "A significant secondary event is detected."
- SWEET EB (A.4.2): "Out-of-phase tidal deformation is detected."
- V Shape Metric (A.4.3): "The transit is deep and v-shaped."
- Unresolved Offset (A.5.1): "A shift in the centroid position occurs during transit."
- Ghost Diagnostic (A.5.2): "The transit strength in the halo pixels is too large."

On binaries detected at half their period, the v0.5.1 plan (section 2, item 5) already cites A.4.1.4.
Their odd/even depths reveal them only "if the primary and secondary depths are dissimilar enough".

## Kunimoto et al. 2025, AJ 170, 280: LEO-Vetter (TESS)

https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10619v1, read 2026-11-11. A Robovetter-inspired automated vetter for
TESS:

- IV.2, V-shaped test: "sufficiently grazing EBs can have shallow eclipse depths that appear
  transit-like."
- IV.4, Odd-Even test: "We fail TCEs with either OE_trap > 3 or OE_transit > 3".
- Pixel-level test: "We fail TCEs as likely off-target when Δθ > 15″."

## Morton 2012, ApJ 761, 6: false-positive probabilities from the eclipse shape

https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.1568, read 2026-11-11 (the search result's abstract). It is "a general
procedure that can quickly be applied to any planet candidate". It compares the trapezoidal eclipse
shape and the implied stellar density across the planet and false-positive scenarios.

## The Neyman-Pearson lemma

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neyman%E2%80%93Pearson_lemma, read 2026-11-11: the likelihood-ratio
test "is a uniformly most powerful (UMP) test in the set of level α tests". It was published in the
Philosophical Transactions in 1933.
