# v0.5.3 stage B, B0b: D1's pre-registered infeasibility test (EB1 and EB2)

**Decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-11, after B0's stop** (#217, comment 6804003610, which also
gives this test's exact procedure). B0 stays recorded as it came out: outcome (C), with no
reinterpretation ([`stageB/README.md`](../validation/v0.5.3/stageB/README.md)). The maintainer's
decision of B0 asked for a "draft infeasibility record if the bound shows a lack of information".
The information a test has on C1 is the light curve and the catalog star, whose radius and mass
errors are 5 % each. B0b fixes an infeasibility criterion on that information before any B0b
measurement, and measures it on new development binaries.

Written before any B0b measurement. `method_status: not_validated`. Nothing in `src/` or in T2a's
fingerprint changes. No confirmation data is drawn or used. D1's target is not relaxed.

## 1. What is measured

Exactly B0's bound ([`v0.5.3-stageB0-design.md`](v0.5.3-stageB0-design.md), section 3), on new
seeds. The script, [`d1_infeasibility.py`](../validation/v0.5.3/stageB/d1_infeasibility.py), imports
B0's [`d1_bound.py`](../validation/v0.5.3/stageB/d1_bound.py) unchanged and reuses:

- its `bound_one`: the frozen C1 generator, the frozen v0.4 candidate generation, v0.5.1's forecast
  eligibility, and v0.5.1's oracle (the nearest planet at the found period, and its Mahalanobis
  distance under the generator's covariance, with the trend factor), for the two stars `catalog`
  and `star_known`;
- its `_family_summary`: the C1' projection `n'`, D1's allowance at `n'`, and for each star and
  level the expected non-rejected count at `n'` and the median of the Poisson-binomial
  probability of at most the allowance over 2,000 bootstrap cohorts of `n'`.

Only four things change:

- **the seeds:** `refute-v053-b0b:<family>:<i>` for i from 0 to 399, EB1 and EB2;
- **the bootstrap seed:** 20261112;
- **the output folder;**
- **the reading** (section 2).

The script checks that none of these seeds is one of B0's. C1', C2' and C3' have never been drawn.
Their seeds will come from a drand round that the lock's commit fixes, so no confirmation seed
exists to collide with.

**The assumptions** are B0's, stated as requirements and not shown:

1. **The per-planet level.** A route must fail each planet of the null with probability at most
   `alpha`. The null is a planet at the found period around a star within its catalog errors,
   over the ±3-sigma box.
2. **Selection** leaves the noise along the binary-to-planet difference unchanged.
3. **Eligibility.** v0.5.1's forecast eligibility rule stands for C1's gate.

The directions are also B0's. The known covariance, the known shape, the fixed period and a fit
that misses the minimum can only make `d` larger, and so the non-rejected binaries fewer. The
selection and the eligibility approximation have no known direction.

## 2. The criterion (fixed before the run; adopted as the maintainer's example)

`d1_infeasibility.outcome` computes the result on the catalog star's figures.

- **(D) No conclusion.** Any record with status ERROR, a family with fewer than 150 eligible
  binaries, or a non-finite distance (B0's `valid`). B0b stops and reports the cause.
- **(I) The criterion is met** for a family when, **with the catalog star, at both levels 0.0027
  and 0.05**:
  - the expected non-rejected count at `n'` is above D1's allowance at `n'`;
  - the median cohort's probability of meeting the allowance is below 0.05.

  (I) holds when the criterion is met for EB1 or for EB2. D1 needs both families, so one family is
  enough for the gate.
- **(N) Not met.** Otherwise. B0b records it and the work goes on. D1 stays pending, with no new
  route in this round.

**Why both levels.** 0.0027 is the project's convention for the false-failure rate a new test may
impose on planets (B0's design, section 3). 0.05 is a far more permissive level, under which a test
could fail one planet in twenty. Requiring the criterion at both protects against a false
infeasibility conclusion: (I) then says that even a test that fails 5 % of the planets is not
expected to meet D1 on C1's information.

**Disclosure.** B0's figures were known when the maintainer gave the example and when this design
was written. With the catalog star at 0.05, B0's median pass probabilities were 0.077 (EB1) and
0.247 (EB2), so B0 itself would not have met the criterion. At 0.0027 they were 0 for both. The
criterion is the maintainer's example unchanged, with no level, threshold or sample size chosen
from these figures. The sample size, 400 per family, is B0's.

## 3. What follows

- **Under (I):**
  - B0b writes a **draft** infeasibility record of D1 for EB1 and EB2. It is conditional on the
    assumptions and cites B0 and B0b as evidence under them, not as proof.
  - The draft lists the routes considered: #211, #215 (stage A (d)'s geometric route) and the
    literature of B0's design, section 2.
  - It says what would make the target reachable. Real data carries information C1 does not
    generate: more precise stellar parameters, ellipsoidal variation, and centroids.
  - The draft goes through Codex's review (at most 3 rounds), then **stops**. The independent
    review and the approval are the maintainer's. Nothing is declared infeasible before that.
- **Under (N):** the result is recorded, D1 stays pending, and B1 goes on.
- **Under (D):** the cause is reported, and a rerun needs a new approval.

## 4. Caps and running

- **The CPU ledger.** [`ledger_b.py`](../validation/v0.5.3/stageB/ledger_b.py) gets a new part,
  `b0_infeasibility`, with 4 CPU-h transferred from `b0_reserve` before the run. Both parts are in
  B0's sub-line. The part's strict cap is 1.5 times its allocation, over the driver's 10 %
  overshoot, so at most 6 CPU-h. B0's 30 CPU-h sub-cap and stage B's 150 also apply, through
  `hard_cap`.
- **After Codex's round 1** (`WEAKENED`, two corrections to the ledger, both applied):
  - **One writer at a time.** A run keeps a copy of the ledger and saves it whole, so with B1
    running in parallel a second writer's transfers, charges or consumption could be lost. Every
    writer of the stage B ledger now holds an exclusive lock file
    (`.cache/v053/stageB/ledger.lock`) for its whole run: B0b's smoke, run and summary, and every
    declaration, transfer and charge. A second process refuses and does not wait.
  - **The cap across attempts.** `hard_cap` subtracts the part's consumption in every earlier
    attempt from its 1.5 times the allocation. A part that ran under another identity, or that has
    an archived attempt, is refused (`refuse_new_identity`): a rerun needs the maintainer's
    recorded approval and a new part. A resume under the same identity is allowed, and its
    consumption is then counted twice, which is conservative.
- **Estimate.** B0 took 2.45 CPU-h for 800 binaries, about 11 processor-seconds each, so B0b needs
  about 2.5 CPU-h.
- **The smoke.** One binary, EB1 index 100000, outside the measured range. It runs through
  `scripts/launch_affinity.ps1` before Codex's review and is charged as `b0_b0b_smoke_<date>`. The
  summary's processor time is charged as `b0_b0b_summarize_<time>`, B0's way.
- **Running.** B0's driver and settings:
  - processors 8 to 31, even processors pinned;
  - 12 workers configured, at most 10 in business hours;
  - BelowNormal priority;
  - 30-minute batches;
  - resumable from its shards.
- **Tokens.** B0b's Codex conversations are named `b0-b0b-…`. They count toward B0's 1.5 M and
  stage B's 4.5 M, with a stop at 80 % (`ledger_b.check_caps`).
- **The fingerprint** covers:
  - the code hash;
  - this script and B0's `d1_bound.py`;
  - the ledger;
  - this design;
  - the imported oracle;
  - v0.5.2's `budgeted` and driver;
  - the v0.4 claim.

  The design and the script are committed and pushed before the run.
