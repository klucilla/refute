## 2. The defect, read in the code at the approval SHA

In `src/refute/packs/tess/screen_d5.py`:
- **(a)** `_noise` (lines 143 to 175) computes ONE `sigma_w` (line 149) and ONE `sigma_r` (lines 152
  to 175) for the whole curve, from the baseline. `_f1_box` takes them once (line 228), and `_pink`
  propagates that pooled noise to each parity by its point and epoch counts (line 242). A parity
  whose cadences have a larger σ is given the pooled noise, not its own: P2.4 and P9.5.
- **(b)** `_wstd` (lines 88 to 93) divides by the total weight but does not standardize the
  residuals by their cadence uncertainties. A single baseline point with a tiny σ takes almost all
  the weight, so `sigma_w` (line 149) and `sigma_bin` (line 168, through the bin weights of line
  163) collapse toward 0: B6.
- **(c)** In T2 and T6, the precise point fixes its parity's weighted mean (line 241), while
  `sigma_tr` (line 242) comes from the global noise. The test is conservative there: it passes,
  but loses sensitivity.
- **(d)** `_mes` (line 314) and `_f2_u1` (line 685) use the same `_noise` and `_pink`, so the
  report-only MES series (F2, U1) share the defect.

## 4. The rule (normative): F1's noise

**The scope, Codex's round 1, item 3, as written:**

> Correction 2 applies to F1 alone and supersedes the noise calculation in specification §5 only
> when used by F1, and `σ_par` in §6. F2 and U1 retain the existing §5 MES calculation. Record its
> treatment of unequal cadence uncertainties as a declared report-only limit. Retain the existing
> F1 metrics except `sigma_w`, and add the baseline scale and both parity weight sums.

- **What it means here.** `_mes`, `_f2_u1` and their noise stay as now.
- **The declared report-only limit:** the MES grid of F2 and U1 pools the baseline noise as the
  approval code does, so with unequal cadence uncertainties its values carry the defect of
  section 2. They stay report-only, and they have no effect on the screen or on level 2.

Unchanged:
- the valid points and correction 1's input rule;
- the epochs;
- the baseline d(t, 0) > T, with at least 10 points;
- the bins: width T over the baseline from the first valid time, at least 2 points per bin, and
  at least 3 bins;
- OE_box's threshold 3;
- the coverage, eligibility, parity and denominator rules.

**The estimator: Codex's round 1, item 1, as written, except that the blind review's B1
(alternative A, quoted exactly) replaces the first two sentences of its second paragraph, and its
last sentence:**

> **F1 noise [choice].** Set `w_i = 1/σ_i²` and `s² = Σ_b w_i(f_i − m_b)²/(n_b − 1)`, where `m_b`
> is the weighted baseline mean. Use the existing baseline and retained bins. For each retained
> bin let `W_k` be its total weight and `μ_k` its weighted mean. In bin order, set `B = Σ_k W_k`,
> `μ̄ = Σ_k W_k μ_k/B`, `Q₀ = Σ_k W_k(μ_k − μ̄)²`, and `C = 2 Σ_k W_k[(Σ_{j<k} W_j)/B]`. Compute
> `σ_r² = max(0, [Q₀ − s²(M−1)]/C)`, where `M` counts retained bins. Use the positive prefix-sum
> expression for `C`.
>
> For each parity, let `W_par,e` be the sum of the in-transit cadence weights of epoch `e` of
> that parity, in epoch order, `W_par = Σ_e W_par,e`, and `N_eff,par = W_par² / Σ_e W_par,e²`.
> Set `σ_par² = s²/W_par + σ_r²/N_eff,par`. `N_par` still counts all distinct epochs represented
> by those cadences; it is recorded, and `N_eff,par` is recorded beside it. With equal epoch
> weights `N_eff,par = N_par`, which is the form of K25 Eq. (2). The red contribution divided by
> the effective epoch count is a modelling choice **[choice]**: it is the variance of the
> weighted in-transit mean under one independent red offset of variance `σ_r²` per epoch.
> Keep the existing coverage and eligibility rules. Evaluate the OE numerator as the absolute
> difference of the two in-transit weighted means, since the common baseline cancels. Keep
> `OE_box > 3`. Non-finite noise arithmetic, `s² ≤ 0`, or `C ≤ 0` gives `not_assessed` with reason
> `noise unavailable (baseline or bins)`. Keep the existing parity and denominator guards.

**What it means here:**
- **The notation** (blind review, N1): in `s²`, the sum `Σ_b` runs over all baseline points, in
  cadence order; `n_b` is their count and `m_b` their weighted mean. They are not the per-bin
  count `n_b` of specification §5, and `s²` is not that section's weighted standard deviation `s`.
- **s²** is the baseline's reduced chi-square. It is dimensionless and about 1 when the σ are
  right. Under the registered independent Gaussian model, (n_b − 1)s² follows a χ² law with
  n_b − 1 degrees of freedom, B6 included (Codex, round 1).
  - **It absorbs red noise** (blind review, N7): its residuals are taken about the global
    baseline mean, so with correlated noise it is above 1 and scales the white term up. That is
    conservative; `s²` is not a pure white-noise scale.
  - **A declared limit** (blind review, N6): `s²` is ONE scale for the whole curve, so the
    estimator assumes that the reported σ_i are right up to a common factor. If the real noise of
    one sector or one parity is larger than its reported σ, section 2's defect (a) returns for it.
    M1 and the checks of section 1 used correct σ.
- **The `C ≤ 0` guard is defensive** (blind review, N4): under correction 1's range and with at
  least 3 retained bins, `C > 0` (section 6, the item on correction 1), so no input reaches it.
  `s² ≤ 0` is reachable (a baseline of constant flux).
- **σ_r²** is the inverse-variance weighted method-of-moments excess of the bin means over their
  expected scatter, with "we set σr=0" when negative (K25 Eq. (3)).
  - It equals the earlier draft's (Q − (M − 1))/c with u_k = W_k/s².
  - C is computed as a positive prefix sum, so that a dominant bin cannot cancel the rest in
    float64 (Codex's witness: bin weights [6e24, 2e-6, 2e-6] with s² = 1 gave 0 by the earlier
    subtraction, against about 8e-6).
- **OE_box** = |m_in,odd − m_in,even| / √(σ_odd² + σ_even²), the in-transit weighted means of the
  two parities (K25 Eq. (11)), `fail` if OE_box > 3.
- Tested only exploratorily, by the second check of section 1, in the earlier form with
  `σ_r²/N_par`. The effective epoch count was added after that check, on the blind review's
  finding B1, and no project check has run on it.
- **Recorded F1 metrics:** the existing ones except `sigma_w`, plus s², W_odd, W_even, `N_eff,odd`
  and `N_eff,even`. σ_r, N_odd, N_even, σ_odd, σ_even and OE_box are kept.
  - **Their keys** (blind review, N3): `s2`, `weight_odd`, `weight_even`, `epochs_eff_odd` and
    `epochs_eff_even`, beside the existing `sigma_r`, `epochs_odd`, `epochs_even`, `sigma_odd`,
    `sigma_even` and `oe_box`. The screen record carries correction 2's hash under
    `spec_correction_2_sha256`, beside `spec_sha256` and `spec_correction_sha256` (section 8).

## 5. Checks of the rule (arithmetic, not runs)

- **Equal σ: Codex's round 1, item 2, as written, with the blind review's condition (B1,
  alternative A) added as its fifth sentence:**

  > With equal cadence σ, the white variance is the baseline sample variance divided by the parity
  > cadence count. With equal bin counts `m` as well, the red variance is the sample variance of
  > the bin means minus the baseline sample variance divided by `m`, clipped at zero. With unequal
  > bin counts, use the weighted expression. This recovers the white-plus-red form of K25 Eq. (2).
  > With equal cadence σ and equal in-transit counts per epoch, the red contribution is
  > `σ_r²/N_par`.
  > The legacy estimator uses different divisors and can give different values. Test these closed
  > forms against the independent oracle, and include an admissible dominant-bin witness for `C`.
  > Use strict expected failures only for documented assertions against the approval code.

  - **Codex's example of the difference:** five bins of two equal-flux points each, with bin means
    proportional to [−2, −1, 0, 1, 2]. The legacy red variance is 1, and the corrected one is 25/18,
    in the same squared units.
  - So the correction does **not** reproduce the legacy values for equal σ. It reproduces K25's
    form with its own divisors.
- **B6.** The precise point's residual about m_b is about 0, so s² stays near 1. Its bin enters Q₀
  with a residual near 0.
- **P2.4, P9.5.** W_odd is smaller by the ratio², so the white term of σ_δ,odd grows by the
  ratio, which is what the parity's noise is (blind review, N10: the red term does not scale so).
- **T2, T6.** W_odd grows by the precise point's weight, so σ_δ,odd shrinks and the test regains
  sensitivity.
