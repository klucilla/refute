# v0.5.6 Apply with declared limits: plan

**Status:** proposed under the maintainer's decision of 2026-11-21 (21:00, #218). **Part A approved
as recorded on #218** (the decision of 22:30, item 3, and the addendum of 22:45, item 3): 240 TOIs,
two arms of 120, about 36 CPU-h, a cap of 50 CPU-h. `method_status: not_validated`.

**The frame as approved** (`docs/validation/v0.5.6/part_a_frame.py`, after Codex's review): TICs
with more than one distinct TOI, with an incomplete or non-positive ephemeris, or whose TOI matches
more than one TESS planet are excluded, each exclusion recorded per TIC. The tolerance is 1 % of
PSCompPars' positive period, inclusive. `tfopwg_disp` is descriptive only (the addendum of 22:45):
never eligibility, arm, split or draw. Where the text below differs, this paragraph holds.

**Principle:** explore freely, conclude strictly. Every output that uses the v0.5 battery carries
its limits label: **"WEAKENED; does not meet D1, D2, D4, T2a"**. No earlier verdict or target
changes.

## Part A: exploratory

**What runs:** the v0.5 battery, unchanged and labelled, on public TOIs with SPOC two-minute light
curves. The ephemerides come from the NASA Exoplanet Archive's TOI table, read by a query that
names its columns and leaves `tfopwg_disp` out.

**The proposed set: 240 TOIs, two arms of 120.** The arms are defined only by open sources
([sources record](sources/v0.5.6-open-sources-2026-11-21.md)):
- **Planet arm:** the TIC is in the Archive's PSCompPars with `disc_facility` TESS, and the
  period matches within 1 %.
- **Open arm:** no such match.

**The sampling frame, frozen before any light curve is read** (committed with its query, its
date and its hashes):
- **The ephemeris source**, its required columns and its matching keys, recorded before part A's
  approval (see "Data use").
- **Eligibility:**
  - a TOI with SPOC two-minute light curves;
  - an ephemeris (period, epoch, duration, depth);
  - at least two transits **predicted** from the ephemeris and the products' metadata (sectors
    and time ranges), without reading any light curve. The observed coverage is recorded after
    the draw.
- **Ambiguous cases:** a TIC with several TOIs, or a TOI matching more than one confirmed planet,
  is assigned by a written rule (the closest period; ties excluded) and logged.
- **The two arms select different populations.** The quotas are a sampling choice, not a
  population estimate.

**Split and draw:**
- First, the frame is split at random **by TIC** into an **exploration half** and a
  **confirmation half**. Every TOI and every data product of a TIC stays in one half.
- The split is logged with its seed and committed before any light curve is read.
- Targets used in Refute's earlier development (v0.1 to v0.5.5 records) are listed, and are
  excluded from the confirmation half.
- Part A draws only from the exploration half.
- An exploratory draw needs no drand round: the seed and the reason for the choice are logged.

**Cost:**
- **CPU.** v0.5's real controls give the estimate: C3 had 21 targets OK and 1 ERROR, costing
  3.10 CPU-h ([cost.jsonl](../../calibration/v0.5/runs/c3/cost.jsonl)).
  - That is 0.148 CPU-h per target on average (median 0.083, maximum 0.573).
  - C3 ran the battery twice, with and without the catalog test, and read pixel files too: the
    figure is a planning estimate from a different workload.
  - So 240 TOIs need about **36 CPU-h** (35.47 CPU-h using the unrounded mean).
  - The proposed cap is **50 CPU-h**, and it gets a caps ledger (the run is above 5 CPU-h). The
    pilot, interrupted attempts and reruns all count against the cap.
- **Wall time.** About 2 hours at 20 workers outside business hours, an ideal projection. Batches
  of up to 30 minutes, every run resumable.
- **Downloads and disk:** not measured; **not confirmed**. A 10-TOI pilot measures them first,
  inside the same approval.

**Why this angle:**
- v0.5 was measured on synthetic populations, a paired benchmark and 21 real controls, but never
  on a broad sample of real TOIs.
- The open question is practical. On real TOIs, how often is each outcome reached, and what does
  the battery do where its failed targets matter (EB-like signals for D1, background cases for
  T2a)?
- The question is also whether its outcomes line up with independent open evidence (confirmed
  planets; SPOC's own DV diagnostics).

**What we expect to learn:**
- outcome rates per arm, with coverage;
- the tests that end INCONCLUSIVE most often, and why;
- the cases where the battery's REFUTED meets a confirmed planet, and which test caused it;
- agreement with SPOC's DV centroid and odd-even statistics, as context only (not labels).

**What would count as "looks like a door".** These are written before the run and are
exploratory, never verdicts. Rates are always taken **within the planet arm**, and the
denominators are the arm's TOIs with nonzero coverage.
1. **A subgroup.** It is defined by observable properties only (depth, magnitude, number of
   transits, sectors) and holds at least 20 planet-arm TOIs with nonzero coverage. It is compared
   with **the rest of the planet arm**, whose denominator must be nonzero:
   - its SURVIVED rate is at least twice the rest's (if the rest has zero SURVIVED, at least 5
     SURVIVED in the subgroup);
   - its INCONCLUSIVE rate is at most half the rest's.
2. **A systematic disagreement:** at least 5 planet-arm TOIs REFUTED with the same cause. The
   cause is the **first decisive fatal FAIL** in the battery's order (the one that makes the
   outcome REFUTED), with its recorded reason.
3. **A dominant cause:** a test with at least 10 INCONCLUSIVE results, more than half of them
   from one recorded cause that can be named and checked. The cause is **the recorded cause of
   each INCONCLUSIVE result of the test being evaluated**.

**How the doors are read:**
- Each door's counts and denominators are recorded.
- Ties between causes count for neither.
- If several doors qualify, the first in this list's order proceeds to part B; the others are
  reported.

## Part B: confirmatory

A door from part A becomes a claim (`claim.yaml`):
- **Hypothesis and predictions:** stated on the subgroup or the cause.
- **Frozen before the drand draw:**
  - the eligible confirmation frame;
  - the exclusions for prior exposure;
  - the sample size;
  - the decision rule;
  - the policy for missing data.
- **Pass/fail criteria:** fixed before any confirmation data is read.
- **Lock:** a pre-registration lock (code, `uv.lock`, claim) made before its targets are read.
- **Targets:**
  - drawn by a drand round, a stop for the maintainer;
  - only from the **confirmation half** (never used in part A), or prospectively from TOIs
    released after the lock, with the same exclusions.
  - The data products' hashes are recorded, and disjointness from part A (by TIC and by product)
    is verified before any confirmation photometry is read.
- **Run:** once, with Codex up to three rounds on the claim. The verdict is reported as it comes
  (`SURVIVED`, `WEAKENED`, `REFUTED`, `INCONCLUSIVE`), with the limits label.
- **Rule change:** a blind review only if the claim needs a rule change.

## Data use

- **Nothing depends on `tfopwg_disp`** until NExScI answers (question sent on 2026-11-21).
- **Open sources** ([record](sources/v0.5.6-open-sources-2026-11-21.md)):
  - SPOC two-minute light curves (MAST);
  - SPOC TCE bulk statistics (MAST);
  - the Archive's PSCompPars (confirmed planets with their TIC);
  - the TOI table's ephemeris columns.
- **How to compare without the disposition:**
  - confirmed-planet membership gives the planet side;
  - nothing gives a false-positive label, so the open arm stays unlabelled and is described, not
    scored;
  - SPOC's DV statistics are reported next to the battery's outcomes, never as truth.
- **Use of `tfopwg_disp`** is only for the purpose explicitly covered by NExScI's reply. An
  approval for T3b's aggregate count covers that count; any comparison by disposition requires
  confirmation for that use, and is then a new dated record that never changes part A's design.
- **The TOI ephemeris columns:** whether they may be reused is part of the maintainer's approval
  of part A.
  - Before that approval, the ephemeris source, its required columns and its matching keys are
    recorded.
  - The SPOC fallback (the TCE statistics at MAST, matched to the TOI by TIC and by the period
    those statistics carry) is used only if the match can be made with approved inputs. Whether
    the bulk CSV carries the needed columns is checked first (**not confirmed**).
  - If the fallback needs a different sampling frame (for instance, TCEs rather than TOIs), that
    frame goes to the maintainer for approval before it is frozen.
- **What the report says:**
  - the rates describe the sample, not the TOI population;
  - SPOC's DV statistics may share photometry with the battery, so agreement is not
    independence;
  - every output keeps `method_status: not_validated`.

## Exit criteria (verifiable)

1. Part A ran on the approved set, within its cap:
   - the frame, the split by TIC and the draw are committed before any light curve is read;
   - the products, sectors, battery configuration and signal identity are recorded per target,
     with the denominators per arm, coverage losses included;
   - every target reports its coverage (zero coverage is INCONCLUSIVE);
   - every output carries "exploratory" and the limits label.
2. An exploratory report is committed, with the outcomes per arm and each door of the list above
   marked found or not found.
3. Then either:
   - (a) a part B claim was locked before its targets were read, ran once, and its verdict is
     reported as it came; or
   - (b) no door was found, and the phase closes with declared limits.

   If a door is found but part B cannot be locked or run, part B is reported as incomplete and
   this criterion stays open.
4. A public report is drafted for the maintainer's approval, with the limits label and v0.5's
   failed targets (D1, D2, D4, T2a) in view.
5. Codex reviews the phase (up to three rounds, resuming one conversation), and a gate review
   follows.

## What the phase delivers outside

A public report, after the maintainer's approval. It covers what the labelled battery does on real
TOIs, what part B concluded (or that no door was found), and its limits in plain view. Nothing is
submitted to any scientific body, and no candidate is described as a discovery.
