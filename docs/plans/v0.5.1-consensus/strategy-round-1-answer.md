## Objections

1. **Critical, verdict precedence makes the D4 conclusion too strong.**  
   The analysis says any inconclusive decisive test makes the verdict `INCONCLUSIVE`. In the project aggregator, a fatal `FAIL` is checked first and produces `REFUTED`, even when another decisive test is inconclusive ([verdict.py:235](/C:/Projetos/refute/src/refute/core/verdict.py:235)). The development forecast happens to show no `REFUTED` outcomes under the tested readings, but that does not prove D4 fails *whatever the confirmation data*: D4 limits the `INCONCLUSIVE` fraction, and `REFUTED` results do not count toward it. D2 and T2a still fail their “kept” requirements when `odd_even` is inconclusive, but D4 is not fixed by that fact alone.  
   **Exact change:** Replace “D2, D4 and T2a fail by rule, whatever the data” with a claim that D2 and T2a cannot meet their kept thresholds under this rule, while D4’s outcome remains data-dependent because fatal failures can take precedence. Update the path comparison accordingly. The implemented precedence is explicit in [verdict.py:260](/C:/Projetos/refute/src/refute/core/verdict.py:260).

2. **Important, the “cannot be passed by any estimator” statement overstates the power calculation.**  
   The per-row calculation is sound for normally distributed pulls: at \(n=400\), the joint mean and SD bands pass with probability about 0.95. The expected 77 failures across 1,548 rows is also valid without assuming row independence. But the \(3.35\times10^{-35}\) probability that *all* rows pass does assume independence; shared curves make that approximation uncertain. Neither result proves that no estimator can pass this acceptance. The 597 observed failures are strong evidence that this A0 run is materially miscalibrated, not proof of universal impossibility.  
   **Exact change:** Replace the sentence with: “Under the independent-row approximation, passing every row with perfectly calibrated Gaussian pulls is extraordinarily unlikely; A0’s 597 failures, versus about 77 expected, indicate substantial miscalibration.” Keep the dependence caveat beside the joint probability. The numbers are in [strata.json](/C:/Projetos/refute/docs/validation/v0.5.1/feasibility/strategy/strata.json), summarized in [README.md:164](/C:/Projetos/refute/docs/validation/v0.5.1/feasibility/strategy/README.md:164).

3. **Important, the mask experiment supports a hypothesis but does not isolate the cause.**  
   The no-mask run brings the pull scatter closer to 1, which is consistent with the proposed asymmetry: real event windows are masked during detrending, while pseudo-windows used for their error are not. But removing the mask also changes the fitted detrend across the curve, so these numbers alone cannot establish that pseudo-windows are quieter specifically because of that asymmetry. The text acknowledges that the no-mask case is diagnostic, yet states “What changes it is the mask” as a settled cause ([README.md:134](/C:/Projetos/refute/docs/validation/v0.5.1/feasibility/strategy/README.md:134)).  
   **Exact change:** Call the mask asymmetry the leading explanation supported by the diagnostic, not the demonstrated cause. The generic red-noise and look-elsewhere references support the broader risks, but they do not establish this specific mechanism. [Pont, Zucker & Queloz](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/373/1/231/1378493) discuss red noise in transit detection; [Gross & Vitells](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.1891) discuss selection over a search range.

4. **Important, the calibration cost estimate needs reconciliation with the plan’s C2′ estimate.**  
   The analysis projects about 87 calibration hours, including about 14 for C2′ and C3′ ([README.md:178](/C:/Projetos/refute/docs/validation/v0.5.1/feasibility/strategy/README.md:178)). But the plan budgets C2′ at 20 hours and C3′ at 4, and cites the prior measured C2 cost of 39.13 hours for 100 stars. Straight scaling to 50 stars gives roughly 19.6 hours for C2′, or about 23.6 with C3′, unless the 14-hour estimate uses a newer measured rate or a different workload ([plan, C2′ sizing and caps](/C:/Projetos/refute/docs/plans/v0.5.1-plan.md:213), [plan, calibration allocation](/C:/Projetos/refute/docs/plans/v0.5.1-plan.md:278)).  
   **Exact change:** Show the C2′ and C3′ arithmetic and its measurement basis. Until then, use the plan’s estimate: about 73 + 20 + 4 = 97 calibration hours, rather than 87. Either estimate exceeds the 80-hour cap, so the conclusion that a cap decision is needed still stands.

## Claims that survive, with qualifications

- The run-time reading is reasonable: the noise model is unavailable at run time, so a cell should be treated as accepted only when supported across the five calibrated noise models. The `strict`, `ignoring_nd`, and `white_only` readings are distinguished correctly. `odd_even` relies on `matched_diff`; using the lowest coverage stratum and the number of sectors containing measured events is a conservative fit to the group-stratum rule. `INCONCLUSIVE` is the right consequence when a decisive test has no accepted usable error. In particular, the three `odd_even` cells accepted only when ignoring `NOT DECIDABLE` are not evidence of calibration.
- The forecast implementation is broadly consistent with its description: it captures the pipeline’s empirical-event result, applies the rule after sensitivity enforcement, and aggregates the adjusted tests. Its summary should still describe the actual project precedence rather than a blanket inconclusive rule. The v0.5 C2 upper bound is appropriately labeled as a proxy, not a version-3 forecast.
- The pool arithmetic is internally consistent under the stated assumption of using the observed 11/41 acceptance rate: 52 remaining after excluding accepted stars corresponds to roughly one 11-acceptance draw; excluding all 41 walked stars leaves 22 and supports none at that rate. “At best one” is too absolute, since new sectors or changed dispositions could increase the usable pool and the observed rate is an estimate. Phrase it as **about one confirmation at the observed yield**, conditional on the stated exclusions.
- The matched-fit results support same-data shape selection as an explanation for the mean bias: removing the depth lower bound or fixing the shape largely removes the positive mean, while the matched difference remains near zero. This is consistent with boundary-selection theory and a look-elsewhere effect ([Self & Liang](https://pages.stat.wisc.edu/~larget/Stat998/Fall2015/Self-Liang-1987.pdf), [Gross & Vitells](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.1891)). The larger scatter remains unexplained by shape selection alone because it persists with fixed shape.
- The lag comparison supports “not the cause in these tested cases”: extending the lag window to 10 and 20 barely moves the reported pull SDs relative to their stated sampling errors. Keep that conclusion bounded to these simulations and correlation models. The three sabotage results called “not distinguishable” are fairly characterized as weak or absent evidence, not proof that the sabotages have no effect.
- D1 is mathematically met vacuously if no eligible curve is `SURVIVED`, because its metric is `SURVIVED / eligible`. Say explicitly that this would not demonstrate false-positive rejection; `INCONCLUSIVE` outcomes remain outside that numerator.
