## What goes there

1. **Calibration with measurement** on development hosts (exploratory). It starts with a
   bounded, metered pilot.
   - Before the first run, the rules are frozen: aggregation, invalid values, availability,
     denominators, ties and outcome vocabulary.
   - The workers are immutable, and their hashes are checked before each run.
   - The orbit and sector scramble and the inversion transforms are part of this phase, not of
     v0.5.11.
2. **The pools,** each behind its own selection protocol and seed, committed before selection:
   - **DEV-T:** exposed TOIs. The v0.5.9 hosts are deduplicated against the part B draw. Codex
     counted 18 overlaps, which leaves 92 planets and 77 FPs in the historical records.
   - **DEV-H:** exposed hosts with no TOI, CTOI, known planet or EB-catalog entry.
   - **DEV-N:** a new named pool of unexposed stars, up to 250.
     - It is decided only after DEV-H is counted, and only for the measured shortfall (Codex's
       recommendation).
     - There is a stop before its draw, and its exposure record is committed before
       acquisition.

   Every pool is reconciled against exposed and embargoed identifiers and excludes every (d1)
   census-source TIC, failing closed.
3. **The named reruns NR-1 and NR-2.** Each needs approval of its execution manifest first: the
   input products and checksums, the command, the configuration, the output and the trigger.
4. **Frozen acceptance targets,** before any version-5 output on hosts.
   - The draft's starting values:
     - A1, completeness: Wilson lower bound at least 0.85;
     - A2, effectiveness: at least 0.90;
     - A3, the planet guard: REFUTED at most 0.05, upper bound at most 0.10.
   - Loss handling and the calibration and futility rules are frozen with them.
   - Every known eligible `unassessed` case stays in its denominator.
5. **Sizing** (Codex's blocking item 3). The host count and the stopping rule are frozen from
   conservative yield and loss bounds for both endpoints.
   - The report gives the probability of reaching each eligible count, and the joint probability
     of passing A1 to A3 under declared alternatives.
   - It accounts for host and shared-sector dependence, and for A1 and A3 sharing injections.
   - Codex's arithmetic, exploratory: at a 20 % null yield, 1,250 hosts give 51.1 % probability
     of 250 detections, and about 1,342 give 90 %.
6. **The acceptance document,** a proposed scientific protocol, approved by the maintainer
   before any sealed host is drawn.
7. **The blind review of the rule change.** It is a fresh, read-only Claude session with only a
   hashed bundle, approved first with its purpose, number and cost, and stored unchanged before
   any adoption.
8. **The lock decision.** It is the maintainer's: proceed to a lock in a later phase, correct
   first, or stop.
9. **TLS and wotan as isolated comparators.** They run in an environment of their own, never a
   project dependency.
   - A benefit bar is written first: a paired synthetic recovery and null benefit at a fixed
     cost.
   - A dependency change is a separate stop, only if the bar is met.
   - Recorded pins: TLS 2.3.0 and wotan 2.1
     ([docs/validation/v0.5.10/engines/wheelhouse-manifest.json](../validation/v0.5.10/engines/wheelhouse-manifest.json)).
     Their licence text inside the wheels and their compatibility with NumPy 2.5.3 and Python
     3.13 are not confirmed.

## Safeguards the future charter must keep

These are Codex's blocking item 4 of the round on the v0.5.11 charter, as written
([v0.5.11-consensus/](v0.5.11-consensus/README.md)):

> The future charter must freeze recovery, gate passage, unknown eligibility, loss handling and
> futility before host runs. Keep the evaluation half unread until the calibration artifact is
> frozen. If the prespecified futility rule fires, stop and report under the frozen criteria.
> Stage 4 may populate only fields determined by the frozen sizing procedure. Deduplicate DEV-T
> by TOI and TIC. Require prior issue approval for repeated historical outputs, including
> transforms. Apply the frozen rules, worker and configuration hashes, and append-only journal
> to every run, including comparators. Any project dependency proposal requires separate
> approval.

## A finding of v0.5.11 that must be resolved before any lock

Recorded by the maintainer's decision received on 2026-11-24 at 22:38 local (#218, comment
7149778857), item 3, without investigation in v0.5.11.
- **What was seen.** CI run 45514526728 (commit `84010d0`; `src/` as at `eb70c13`). The output
  of discovery version 3 on a synthetic scenario (`verdict-v3.txt` of
  `tests/baseline_code_v0511.py`) differed in the last digits of some floats, for example
  `amplitude_snr`, between two computations with the same code:
  - one in the pytest process, after thousands of other tests;
  - one in a fresh subprocess.

  This happened on ubuntu-24.04 with Python 3.12 and on windows-2025 with Python 3.13.
- **What was done.** `64f3331` isolates the two sides: both are computed in fresh subprocesses.
- **Why it matters.** The dependence on the process's state is a risk for byte-for-byte
  reproducibility. Its cause must be identified before any lock, and this phase's charter must
  include that work.

## A lesson of v0.5.11 on reviews: one round per question, with a closed scope

Recorded by the maintainer's decision received on 2026-11-25 at 09:34 local (#218, comment
7162573384), item 3.
- **The cost.** v0.5.11's Codex thread used **4,036,836 tokens** over 9 rounds (the real sum of
  the events; [v0.5.11-consensus/README.md](v0.5.11-consensus/README.md)).
- **Why it grew.** The rounds reopened the same numerical question (the weights at the limits of
  float64) three times: in the diff rounds, in a fix by arithmetic (E1), and in the review of all
  commits. The question was closed by an input rule
  ([v0.5.11-spec-correction-1.md](v0.5.11-spec-correction-1.md)), not by review.
- **The lesson for the future charter:**
  - one Codex round per question;
  - the question and its scope are written and closed before the round;
  - a finding outside that scope becomes a recorded item, not a new round in the same thread.
- **Version 5's input rule.** Correction 1 bounds every σ to [1e-12, 1e3]. A curve outside the
  range gives an unassessed screen. Its frequency on real targets is unknown.

## What waits until after v0.5.12

- **Route (d1)'s claim.** Its groups are derived again with full provenance and frozen before
  its own lock (rule (a)). It needs:
  - a common support filter;
  - the exposure reconciliation;
  - label-bearing catalog inputs disabled;
  - a counting rule for unmatched signals and missing data;
  - a power and eligibility gate.
- **A sealed acceptance run of version 5,** under its own lock.

## Caps

v0.5.12 sets its own caps in its charter, from v0.5.11's measured whole-path costs and the
pilot. The draft's 50 CPU-h, 30 GB transferred and 20 GB peak disk (75 CPU-h with the comparator)
are a starting point. Codex found the 25 CPU-h comparator increment tight: the historical TLS
mean implies 23.1 CPU-h for 300 curves before setup.
