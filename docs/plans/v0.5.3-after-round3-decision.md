## (a) Change the approach: a first stage of cheap information checks only

The stage runs four checks, in the independent review's order. Each is designed and reviewed by
Codex, its approval and exact procedure are posted on #217 before it runs, and its rule is frozen
before its data are read. All four use development data only, from existing records, caches and
development seeds, disjoint from every confirmation seed. Nothing in them is an acceptance, a draw,
a lock or a change to `src/`, and no target or test rule changes.

**The outcomes.** Each check ends in one of three ways:
- **route:** the bound leaves room, so the estimator has a target;
- **draft infeasibility:** a draft only. Recording it is the maintainer's non-delegated stop, with
  Codex's and an independent review;
- **unresolved:** brought to the maintainer.

I1 is a diagnostic and cannot end in a draft. I2 ends in route or unresolved until its controls
on `systematics`' FAILs survive review. I3 can end at most in a "fatal-class infeasible"
finding, never in a general D1 draft, and only in route or unresolved until its planet side is
designed.

### I1. What the records cannot tell (first: it defines I2's and I4's populations)

- **What:**
  - **T2a:** the 169 `systematics` losses of B (110 where E4 measured, 17 where it completed no
    measurement, 42 grid refusals), rerun with version 5's frozen composition from the T2a cache,
    logging each sub-check's status, message and inputs.
  - **D2:** the 37 version-5 losses (seeds P 3000 to 3199), logging `odd_even`'s and `sinusoid`'s
    messages, classes and inputs.
  - Each rerun must reproduce its B1 verdict.
- **The rule:** each held loss falls into one class of a table fixed before the run:
  - (i) sensitivity at the declared alternative;
  - (ii) a refusal or procedure (domain, not enough data);
  - (iii) a code defect, which is a binding stop.

  **Route** for (ii). Class (i) is passed to I2 (T2a) and I4 (D2) as their population. A rerun
  that does not reproduce its verdict, a loss that fits no class, or (iii) ends in **unresolved**,
  brought to the maintainer. No case is dropped silently. It never ends in a draft.
- **Price:**
  - 6 to 12 CPU-h (169 injections at about 100 to 250 CPU-s, 37 planets at about 50);
  - 0.45 to 0.65 M tokens (three Codex conversations);
  - 1 to 1.5 agent-days;
  - 2 to 3 calendar days.

### I2. T2a: the per-host bound on the background-rise error

- **What:** for the 84 hosts' background series in the T2a cache, a model of the slow variability
  is fitted per sector with an exact likelihood on gapped data, in two families: a state-space ARMA
  through a Kalman filter (Jones 1980), and a celerite kernel (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2017).
  - **Frozen before any read:**
    - the orders and kernels;
    - the selection within each family;
    - an adequacy test of the standardized residuals at a fixed level;
    - the treatment of a failed or inadequate fit: that sector is unresolved, and its injections
      never enter U.
  - **Then:** the Cramér-Rao error of the rise at each injection's windows, and the sub-check's
    power under its declared alternative (0.8 of the depth at power 0.95, unchanged).
- **The counts:**
  - **U_k:** the injections of 615 information-limited under family k. Injections above 20 sectors
    count only if they are information-limited too.
  - **U_∩:** the injections information-limited under every family.
  - Injections whose host's original background shows a detected rise of its own, by a test
    frozen first, are left out of U.
- **Not kept, not only not passed.** Below 0.95 power, the frozen sensitivity rule makes the
  background sub-check INCONCLUSIVE, which blocks: `aggregate` checks INCONCLUSIVE before WEAKENED.
  - **The only way around it** is a FAIL inside `systematics` itself, from any of its sub-checks;
    the sensitivity rule changes only PASS results. That FAIL gives WEAKENED, which is kept.
  - **A FAIL of another warning test does not help:** it does not remove `systematics`'
    INCONCLUSIVE.
  - **Such a FAIL is not necessarily a false alarm.** Real problems unrelated to the background
    (momentum dumps, SAP against PDCSAP) can produce it, and leaving out the hosts with a
    *detected* rise does not show that no real rise exists.
- **The rule:**
  - **Route** if |U_k| ≤ 63 for every family.
  - **Unresolved** otherwise, as to infeasibility. A draft would need two things:
    - **a bound on `systematics`' FAIL probability on the U population itself:** after the fits
      and exclusions, for every sub-check, with its null justified;
    - **a deduction valid under any dependence,** such as Markov's: draft only if |U_∩| ≥ 64 and
      |U_∩| − 63 ≥ 20 · p_FAIL · |U_∩|, where p_FAIL bounds that probability. Then
      P(kept ≥ 552) ≤ 0.05.

    Both would be designed in I2's own design and reviewed before any read. Until those controls
    survive Codex's review, I2 never ends in a draft.

    *[The tie-break after three Codex rounds (`b1-decision`), recorded: the more conservative
    option, I2 unresolved as to infeasibility without valid controls, as Codex's round 3 stated.]*
  - **Any draft** would be a probabilistic bound under its assumptions, not a deterministic "at
    most 551". It would hold only under the fitted models, the declared alternative, and design
    question 1, which stays open (whether a warning INCONCLUSIVE blocks), with the other blockers
    counted as recoverable.
- **Price:**
  - 3 to 12 CPU-h (v0.5.2 estimated 2 to 10 for one family);
  - 0.5 to 0.8 M tokens (three conversations);
  - 2 to 3 agent-days;
  - 3 to 4 calendar days.

### I3. D1: the joint test, with the planet side and the severities

- **What:** B0's oracle (v0.5.1's assumptions, catalog star) on development binaries EB1 and EB2,
  as B0b, together with development planets P and PG. It runs at each level of B0's full grid
  (0.00135, 0.0027, 0.01, 0.05).
  - **Binary side:** D1's allowance, with its Wilson rule **and** "no more SURVIVED than v0.4",
    which B0 left out.
  - **Planet side:** D2's REFUTED fraction (≤ 0.05, Wilson upper end ≤ 0.10), D2's kept fraction,
    D3 and D4.
- **What is still to be designed.** B0 gives a separation bound per binary at a level. It does not
  give the rejection probability of P and PG planets under one common test.
  - I3's own design must first define a single test, its statistic and threshold, and the planets'
    rejection probabilities under it.
  - It must also show that the binaries' and planets' outcomes form a valid joint distribution.
  - It must never replace a planet's probability by its α bound to conclude infeasibility.

  **Until that design survives Codex's review, I3 can end only in route or unresolved.**
- **The rule,** once the design exists:
  - **The joint probability, for one cohort:** the fraction of simulated outcomes in which every
    rule holds at once. The statistic is the median over 2,000 bootstrap cohorts, as in B0b.
  - **A "fatal-class infeasible" finding** if that median is below 0.05 at every level.
  - **Route** if it is at least 0.95 at some level.
  - **Unresolved** otherwise.
- **The limits of the finding:**
  - **It never becomes a general D1 draft by itself.** A warning-severity path stays open:
    binaries flagged by a warning FAIL are WEAKENED, so out of D1's count, and so are the flagged
    planets, which D2 counts as kept and no rule bounds. An INCONCLUSIVE path stays open as well.
    Both need the maintainer's ruling.
  - **The finding is restricted** to the class examined.
- **Price:**
  - 5 to 8 CPU-h (B0b: 2.45 CPU-h for 800 binaries, and about as much for the planets);
  - 0.5 to 0.8 M tokens (three conversations, the design the longest);
  - 1.5 to 2.5 agent-days;
  - 2 to 3 calendar days.

### I4. D2: `odd_even`'s bound with the transit shape as a nuisance parameter

- **What:** on the 178 eligible fresh planets, and on v0.5.2's 112 against stage A's known-template
  bound:
  - the Fisher information of the odd and even depths, together with the transit shape (duration,
    impact parameter, mid-time, and limb darkening if the generator uses it), under the generator's
    covariance (Carter et al. 2008 for the transit's Fisher matrix);
  - the efficient variance of the difference, which is the inverse of the Schur complement;
  - the frozen `odd_even` rule's power at that variance.
- **The rule:**
  - **L** counts the eligible planets whose `odd_even` power at the nuisance bound is below 0.95.
    `odd_even` is fatal: below that power its frozen rule leaves it INCONCLUSIVE, which blocks.
  - **Draft infeasibility** of D2 if L ≥ 23 (so at most 155 kept, against 156), with every other
    blocker counted as recoverable.
  - **Unresolved** if the Fisher matrix is singular, or the model or a computation is invalid, for
    enough planets to move L across 23.
  - **Route** otherwise; the gap between version 5's error and the bound is the estimator's target.
- **Kept apart:** the planets below 0.95 at stage A's oracle sinusoid residual, recomputed on the
  178. They are reported for a qualified PASS only and never enter L, because the sinusoid is a
  warning test and stage A treats it as recoverable for kept retention.
- **Its conditions:** the Cramér-Rao bound for unbiased estimators, the generator's covariance and
  the transit-shape family. It is not a bound for every estimator on real data.
- **Price:**
  - 0.5 to 2 CPU-h;
  - 0.3 to 0.45 M tokens (two conversations, design and result; its code is short and reviewed with
    the design);
  - 1 to 1.5 agent-days;
  - 1 to 2 calendar days.

**Totals:**
- 15 to 35 CPU-h;
- 1.75 to 2.7 M review tokens for Codex, the sum of the per-check estimates. That is eleven
  conversations (three per check, two for I4) at about 150,000 to 270,000 each; round 3's ranged

- 5.5 to 8.5 agent-days, each 8 hours of agent wall clock.
