# v0.5.1 plan: battery corrections

**Status: adopted on 2026-11-06 (WP0)** by the consensus between Claude and Codex under the
maintainer's delegation (three rounds; record at the end). Written before any v0.5.1 code.

The maintainer's decision of 2026-11-06 (tracking issue #208): v0.5 closed as
`WEAKENED` with declared targets that failed; by the advance rule (ROADMAP, decision of
2026-11-05), the next phase is a correction phase on the same line. The maintainer delegated the
scope to a consensus between Claude and Codex, within one direction: **correct the targets that
failed in v0.5 without losing the ones that passed.** No new capability phase (v0.6 onward) starts
before each failed target PASSes or is shown infeasible with the available data (one

maintainer's approval).

`method_status: not_validated` stays in every v0.5.1 output. Refute produces candidates and
verdicts only; nothing in this phase is a confirmed discovery.

## 2. What the v0.5 records show (development data only)

Read on 2026-11-06 from the v0.5 records, which this phase may use only for development (section
5): `.cache/v05/calibration/c1-confirmation/records.jsonl` (SHA-256 `538382d0...`, equal to its
Drive manifest) and `.cache/v05/calibration/c2/records.jsonl`. The scripts that produced the
numbers below are committed with this plan in `docs/plans/v0.5.1-wp0/`.

1. **Planets are lost to the sensitivity rule of four decisive tests, not to refutation.** In C1's
   family P, 83 of 186 eligible curves are INCONCLUSIVE; the decisive tests that refused a PASS:
   `aperture_depth` 65, `sinusoid` 33, `systematics` 28, `odd_even` 23, `period_alias` 1. In C2's
   injections, `systematics` is in 586 of 631 INCONCLUSIVE outcomes, `sinusoid` in 325, `odd_even`
   in 285.
2. **Some INCONCLUSIVE is a defect of the rule's computation, not of the data:**
   - `aperture_depth` and `systematics` (SAP) draw two depths measured from **the same photons**
     as independent (`power.py`, `aperture_blocks`, `systematics_blocks`), and their statistic
     uses `hypot` of the two errors; the large aperture contains the core, and SAP and PDCSAP share
     the photon noise, so the difference's error is overstated and the power understated;
   - `sinusoid` returns INCONCLUSIVE for P at or above 10 d before reading data (#203);
   - `period_alias` counts its 50 simulated sinusoids at the fixed threshold, and the test itself
     prefers a box in 7 of 50 pure sinusoids on the reviewed control (#204).
3. **Some INCONCLUSIVE is honest.** `odd_even`'s alternative is the full depth (#185, the
   maintainer's decision of 2026-11-01): with group errors `e`, it is detectable only when
   `depth / (sqrt(2) e) >= 3 + 1.645`, about SNR 9.3 x the red-noise factor. In family P (target
   SNR uniform 7.1 to 30) that floor alone may hold back about 10 to 18 % of planets (an
   illustrative Gaussian approximation, not a measured floor; WP1 measures it). The counts of
   this section are incidences: one curve can name several tests.
4. **The spurious families that PASSed D1 are partly held by INCONCLUSIVE.** Among eligible
   curves, INCONCLUSIVE with no decisive FAIL: SE 39 of 98, EB1 39 of 190, EB2 67 of 186 (V1 1,
   V2 1, ER 1, NR 2, PX 2). In SE, the diagnostic `single_event` FAILs on 34 of the 45
   INCONCLUSIVE curves. **Consequence:** any correction that turns undetectable PASSes into
   decidable ones also releases these curves, unless a decisive test refutes them. D1 for SE
   cannot be kept without a decisive single-event test.
5. **The surviving eclipsing binaries are mostly folded at half their period.** EB1: 33 of 35
   survivors were found at half the true period (median secondary ratio 0.86 among survivors,
   0.50 among the others); EB2: all 40 at the found period P, true 2P (median odd/even ratio 0.79).
   The C1 generator gives an EB the trapezoid of a planet of radius ratio `sqrt(depth)`, on the
   target, with no ellipsoidal or beaming signal, so a folded EB with near-equal eclipses differs
   from a planet at the found period only by its odd/even depths and by a transit duration too
   long for the found period around the generator's star. A rough bound (each curve's own
   truth; a 3-sigma odd/even test at the generator's noise; a perfect duration bound against a
   central transit at R = 1.05 R_sun) still leaves about 25 (EB1) and 20 (EB2) expected survivors,
   against at most about 11 that D1 allows. This is an exploratory calculation and a risk signal,
   not evidence of infeasibility; WP1 replaces it with a bound fixed before it is computed. Kepler's Robovetter says the same of this case
   (Thompson et al. 2018, A.4.1.4: a binary detected at half its period can be caught by odd/even
   depths only "if the primary and secondary depths are dissimilar enough").
6. **What does not move a failed target.** On-target EBs gain nothing from a centroid (#200); the
   C1 EB families hold no low-mass companion (EBLM) signature; T2a is a stage-1 measurement
   (no pixels, no catalog dependence on TESS-EB's host).

## 4. Feasibility forecast (WP1) and the infeasibility path of each target

**WP1 is a forecast, not a decision** (Codex's round 2, objections 1 to 3): no bound computed
before the code can settle a target. D1 is a rate conditional on the candidate search and the
gate, and it counts SURVIVED only, so a battery could lower it by returning INCONCLUSIVE or
WEAKENED; an infeasibility argument for D1 must therefore be joint with D2 (a battery that keeps
the planets), and conditional on the search. A Cramer-Rao floor bounds an unbiased estimator's
variance, not a rule that uses an estimator's reported error. So WP1 informs the order of the work
and the maintainer; it never removes a target's correction route, and it is never by itself an
infeasibility record.

Computed once, on development data only, before WP2 and WP3 code; recorded in
`docs/validation/v0.5.1/feasibility.md` with its scripts, labelled "diagnostic forecast".

- **D1 EB1, EB2.** On 400 development binaries per family (prefix `refute-v051-feas`), run through
  the frozen v0.5 search and gate (eligibility as in C1): the nearest planet at the found period
  (a seeded multi-start fit of the C1 transit model; radius ratio, impact parameter and epoch
  free; the star's radius within 1.0 +- 0.05 R_sun) and its Mahalanobis distance under the
  generator's own noise (white plus AR(1); the trend given exactly); the optimistic per-curve
  survival `1 - Phi(d - z_0.05)`, as if a battery had a whole-battery false-failure rate of 0.05
  on that nearest planet; the expected count of survivors and how often it would meet D1's
  count at C1' size. It describes which binaries are photometrically close to a planet; it is
  not a bound on the conditional experiment.
- **D2, D4.** On 400 development planets: how many have an `odd_even` or `secondary_eclipse` rule
  (#185's alternatives) that a white-plus-AR(1) error at the Cramer-Rao value would leave not
  detectable: an indication of the floor that the pre-data rule sets, not a bound.
- **T2a.** On the v0.5 C2 development injections: the same indication with each curve's white
  error.
- Cap: 0.7 agent-day and 4 processor-hours; no second forecast.

**The infeasibility path, after the confirmation.** Every failed target has its correction route
built (section 3a) and is measured in the confirmation. A target that still fails there is
outstanding under the advance rule. Its infeasibility record, if Claude and Codex judge one
defensible, is prepared after the confirmation, never before: the confirmation's result, the
forecast, and an argument that holds for the actual conditional, end-to-end experiment (for D1,
jointly with D2: no battery that keeps the planets as D2 requires can bring the binaries'
SURVIVED fraction to D1's bound with photometry on this generator's binaries); it goes to the
maintainer (a stop) and, if approved, to an independent review (budgeted in section 10). It says
"not reachable under the stated rules with these data", names what would change the answer (other
data such as radial velocities; a different alternative, which only the maintainer can decide),
and never claims that no method could ever succeed. Otherwise the target goes to the next
correction round, by the maintainer's decision.

## 6. Calibration outline (the protocol is a stop)

Two batteries on the same candidate, as in v0.5: the v0.4 compatibility battery and the v0.5.1
battery. The compatibility battery is checked against the v0.5 records of the same code path on
synthetic fixtures (the new stars have no v0.4 record).

**Sizes, fixed now as minimums, with no cost-based fallback** (a prefix may only show that the
frozen design does not fit its allocation; then the step stops before the confirmation and the
maintainer decides; it never selects a smaller sample):

- **C1'**: the v0.5 sizes, 200 per family and 100 for PA and PX (2,400 curves). Rationale: the
  same precision as v0.5 (for D1, at about 190 eligible curves the rule allows at most about 11
  survivors); the v0.5 halving rule is not used.
- **C2'**: 50 new stars, 3 transformed curves per star, 10 injections per curve (the v0.4 grid).
  Rationale: about 50 x 10 x 0.73 = 365 recovered injections if the v0.5 recovery fraction
  holds, so a survival proportion near 0.9 has a standard error near 0.016 under independence,
  well inside T2a's margin of 0.10 even with a design effect of 4 from clustering by star; the
  star stays the cluster unit and results are reported per star. At v0.5's measured 39.13
  processor-hours for 100 stars (`calibration/v0.5/runs/c2/report.json`), about 20 hours before
  the cost of the changed code.
- **C3'**: 8 confirmed planets and 8 TESS-EB false positives, 3 ordered reserves per pool, as in
  v0.5.

## 10. Caps, rounds and the stop rule

| Cap | Value | Allocation |
|---|---|---|
| Agent-days | 12 (warning at 9.6) | WP0 1; WP1 0.7; WP2 1.5; WP3 2.5; the EB route of section 3a 0.7; WP4 0.3; WP5 0.5; WP6 0.3; infeasibility records after the confirmation (writing and answering the reviews) 0.5; calibration (protocol, selection, lock, runs, dossier reviews, gate) 3.5; slack 0.5 |
| Processor-hours | 90: 30 development, 60 calibration (warnings at 24 and 48) | development: WP1 4, combined acceptance and test acceptance 15, diagnostics 6, reserve 5; calibration: C1' 30, C2' 20, C3' 4, prefixes 3, reserve 3 |
| Disk | 20 GB (warning at 16) | C3' pixel files about 14, C2' light curves about 1, the rest caches |
| Reviewer tokens | 1.0 M (warning at 0.8 M) | independent reviews of infeasibility records 0.2 M; C3' dossier reviews 0.45 M; the gate 0.35 M (a second gate needs approval) |
| Codex | at most 3 exchange rounds per decision | divergence after 3 rounds goes to the maintainer |

**Accounting.** Agent-days: Claude's working time is the wall-clock time between the UTC start
and end of each working round (logged in the round's comment on the tracking issue), and Codex's
is each run's wall time from its event log; time when both run at once is counted once for each
agent (both are spent); independent review sessions count in reviewer tokens and in agent-days by
their wall time; the sum is divided by 8. Processor-hours: each run's `cost.jsonl` (task CPU plus
the parent's, as in v0.5); development runs log theirs in `.cache/v051/cost.jsonl`. Disk: point
measurements of the phase's folders with the time (baseline 2026-11-06: `.cache/v051` empty,
`.cache/v05` 0.30 GB). Every independent review needs the maintainer's approval of its number
and cost before it runs (CLAUDE.md, rule 5 of the working mode).

**Correction rounds.** v0.5.1 is correction round 1 of at most 3 (v0.5.1, v0.5.2, v0.5.3); after
the third without success, the maintainer decides between a change of approach, an infeasibility
record or a pause. Inside v0.5.1 there is one confirmation, never rerun.

**Stop rule** (against the v0.2.1 failure mode):
1. One acceptance section per WP and at most one revision after Codex's review, before its code;
   then only bounded repairs to meet the frozen specification.
2. A WP whose agent-days or processor-hours exceed 1.5 times its allocation stops; what is
   accepted stays, the rest is recorded as not accepted.
3. Reserve invariant: before each WP, the remaining budget must cover the calibration allocation
   and the infeasibility reviews; otherwise implementation stops and the scope is reduced,
   recorded.
4. A failed target after the confirmation: no rerun, no retuning; the maintainer decides.
5. At any hard cap, a terminal report; no extension.
6. **An incomplete phase is never a success.** A cap reached, a scope reduction, a WP stopped or a
   confirmation not run leaves every failed target outstanding: it counts neither as a PASS nor
   as infeasible. Only a PASS in the confirmation, or an infeasibility record approved by the
   maintainer after Codex's and an independent review, settles a target under the advance rule.
