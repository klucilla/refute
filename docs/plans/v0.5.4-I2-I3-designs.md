# v0.5.4: the designs of I2 and I3, on paper (not frozen; neither runs)

**The maintainer's decision of 2026-11-12, 14:00, item 3 (#217):** I2 and I3 are written and
reviewed by Codex in the first half. They are **frozen only after I1's result**, which defines I2's
population, and **neither runs** before the maintainer's decision on the second half. Their outcomes
are those of [`v0.5.3-after-round3-decision.md`](v0.5.3-after-round3-decision.md):
- **I2** ends in route or unresolved. It never ends in a draft until controls on `systematics`'
  FAIL probability on the bounded population survive review.
- **I3** ends in route or unresolved until its planet side is designed. At most it gives a
  fatal-class finding, never a general D1 draft.

`method_status: not_validated`. Development data only. No change to `src/`, to a battery, a target
or a test rule.

**Not a dependency.** Neither design adds a dependency or runs third-party code. The `celerite`
package is not installed: using it would be a non-delegated stop (a dependency, and third-party
code). The celerite-type kernels below are therefore fitted with this project's own code, as
continuous-time state-space models through a Kalman filter on the irregular times, using numpy and
scipy only.

## I2. T2a: the per-host bound on the background-rise error

### The population and the data

- **All 615 injections of T2a's census**, in 84 hosts (as B1.4's census):
  - their SAP background series (`SAP_BKG`) per sector, from the T2a cache;
  - each injection's final candidate, its in-transit events and windows as the sub-check uses
    them, and the SAP depth and its error. B1.4's records do not store the SAP depth's error
    (`check_systematics`' metrics omit it), so I2 rebuilds each injection as I1 does and calls the
    sub-check's own measurement on the SAP and background series. The full battery does not run.
  - The 56 above 20 sectors use configuration C's measurement, **a scenario conditional on the
    selector's certification**, never a certification granted.
- **I1's class-(i) losses with `background` non-detectable** are reported as a subgroup. U counts
  over all 615, as the decision page says.

### The model (frozen when this design is frozen, after I1)

Per host and sector, the background series is fitted by maximum likelihood, in two families, each
an exact Gaussian likelihood on the irregular, gapped times:
- **family ARMA:** discrete ARMA(p, q) on the cadence grid, with the gaps as missing observations
  (Kalman, Jones 1980), (p, q) ∈ {(1, 0), (1, 1), (2, 1)}, plus a white term;
- **family kernel:** continuous-time kernels of celerite's family (Foreman-Mackey et al. 2017), in
  state-space form. The candidates are one exponential (an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process), one
  stochastically driven damped oscillator, and their sum, each plus a white term.

Common to both families (to be frozen in full, numerically, before the freeze commit; Codex's
round 1 asks for every one):
- **Parametrizations, bounds and starts** (v the sector's sample variance after its mean and
  trend, L its length in days, Δ = 2 minutes):
  - **ARMA:** the AR and MA coefficients through partial autocorrelations in (−0.99, 0.99)
    (Monahan's mapping), so the process is stationary and invertible. The innovation variance and
    the white variance are given as logs in [log(10⁻⁶ v), log(10 v)]. Starts: the AR partial
    autocorrelations at 0.0, 0.5 and 0.9, the MA ones at 0, and the variances at 0.9 v and 0.1 v.
  - **Ornstein-Uhlenbeck kernel:** log σ² in [log(10⁻⁶ v), log(10 v)] and log τ in [log Δ, log L].
    Starts: τ at 0.1, 1 and 5 days, σ² = 0.9 v.
  - **Damped-oscillator kernel** (celerite's SHO term): log S₀ unbounded above 10⁻¹² v, log ω₀
    with ω₀ in [2π/L, 2π/(2Δ)], and log Q with Q in [0.1, 100]. Starts: Q at 0.5, 1/√2 and 5, and
    ω₀ at 2π/2 days. The sum kernel starts from the best single fits.
  - **The white term:** a log variance in [log(10⁻⁶ v), log(10 v)], started at 0.1 v.
  - **Not yet fixed, an impediment to implementation** (round 3): S₀'s start, and the three starts
    of the sum kernel. They must be fixed in the implementation's review, before any fit. No
    choice is left to be made during the fits.
- **The ARMA's time grid:** the 2-minute cadence grid of each sector, with the missing cadences
  as missing observations. The state is initialized at its stationary distribution, and the
  kernels' state from the stationary covariance.
- **The optimizer:** `scipy.optimize.minimize`, L-BFGS-B, gradient tolerance 10⁻⁶, at most 500
  iterations. Three frozen starting points per model, the best likelihood kept, ties broken by the
  simpler model.
- **Selection** within a family by BIC, per sector.
- **The sector's mean:** a constant and a linear trend, fitted jointly.
- **Adequacy, a heuristic diagnostic, not a calibrated test:** a Ljung-Box statistic on the
  standardized innovations at lag 20, compared with χ² on 20 − (p + q) degrees of freedom (20
  minus the kernel's free parameters) at 0.01.
  - An inadequate, failed or non-converged fit makes that sector **unknown**.
  - Passing at lag 20 does not certify all the slow variability. The bound is conditional on the
    fitted model.

### The bound and the power

- **Two errors per injection,** kept apart (Codex's round 1: the GLS box error is not, by identity,
  the error of the rise the sub-check measures):
  - **σ_op, the sub-check's own estimator under the fitted covariance.** The rise is `−g_bkg.mean`
    of A0's group depth on the background series: per-event depths, each against its local
    baselines, then A0's inverse-variance weights. In B1's version 5, E4's window-local quadratic replaces the baselines: a constant, a
    linear and a quadratic term per window, with a fallback to the bands' mean. The series has no
    earlier detrending in the sub-check: `depth` reads the raw background (`sap_bkg`). With its
    adaptive choices frozen as the rebuild made them (events kept, each window's quadratic or
    fallback, the weights), the measured rise is a linear contrast `c` of the series. Then
    `σ_op = sqrt(cᵀ Σ c)`, computed with the Kalman filter. **It is the variance of the procedure
    with those choices fixed, not of the full adaptive procedure.**
    - **Corrected in round 3:** receiving the raw `sap_bkg` does not remove an *internal*
      detrending. E4 calls `AdditiveDetrender` and measures the transformed series
      (`battery_b1.py`). So `c` must include that transformation, with its adaptive choices frozen
      as the rebuild made them, or Σ must be transformed to match.
    - **Before σ_op and σ_GLS are compared,** a unit response to the amplitude and the removal of the
      nuisances must be shown, on each injection. Where they are not shown, the injection is
      **K** (unknown), and no information limit is declared for it.
  - **σ_GLS, an information bound for the same amplitude.** The amplitude is the in-transit rise on
    the same events the sub-check uses. The nuisances are the ones the sub-check removes: per event
    window a constant, a linear and a quadratic term (E4's), or a constant where the rebuild fell
    back to the bands' mean. Its error under the fitted covariance is
    `sqrt([(Zᵀ Σ⁻¹ Z)⁻¹]_rise)`. It is a bound for unbiased estimators of that amplitude under that
    model, not for every estimator, and with the selected covariance plugged in it is not a
    universal limit.
- **The power:** `background_block`'s own rule (`battery_v51_diff.background_block`), unchanged.
  The sub-check's alternative is a rise of `max_background_fraction +
  background_alternative_increment` of the SAP depth (0.8 of the depth). The rejection needs
  `sigma ≥ max_background_sigma` and the fraction above `max_background_fraction`, both with
  `>=`, and `s_hat > 0`, simulated at the block's frozen seed, with A0's error replaced by
  `σ_GLS`. The SAP depth and its error are the sub-check's own, from the rebuild. Like the block
  itself, this treats the measured rise and depth as Gaussian and independent. That is the
  block's model, not a proof that it represents the real measurement.
- **An injection is information-limited** under a family if the power at `σ_GLS` is below 0.95.
  The power at `σ_op` is reported beside it, as the current estimator's distance from the bound.

### The counts and the rule

- **The counts:**
  - **U_k:** the injections of 615 information-limited under family k. Injections above 20
    sectors count only if they are information-limited too.
  - **U_∩:** the injections information-limited under both families.
  - **Left out of U:** injections whose host's original background shows a detected rise of its
    own at the same windows. The test is frozen: the same amplitude and nuisances as `σ_GLS`, on the
    un-injected host, at least 3 of its errors.
  - **K_k:** the injections that are unknown under family k: an unknown sector, a failed rebuild,
    or a non-reproducing measurement. They are counted apart and never dropped.
- **The rule:**
  - **route** if |U_k| + |K_k| ≤ 63 for both families. The unknowns cannot turn a route into
    anything else;
  - **unresolved** otherwise.
- **Never a draft in this design.** A draft would need a bound on `systematics`' FAIL probability
  on the U population itself: after the fits and exclusions, for every sub-check (momentum dumps,
  SAP against PDCSAP, background), with its null justified. Then a Markov deduction: draft only if
  |U_∩| ≥ 64 and |U_∩| − 63 ≥ 20 · p_FAIL · |U_∩|. That bound is not designed here, and designing
  it is a later step for the maintainer.

### Price

- **CPU:**
  - the rebuilds: 615 injections at about 20 to 60 CPU-s each (the candidate and the
    sub-check's own measurement only), so 3 to 10 CPU-h;
  - the fits: 84 hosts × about 9 sectors × 2 families × up to 3 models × 3 starts, about 13,600
    starts. At up to 500 iterations of one Kalman likelihood over about 13,000 cadences, at 0.05 to
    0.2 s each, that is **95 to 380 CPU-h at the upper iteration count** (Codex's round 2
    arithmetic), before the gradient's extra evaluations. Early convergence would lower it, but
    nothing measured supports a smaller figure.
  - **In all, above the second half's 25 CPU-h, and possibly above the phase's 50.** A timed
    smoke on a few sectors is a run, so it comes only after the maintainer's authorization. Without
    a measured, smaller cost, I2 cannot run within this phase's caps.
- 0.5 to 0.8 M tokens; 2 to 3 agent-days.

## I3. D1: the joint test with the planet side and the severities

### What is missing before I3 can run

B0's oracle gives each binary's non-rejection probability `Φ(z_α − d)` against its nearest planet,
at a level α. That is a separate most powerful test for each binary, not one test applied to every
curve. A joint rule needs **one common test**, and the planets' rejection probabilities under it.
This design defines that test. Until it survives Codex's review, frozen after I1, I3 can end only in
route or unresolved.

### The common test (proposed)

- **The statistic:** for every curve (binary or planet), `T = 2 (ℓ_planet − ℓ_binary)`, the
  log-likelihood of the best planet model minus that of the best binary model. The numerator is
  the planet family and the denominator the binary family, so large T favours the planet and
  small T flags a binary. The families are B0's generator families.
  - **Aliases:** the binary family includes both the found period and twice it.
  - **The catalog star** enters as a **penalized** likelihood: Gaussian penalties of 5 % on the
    radius and mass, not a marginalization. B0 used hard three-sigma bounds on the effective
    radius instead (`d1_bound.py`), so this is **not B0's oracle made common**. It is a new test,
    and B0's bound does not carry over to it.
  - **Not yet fixed, an impediment to implementation** (round 3): the starts, the bounds and the
    convergence criteria. A failed fit is unknown. No choice is left to be made during the fits.
- **The threshold** at level α is the **lower α quantile** of T over a planet calibration set
  (small T flags a binary).
  - **The estimator:** the empirical quantile by the inverse of the empirical distribution function
    (Hyndman and Fan's type 1).
  - **Rejection** happens only if a curve's T is **strictly below** that quantile, so ties never
    reject.
  - **No population guarantee:** that is an estimated calibration of the mixture. It does not
    guarantee a population level of at most α (round 3), so it is subject to the uncertainty
    treatment below.
  - **The calibration set** is frozen: 2,000 eligible development curves, 1,000 P and 1,000 PG,
    seeds `refute-v054-i3-cal:<family>:<i>`. Eligibility is D2's (the gate passed, the identity
    held). It is disjoint from the evaluation set and from every confirmation seed.
  - The quantile calibrates that mixture. It does not guarantee level α for each planet.
- **The planet side:** each evaluation planet's rejection probability is estimated by Monte Carlo,
  as the fraction of N noise realizations, under the generator's covariance, whose T crosses the
  threshold. It is never replaced by α.
- **The uncertainty is propagated, by a frozen procedure:**
  - the quantile: 1,000 bootstrap resamples of the calibration set, so the whole joint computation
    is repeated at the 2.5 % and 97.5 % quantiles of the threshold;
  - each Monte Carlo probability: its Clopper-Pearson 95 % interval. With N = 200, zero rejections
    still allow about 1.5 % at the one-sided 95 % level;
  - the cohorts: the median over 2,000.

  **Corrected in round 3: the ends of the intervals do not bound the joint probability.** More
  rejection helps D1 on the binaries and hurts D2 on the planets, so the joint probability can peak
  inside the region. Separate Clopper-Pearson intervals also give no simultaneous coverage, and
  the cohorts' median alone does not measure the Monte Carlo error. The decision therefore needs
  **a valid envelope over the whole region of uncertainty, with its joint coverage stated**:
  - **route** only if the envelope's lower bound is at least 0.95 at some level;
  - **a fatal-class finding** only if its upper bound is below 0.05 at every level;
  - **unresolved** otherwise, and in particular **unresolved while no such envelope is designed**.

  Designing the envelope is an impediment to running I3.
- **The binary side:** the same, for each EB1 and EB2 development binary.
- **The joint distribution, as a conditional scenario** (Codex's round 1: one draw of the new test
  per curve does not give the full vector the rules need):
  - every other outcome of each curve is fixed at its recorded value: version 5's verdict, v0.4's
    paired verdict, eligibility, identity, and the SNR ≥ 15 subset. Only the new test's outcome is
    drawn. By `aggregate`'s precedence, a fatal FAIL of the new test turns **any** verdict whose
    gate passed into REFUTED: SURVIVED, WEAKENED and **INCONCLUSIVE** alike. A curve whose gate
    failed stays INCONCLUSIVE. So the new test can also lower D4's INCONCLUSIVE count while raising
    D2's REFUTED count, and both are checked;
  - D1's allowance and its v0.4 clause, and D2's REFUTED fraction, its Wilson upper end, its kept
    fraction, D3 (over its own families: PG, PV and PA) and D4, are checked at once on each draw;
  - 2,000 draws per cohort and 2,000 bootstrap cohorts of the projected sizes (C1''s), with the
    median joint probability over the cohorts.
  - **The scenario's limit:** with the other outcomes fixed, D4 can become trivial. The conclusion
    carries that limit. A full simulation of the vector by shared realizations would need every
    battery run per realization, priced far above this phase.
- **The rule, at each α of B0's grid (0.00135, 0.0027, 0.01, 0.05):**
  - **a fatal-class infeasible finding** if the median joint probability is below 0.05 at every
    level;
  - **route** if it is at least 0.95 at some level;
  - **unresolved** otherwise.
- **The warning class** is reported apart.
  - Flagged binaries and planets become WEAKENED, when no fatal FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE takes
    precedence. D2 counts a WEAKENED planet as kept, and no rule bounds it.
  - The INCONCLUSIVE path is open as well.
  - Both go to the maintainer as questions, never as a route or a finding.
  - **The finding stays "this test, under these hypotheses":** a failure of this likelihood-ratio
    test does not show that the whole fatal class fails, and never shows a general infeasibility
    of D1.

### Price

- **The cost of the planet side:** 200 realizations × the two fits per curve. With about 800
  binaries and 600 planets, that is about 280,000 pairs of fits. Extrapolated directly from B0b
  (2.451 CPU-h for 800 oracle evaluations), that is **about 860 CPU-h**. That is not a measurement
  of these fits, but it is far above the second half's 25 and the phase's 50.
- **Before any run,** the design must reduce it, and justify the reduction:
  - fewer realizations, with exact binomial bounds; or
  - an asymptotic distribution for T. Wilks' conditions are not automatic for this composite,
    constrained problem, so that would need its own demonstration on a subset.

  Otherwise I3
  waits for the maintainer.
- **Tokens and time:** 0.5 to 0.8 M tokens; 1.5 to 2.5 agent-days.

## Freeze status (2026-11-12, after I1; the tie-break after three Codex rounds, `h1-i2i3`)

These two designs are **frozen as paper designs only**. They carry round 3's conservative options
above and stay **conditional and unexecuted**.

**Impediments to running,** each to be resolved by a new, reviewed design step and by the
maintainer's authorization:
- **I2:**
  - the internal detrending in the contrast, and the demonstration of unit response and nuisance
    removal;
  - the remaining numerical starts;
  - a measured, smaller cost: the estimate is 95 to 380 CPU-h, against a second half of 25.
- **I3:**
  - the starts, bounds and convergence criteria;
  - the joint-coverage envelope;
  - a justified reduction of its cost, about 860 CPU-h by extrapolation.

Neither runs before the maintainer's decision on the second half.

## What happens after I1

**I1's result** (`67e179d`, `docs/validation/v0.5.4/i1-diagnosis.json`): of the 169 `systematics`
losses of T2a's B, 103 are class (i). In 102 of them the `background` block is non-detectable, with a
median power of 0.371, and in 1 it is SAP. The other 66 are class (ii), 42 of them grid refusals.

- **I2:** I1 confirms I2's purpose. Its 102 cases are the reported subgroup; U is still counted over
  all 615. (I1's median power of 0.371 is over the 103 class-(i) cases, the 102 included.)
- **I3:** independent of I1.
- **The freeze:** both designs are frozen (committed) only after I1's result, with any change
  disclosed. Neither runs before the maintainer's decision on the second half.
