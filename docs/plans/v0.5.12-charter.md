## 1. The question

Are version 5's core (F1, decided by OE_box) and its report-only quantities (F1_trap, F2, F3, F4,
U1) correct enough to start the calibration?

It answers with three items, each with evidence:
- **#222:** F1's rejection rate on equal-depth synthetic curves with unequal σ, measured with the
  project's code against a limit fixed here;
- **#223:** one synthetic curve for each case of v0.5.11's step 1, with assertions of `computed`
  and of values against their expectation, and one test per `unavailable` path;
- **F-1:** the cause of version 3's process-state dependence, found and recorded with synthetic
  fixtures only.

It runs no calibration and measures nothing on real targets. It produces no verdict and no
acceptance, and it creates no lock. "Correct enough" means only that these three items met their
fixed rules; it adopts nothing.

### Step 1. #222, the measurement M1 (pre-registered here)

**The question.** With the project's code at the approval commit, how often does F1 give `fail` on
equal-depth curves whose σ is correct but unequal?

**Codex's blocking item 1, as written** (the null at the level F1 measures):

> Keep the specified times, sectors, seven σ masks, and cadence selectors. Use a noiseless box with
> flux `1 − 0.002` on F1's exact `≤ T/2` mask and `1` elsewhere. S9.5 varies noise by sector and
> preserves the actual parity sampling. Freeze the RNG implementation and stream before M1. The
> ideal known-variance benchmark is 0.0026997961. Under that benchmark and full assessment, the
> probability all seven pass is at least 0.997980 by the union bound. The families share noise
> draws.

**The rule, fixed before any measurement.**
- **The threshold.** F1 fails when OE_box > 3 (K25 IV.4, Eq. 11: "We fail TCEs with OEbox>3";
  [sources/v0.5.11-screen-papers-2026-11-24.md](sources/v0.5.11-screen-papers-2026-11-24.md)).
  With calibrated noise, OE_box under equal depths is |Z| with Z standard normal, so the
  known-variance benchmark is P(|Z| > 3) = erfc(3/√2) = 0.0026997961.
- **The limit.** For each family, the 95 % Wilson score upper bound of the rejection rate must be
  **at most 1 %** (z = 1.959964), and every case must be assessed. With n = 3000 cases per
  family, this is **at most 19 `fail` of 3000, and 0 `not_assessed`**.
  - The upper bounds for 19 and 20 failures are 0.98710 % and 1.02753 % (Codex's check).
  - Per family, the probability of passing is 99.9711 % at the benchmark, 26.9643 % at a true
    rate of 0.75 %, and 2.1383 % at 1 %. All seven pass with probability at least 0.997980 at
    the benchmark (the union bound; the families share noise draws).
  - The arithmetic is in [v0.5.12-consensus/wilson_design.py](v0.5.12-consensus/wilson_design.py)
    (the standard library); Codex checked it.
- **The curves.** For each seed s and family:
  - the times and sectors come from `synthetic.make_lightcurve(PLANET, seed=s, noise_ppm=0.0)`
    (two sectors, `TWO_YEARS`; `PLANET`: P = 3.7 d, t0 = 1326.3, T = 2.5/24 d), whose times do
    not depend on s;
  - **the model is a noiseless box:** flux 1 − 0.002 on F1's exact in-transit mask,
    |t − (t0 + kP)| ≤ T/2 with k = ⌊(t − t0)/P + 0.5⌋, and 1 elsewhere. Every transit has the same
    depth on the cadences F1 averages;
  - σ0 = 600 ppm, the default of `make_lightcurve`;
  - **the noise stream, frozen:** z = `numpy.random.default_rng([s, 222]).standard_normal(N)`, with
    NumPy's default bit generator (PCG64) at the version in `uv.lock`; N is the number of
    cadences;
  - flux = box + σ_i·z_i, and `flux_err` = σ_i. **σ is correct for every cadence.** The families
    share z.
- **The seven families** ("odd" means k odd):

  | Family | σ_i | What it tests |
  |---|---|---|
  | U | σ0 for every cadence | the control, the uniform case |
  | S9.5 | σ0 in the first sector, 9.5·σ0 in the second | a noise change by sector, with the actual parity sampling |
  | P2.4 | 2.4·σ0 for every cadence of an odd epoch, σ0 otherwise | a noisier parity |
  | P9.5 | 9.5·σ0 for every cadence of an odd epoch, σ0 otherwise | a much noisier parity |
  | T2 | σ0/100 for one cadence, σ0 otherwise | a precise point inside a transit |
  | T6 | σ0/10⁶ for the same cadence, σ0 otherwise | a very precise point inside a transit |
  | B6 | σ0/10⁶ for one baseline cadence, σ0 otherwise | a very precise point in the baseline |

  - The T2 and T6 cadence: among the in-transit cadences of the smallest odd k that has any, the
    one nearest the transit's centre; a tie goes to the earlier time.
  - The B6 cadence: the earliest cadence of the baseline (d(t, 0) > T, the specification's
    baseline).
  - Every σ_i lies in [1e-12, 1e3] (correction 1), so no family is unassessed by the input rule.
- **The seeds of M1:** s = 1 to 3000, the same seeds for every family.
- **The call:** `refute.packs.tess.screen_d5.f1_box(time, flux, flux_err, 3.7, 1326.3, 2.5/24)`,
  the project's code, with the true P, t0 and T.
- **The record:** per family, the counts of `pass`, `fail` and `not_assessed`, of OE_box > 2, and
  the Wilson bound; the per-seed OE_box values in committed JSON files, one per family (about
  0.1 MB each; every required output stays in git, below 5 MB per file, split by seed range if
  needed).
- **The script** is committed before its run, as one unit spec and one script under
  `docs/validation/v0.5.12/`, with the recipe above and its realized input identity (section 4).
  A rule written after the run is post hoc and is labelled so.

**The outcome.**
- **All seven families pass:** item 1 is met by M1. No correction of F1 is written.
- **Any family fails:** the finding is shown with the project's code. Step 4 follows. M1's
  results stay as recorded and are never replaced.

### Step 2. #223, the cases and the `unavailable` paths

procedure):

> Before the first probe, commit the cases, expected fields, and tolerances. Require a `computed`
> witness with numeric assertions for each report-only record. Assert F2's significance and
> occultation first-condition flags where applicable. Include a genuine threshold crossing and
> independently assert isolation from the core and level 2. Use absolute error at most
> `max(atol, rtol × |expected|)`, with a predeclared absolute floor in the metric's units. Use
> `rtol=1e-9` for closed-form values and `1e-3` for identifiable noiseless fitted values,
> comparing epochs as offsets in duration units. For noisy metrics, use five analytic standard
> errors only when justified for the actual statistic, including selection. Otherwise freeze a
> deterministic independent oracle on the realized fixture. Inventory branches by entry point and
> record, distinguish natural witnesses from fault injection, and require a proof and issue for
> unreachable branches. Run the committed baseline probe before pushing the test suite. Only the
> documented, narrowly matched assertion failure may become `xfail(strict=True, raises=...)`. Stop
> on any other failure. Final evidence requires every specified ID to pass with zero skips or
> xfails.

**What it means here:**
- **The inventory, first.** A committed script lists, by a static reading of `screen_d5.py` at the
  approval commit, every branch that ends in a `not_assessed` core test or an `unavailable`
  report-only record, by entry point (`f1_box`, `report_only`, `compute_screen`) and by record,
  with its reason string. It is committed before any probe.
- **The case record, before the first probe.** One committed file,
  `docs/validation/v0.5.12/step2/cases.json`, fixes for each case:
  - the curve's recipe (model, parameters, seed, σ) and whether it is a natural witness or a fault
    injection;
  - the expected state of every record (`pass`, `fail`, `not_assessed`, `computed`,
    `unavailable`), its reason, and F2's `would_be_significant` and `exception_first_condition`
    where applicable;
  - for each value asserted: its expectation, from the generating model's parameters or from a
    deterministic independent oracle frozen on the realized fixture, never from the project's code;
    its `atol` in the metric's units and its `rtol`.
- **The cases.** One synthetic curve for each case of step 1 of v0.5.11's charter (lines 263 to
  271):
  - a planet;
  - a binary with unequal depths at half the period;
  - a binary with a secondary away from phase 0.5;
  - an occultation that meets F2's exception;
  - a grazing binary, and a high-impact giant (F4, and F3 with a star record);

  - one `computed` witness with numeric assertions for each report-only record (F1_trap, F2, F3,

    `xfail(strict=True, raises=...)` in the suite; its correction is a `src/` change behind the

  - **Any other failure stops the work.**
- **Check:** the order in git (inventory, then case record and probe, then the probe's run, then

### Step 3. F-1, the cause of version 3's process-state dependence

**What was seen** (the v0.5.11 report, F-1): in CI run 45514526728 (`84010d0`), the bytes of
`verdict-v3.txt` of `tests/baseline_code_v0511.py` differed in the last digits of some floats,
`amplitude_snr` among them, between the pytest process (after thousands of tests) and a fresh
subprocess, on ubuntu-24.04 with Python 3.12 and windows-2025 with Python 3.13. `64f3331` computes
both sides in fresh subprocesses.

**Codex's blocking item 4, the sentences on F-1, as written:**

> For F-1, use a new synthetic fixture or a pre-logged intermediate diagnostic quantity absent from
> the old record. Freeze the source, environment, synthetic conditioning workload, and three
> fresh-process A/B pairs. Require the factor to toggle the same affected field signature,
> including `amplitude_snr` where applicable, and trace it to the first differing operation.
> Without that link, report "cause not found." Cap the step at 0.8 CPU-h. Stop before any
> correction that could change a version 1–4 output byte.

**What it means here:**
- **No recorded case is recomputed.** The diagnostic uses a new synthetic fixture (a scenario
  seed absent from every committed record, checked by a committed script before the first run),
  or an intermediate diagnostic quantity, logged before the run, that no record holds.
- **Frozen before the first run,** in a committed unit: the source commit, the environment, the
  synthetic conditioning workload (named test modules run in-process first), the candidate
  factors, and three fresh-process A/B pairs per factor. The factors are taken one at a time: the
  conditioning workload; the thread counts of the numerical libraries (`OMP_NUM_THREADS`,
  `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS`, `MKL_NUM_THREADS`); the import order; the memory alignment of the arrays
  summed; any global random state; the floating-point control state. A factor is added only by a
  logged entry before its run.
- **"Found"** means: the factor toggles the same affected field signature, `amplitude_snr` among
  the fields where applicable, in all three pairs, and the difference is traced to the first
  differing operation. Without that link, the record says "cause not found", with what was
  excluded.
- **The record:** `docs/validation/v0.5.12/step3/f1-cause.md`, with the factor, the evidence, the
  first differing operation and the lines of code involved.
- **The budget:** at most 0.8 CPU-h for this step.
- **No correction** that could change an output byte of versions 1 to 4: the work stops before it
  (section 2.2).

### Step 4. Only if M1 fails: a correction of F1, then the measurement M2

**Codex's blocking item 2, as written** (an estimator must not pass by inflating its uncertainty):

> Freeze a sensitivity control before M1 and run it only if M2 runs. Use the same seven families
> and seeds 200001–200300. Increase the odd box depth by `5 × sqrt(1/sum(w_odd) + 1/sum(w_even))`,
> with `w=1/σ²`. Each family must give at least 281 `fail` and zero `not_assessed` out of 300,
> equivalent to a 95% Wilson lower endpoint above 90%. Keep the original unequal-depth regression.

**The steps:**
1. **A dated specification correction,** `docs/plans/v0.5.12-spec-correction-2.md`:
   - the estimator of σ_odd and σ_even, sourced in the papers (K25 II.1 and IV.4, T18, SM03)
     through the committed excerpts, each with its URL, date and short literal excerpt;
   - the starting point is the v0.5.11 gate's reading (finding 7) that K25 gives σ_odd and σ_even
     only as the noise over the corresponding transits. That reading is checked against the
     committed excerpts before use; if they do not settle it, the work stops, since reading a paper
     again would be a download. The estimator's form is the correction's choice, marked
     **[choice]**;
   - the results known when writing it (M1).
2. **A fresh blind review of the rule change,** separately authorized by the maintainer with its
   purpose, number and cost, before adoption (Process diet 2; ROADMAP.md, "How phases work",
   item 6). It is stored unchanged.
3. **STOP before adoption.** The maintainer's approval is recorded against the correction's
   SHA-256.
4. **Tests before code.** After the adoption, tests are committed and pushed before any `src/`
   change, with strict expected-failure markers only on the tests that need the new estimator, and
   a record of their IDs and intended failure reasons. The existing regression tests pass, the
   unequal-depth binary test among them.
5. **The `src/` change.** **STOP before its first commit,** with Codex's review of the exact diff
   attached; the approval is bound to that diff. The change touches only version 5's screen. It
   removes the markers and adds none.
6. **The measurement M2,** after the `src/` commit, with the rule of step 1 unchanged, the same
   seven families and new seeds **s = 100001 to 103000**. M1's results are kept and reported beside
   M2's. **Every family must pass.**
7. **The sensitivity control,** frozen here and run only if M2 runs, as the quoted item states:
   the seven families, seeds 200001 to 200300, the odd box depth increased by
   5·√(1/Σw_odd + 1/Σw_even) with w = 1/σ² over each parity's in-transit cadences; each family
   must give at least 281 `fail` and 0 `not_assessed` of 300.
8. **If M2 or the control fails,** item 1 is not met, and the work stops. A second correction needs
   the maintainer's decision.

## 4. Data, exposure and reruns

**Codex's blocking item 4, the sentences on case identity, as written:**

> Freeze each measurement case by its realized input identity and generation recipe. M1 uses seeds
> 1–3000 and M2 uses 100001–103000. Both must use inputs distinct from recorded cases. A rerun is
> giving a recorded case another value of its recorded output, even in memory or in a new file.
> Frozen assertion tests and identity or integrity verification may record their own check
> results. Preserve `step1/f1-calibration.json`.

**What it means here:**
- **No target is read** (section 2.1). The phase works on synthetic data, the repository's
  fixtures and committed paper excerpts.
- **Case identity.** Each measurement case is identified by its realized inputs (the SHA-256 of
  its time, flux and `flux_err` arrays) and its recipe. M1's and M2's cases use the box model and
  the noise stream `default_rng([s, 222])`. v0.5.11's step 1 calibration used
  `make_lightcurve(PLANET, seed=s)` with its own stream, so the inputs differ even where the seed
  integers coincide.
- **Codex's round 2, item 4, as written:**

  > Before each of M1, M2 and the sensitivity control calls F1, freeze that batch's realized-input
  > hashes and generation recipe. Check them against prior records and earlier phase batches. Stop
  > on any match or unresolved identity.

  A committed script writes each batch's hashes and recipe to a committed file, and checks them
  before the batch's first F1 call. Where a prior record holds no input hashes (v0.5.11's
  `step1/f1-calibration.json`), its inputs are regenerated from its recorded recipe and hashed:
  inputs only, never an output, so this is not a rerun.
- **What is not a rerun:**
  - frozen assertion tests on synthetic fixtures, locally and in CI;
  - identity and integrity verification: `golden-local`, `claim-hashes`, `verify_locked_tags.py`,
    `refute verify` and `scripts/ci_evidence.py`;
  - M1, M2 and the sensitivity control: new cases, given a first value;
  - F-1's diagnostic on a new fixture or a pre-logged new quantity, reported as exploratory and
    post hoc beside the v0.5.11 record; it replaces nothing.
- **What is a rerun, and stops:** giving a recorded case another value of its recorded output,
  even in memory or in a new file. v0.5.11's `step1/f1-calibration.json` is preserved: it is not
  regenerated or replaced.
- **A repetition of a unit of this phase** with a recorded start or outcome needs prior approval in
  #218, a bug included. A unit started and not finished is recorded `abandoned`.

## 6. Resource caps and the time box

| Item | Ceiling | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| CPU, the whole local path | **3 CPU-h** | Estimates: M1 about 0.27 (Codex's extrapolation: 0.265 CPU-h per 21,000 calls); M2 and its control, if needed, about 0.3; F-1 at most 0.8; targeted test units about 0.8; golden and claim hashes, before and after, about 0.2 (Codex: 0.19 to 0.22); the demonstration, tag verification and commits about 0.15; the rest is margin. v0.5.11's whole path measured 3.015 CPU-h, of which tests 2.638 |
| Downloads | **0 GB** | zero acquired external data, papers, code, packages, environments or artifacts (step 0) |
| Peak disk | **2 GB** | the conservative bound of step 0, warm-environment growth included |

- **Codex's choice:** keep the three caps, conditional on demonstrating the amended meter before M1
  (step 0).
- **The caps are limits, never targets.** Each unit reserves its allowance before it starts. If the
  rest would not cover it, the unit does not start and the work stops for the maintainer. A unit is
  stopped before it can exceed its allowance, and the interruption is recorded. The runner never
  raises a cap.
- **Targeted local tests first.** The full matrix runs in CI; a full local suite is preflighted
  against the remaining allowance before it starts.
- **Compliance is exit criterion 6.** It is never written as met without its evidence.
- **Machine rules:** processors 0 to 7 stay free; at most 10 workers in business hours and 20
  outside them; BelowNormal; batches of at most 30 minutes; every execution resumable.
- **Time box: seven calendar days from approval** (ROADMAP.md, "How phases work", item 3).
  - **Checkpoints:** the demonstration, the "before" proofs and M1 by day 2; step 2's probe and
    F-1's record by day 4; step 4's stops, if any, by day 5. A missed checkpoint is reported in
    #218 the same day.
  - **On day 7,** unless the maintainer has recorded an extension with its reason and deadline,
    execution stops. Each criterion is recorded as met, failed or not run, and the phase waits for
    the maintainer's close decision.
- **Outside the meter, reported apart:** remote CI provisioning, and reviewer inference (the
  tokens of the Codex rounds, of a blind review and of the gate session). Their local processes are
  metered (step 0).
- **Before approval:** this charter's drafting and its Codex rounds are not v0.5.12 work. They are
  reported in the consensus record, with their tokens.

## 7. Reviews and the gate

- **Codex** (read-only, through `.cache/v04/tools/codex_affinity.ps1`, as a launcher unit):
  - the review of any `src/` diff, attached to its stop;
  - the review of a specification correction, if step 4 runs;
  - one review of all the phase's commits before the seal.

  Each is one round on one closed question; a later round on the same question resumes its thread.
  Each answer is stored unchanged with the model, the effort, the CLI version and the tokens by the
  real sum of the events.
- **A blind review of a rule change,** only if step 4 runs: a fresh session, separately authorized
  with its purpose, number and cost, before the correction's adoption, stored unchanged.
- **The gate.** The phase may change a rule in `src/`, so a fresh Codex session cannot be its gate
  (ROADMAP.md, "How phases work", item 6).
  - **The reviewer:** one new, read-only Claude session on a clean checkout of the seal's pushed
    SHA. It receives **only the gate-review protocol and the phase name**, with no focus points and
    no summary from the builder.
  - **Its launch:** by the maintainer's adviser, with the maintainer's approval, after its purpose,
    its number (one) and its cost are given. The estimate is of the order of 500 thousand tokens
    (v0.5.11's gate: 462,434).
  - **Its record:** the actual prompt bytes and the unchanged review, stored afterward in a commit
    after the reviewed SHA, with the model and thread ID.
  - **Its outcome:** it passes with `SURVIVED`, or with `WEAKENED` and every finding tracked as a
    GitHub issue. A `REFUTED` gate closes nothing. A review after fixes is a fresh session,
    approved separately.
  - **The tail.** The gate's preparation and the closure are metered and reconciled against the
    seal before the report's approval, with the terminal receipt in the journal.

## 8. Exit criteria (each checkable from the repository)

The phase has **six exit criteria**, sealed at a pushed SHA and a journal cursor (step 5). The gate
judges them at the seal. The gate itself is not an exit criterion it would have to judge: it is the
closure condition of ROADMAP.md ("Every phase ends with a gate"), recorded at closure (section 7).

**Codex's round 2, item 6, proposed this addition to the committed gate protocol**
(`.claude/commands/gate-review.md`):

> For a phase with a sealed review and a separate closure criterion, judge criteria 1–6 at the
> seal. Mark criterion 7 pending closure and exclude it from the gate verdict. Use committed
> evidence and authorized metered verification.

A change to the gate protocol is a rule change, so it is **not made here**: it is the maintainer's
decision, separate from this charter's approval. This charter avoids the self-reference instead,
with no change to the protocol: the gate is the closure condition, not an exit criterion.

1. **#222 is answered by the fixed rule.** M1 is recorded, with its script, unit and journal entry
   before its run. Either every family passes in M1, or: the correction passed its blind review and
   was adopted at its stop; its tests came before its code; every family passes in M2 on the new
   seeds; and the sensitivity control passes. The unequal-depth binary regression passes.
2. **#223 is covered.** The inventory and the case record precede the probe and the tests. Every
   case, every `computed` witness and every `not_assessed` and `unavailable` branch has its test,
   or a written proof of unreachability with an issue. Every specified test ID passes, with zero
   skips and zero xfails, counted from a JUnit XML file kept in git with its checker.
3. **F-1's cause is found and recorded,** as step 3 defines "found". No byte of versions 1 to 4
   changed.
4. **Versions 1 to 4 did not move.** The golden and claim-hash payloads are equal byte for byte
   before and after, provenance fields excluded; `verify_locked_tags.py` gives joint PASS.
5. **Full CI.** `scripts/ci_evidence.py <sha> --level full` holds on the commit named in step 5,
   with the run ID and job evidence committed. `uv.lock` and `pyproject.toml` are unchanged.
6. **The records are complete and the caps were measured and met.** The journal covers every local
   execution up to the seal, with no gap; every unit is in the ledger; the three ceilings held; no
   traffic is unclassified; every repetition has its approval.

**At closure** (not an exit criterion): the gate passed and is stored (section 7), and the tail is
reconciled against the seal.

## 10. Stops (work halts for the maintainer)

These add to every stop of Process diet 2, item 2, and remove none. Decisions ready at the same
time go in one issue comment, each with its own explicit approval; a stop is never merged with the
step after it unless the approval names the deterministic check that must pass in between.
1. The approval of this charter, which opens the phase.
2. **Before adopting any specification correction,** after its blind review (step 4).
3. **Before the first commit in `src/`,** with Codex's review of the exact diff attached; also any
   later `src/` commit that changes a rule or a threshold.
4. **Any correction that could change an output byte of versions 1 to 4** (F-1, or any other).
5. **Before the gate:** its authorization, with purpose, number and cost; likewise a blind review.
6. **Before approving the report.**
7. Any dependency change, first run of third-party code, download, target, pool or rerun; a
   failure of the meter's demonstration; a failure of step 2's baseline probe other than a
   documented assertion failure. None of these is planned.
8. A cap reached, a missed time box, work beyond this charter, a deletion outside `.cache/`.
9. Public text, the site, a release, Zenodo.

No lock, tag or drand round is created in this phase.
