## Respostas

**1. Conflito com regra que não pode mudar.** Não há revogação explícita. Há enfraquecimento por brecha em:
- **Ordem e fonte da verdade:** o charter fica só na issue (item 1) e vira o plano (item 5). Comentário de issue é editável e não entra na ordem verificável por git (CLAUDE.md:11, 121-123; ROADMAP.md:3, 22-24). Ver B4.
- **Checkpoints:** "batch decisions ready at the same time" pode juntar um checkpoint com o passo seguinte, contra "explicit stop and approval at each step" (CLAUDE.md:203-204). Ver B5.
- **Dado oculto:** "active holdouts" exclui, por leitura literal, holdouts de fases pausadas ou fechadas e nunca revelados (CLAUDE.md:65-74). Ver B3.
- **Regra de avanço:** o item 3 parafraseia o caso (c) e não diz quem fecha (ROADMAP.md:28-34). Ver B1.
- **Listas fechadas:** os itens 2 e 7 enumeram e omitem "results are never tuned" (ROADMAP.md:15-17), "no earlier verdict changes" (CLAUDE.md:39), as regras inegociáveis (CLAUDE.md:104-117) e paradas de CLAUDE.md:223-234 (código de terceiros, exclusão fora de `.cache`, force push, Administrador). Ver B7.
- **Sem conflito:** PASS com evidência, cobertura zero, vocabulário, segurança, privacidade, máquina, exclusão e dependências (CLAUDE.md:115-117, 126-137, 141-180).

**2. Abuso do item 4.** Sim, do jeito que está. A definição de rerun é tão larga ("even with different code...") que quase tudo é rerun, e a exceção "genuinely new diagnostics" não tem teste: quem quer fugir da parada chama de diagnóstico novo. A linha **não é operacional**. O texto já cobre bem o relato seletivo (falhas e tentativas abandonadas) e a confirmação em dado não usado. Faltam: o teste do diagnóstico novo (B2), o registro de exposição de alvos, e a citação das tentativas anteriores quando uma nova alegação sucede uma que falhou (N3).

**3. Portão e prazo.**
- **Item 6:** a independência fica no nível da sessão, igual ao portão atual (docs/gates/v0.5.8-review.md:4-5). Mas o Codex é o consultor de desenho de toda fase (CLAUDE.md:218-222) e a rodada seguinte retoma a mesma conversa (CLAUDE.md:52). "Uninvolved" e "when independence permits" não dizem como provar isso, nem o que acontece com um portão `REFUTED`. Ver B6.
- **Item 3:** sim, há risco. Listar alvos e colocar rótulo são atos que o agente faz sozinho. Todo fechamento por (c) até hoje foi decisão do mantenedor (ROADMAP.md:94-95, 951-953, 1098-1099). Sem essa frase, o prazo vira o caminho barato: deixar vencer, rotular, avançar. Ver B1.

**4. Menos paradas ou mais papel.**
- **Remove:** a aprovação separada da execução e do rerun, que passa ao charter (ROADMAP.md:66, 1284-1288; v0.5.9-plan.md:46); a autorização de revisão Claude no portão de fase exploratória (docs/gates/v0.5.8-review.md:4); idas e voltas, com decisões em um comentário; o arquivo de plano separado.
- **Cria:** uma entrada de log antes de cada execução exploratória (só é barato se o runner escrever); o log de errata para relatório de trabalho, que hoje se corrige com um commit; o controle do prazo e da extensão; a soma de CPU-h abaixo de 5, onde hoje não há ledger (CLAUDE.md:53); e, sem B2, aprovação até para recontagem e verificação.
- **Não toca:** o ROADMAP repete cada fase três vezes (ROADMAP.md:49-68, 70-90, 1155-1244). É a maior fonte de papel e continua. Também não desestimula microfases: foram quatro fases entre 21 e 23 de novembro, cada uma com charter e portão.
- **Saldo:** de cerca de cinco paradas por fase exploratória para duas (charter; relatório mais fechamento). Ganho real, modesto.

**5. Ambiguidades.** Ver B2, B3, B5 e N1 a N6.

## Texto final que eu adotaria

**Process diet 2 (#218).** Applies to phases opened after its adoption is recorded. Existing phase authorizations and historical records remain in force. Apart from what items 1, 5 and 6 state, it relaxes no rule of CLAUDE.md or ROADMAP.md; in doubt, the existing rule applies.

**1. Charter.** Before work starts, one charter file is committed and linked from the phase's ROADMAP section, and the maintainer approves it in the phase issue, citing its commit SHA. It fixes the question, target and data scope, resource caps (CPU-h, downloads, disk peak), exit criteria, reviews and stops. It may approve named reruns, each with its reason, exact procedure and the conditions that trigger it, written so that a record can check them. A charter adds stops and never removes one of item 2 or a safeguard of item 7. A change to it is a dated amendment, approved the same way before the work it covers, that states which results were known. Authorized work proceeds within that scope.

**2. Stops.** Keep all mandatory individual approvals, pre-registration checkpoints and phase-specific stops. The list that follows is not exhaustive. Stop before a lock or drand checkpoint, the approval of public text or of the phase report, a publication, a dependency change, or work beyond the charter. Where the charter calls for it, stop before the first commit in `src/`, with Codex's review of the diff attached. Decisions ready at the same time go in one issue comment, each with its own explicit approval. A pre-registration checkpoint is never merged with the step after it: that step's approval may share the comment only if it names the deterministic check that must pass in between, and a failed check returns the stop to the maintainer. Obtain any Claude review authorization with purpose, number and cost.

**3. Time box.** Execution stops seven calendar days after the charter's approval, unless the maintainer has recorded, before expiry, an extension with its reason and a new deadline (one at a time). A locked run that has started finishes as registered, and the time box shortens no right-of-reply period, dependency cooldown or registered wait. At the stop, record each criterion as met, failed or not run. Expiry closes nothing and advances nothing: the phase waits for the maintainer, who alone records a close under case (a), (b) or (c) of the 2026-11-21 amendment, as written in "How phases work". Expiry opens no automatic rescue round. Any later correction phase follows the advance rule.

**4. Exploration and reruns.** Within the charter and all applicable stops, explore on synthetic development data or on public data already exposed. A public target not yet exposed may be looked at only inside a pool the charter names; the choice and its reason are logged, and from then on it is exposed and unusable for confirmation. Without a higher cap in the charter, exploration stays within 5 CPU-h and 20 GB of downloads per phase, cumulatively; above 5 CPU-h the caps ledger applies. A rerun is any execution that gives a case a new value of an output it already has on record (a test status, class, score, door or criterion), on all or part of a recorded run's cases, including failed or incomplete ones, whatever the code, seed, threshold, input or entry mode. Every rerun needs issue approval of its reason and exact procedure before it runs, in the charter or later. A reviewer's read-only recount, `refute verify` and `refute check-dossier` are verification, not reruns. A new diagnostic may reuse exposed data without that approval only if (i) it yields a quantity no recorded run yielded for those cases, (ii) its question, quantity and cases are logged before it runs, and (iii) its result replaces or re-scores no recorded result and is reported beside it as exploratory and post hoc. In doubt, it is a rerun. The runner writes one append-only log per phase: before each run, the question and choices with a machine timestamp; after it, provenance, coverage and every outcome, including failures and abandoned attempts. The phase report links that log. Label outputs `exploratory` and carry applicable limits. Use findings only to generate hypotheses. Claims, acceptance and verdicts require locked, pre-registered confirmation on targets or data that are unexposed and did not inform the choices, or on prospective data; a claim that follows a failed or weakened one on the same question cites every earlier attempt in its pre-registration. No holdout, hidden window or reserved confirmation set is read before its own lock reveals it, whether its phase is open, paused or closed. Preserve the original results and stop decisions.

**5. Records.** The charter is the plan; keep one phase report. A working exploratory report (not approved, published, locked or hashed) is corrected in place by a commit, with a dated entry in one append-only errata log per phase: source commit, correction and impact. Approved, published, locked or hashed records, and any verdict-related correction, get a separate dated correction file, and the original is never edited. Track findings that affect a gate.

**6. Gate.** For a solely exploratory phase with no lock, scientific verdict, scientific acceptance, change of a rule or threshold in `src/`, or separately mandated review, one fresh, read-only Codex session may serve as the independent gate. Fresh means a new thread, never a resumed one, that has seen neither the design round nor any other thread of the phase. It receives only the dossier: a clean checkout of a pushed SHA, with the gate-review protocol and the phase name as its whole prompt, and no summary from the builder. Store its review unchanged with the model, thread id, prompt and SHA. A `WEAKENED` gate has every issue tracked; a `REFUTED` gate closes nothing, and a review after fixes is another fresh session, with every review stored. It also counts as the round's final Codex review only when it is that fresh session. Retain the required independent reviews for confirmatory work and infeasibility decisions. Rule changes receive a fresh blind review before adoption.

**7. Safeguards.** Preserve pre-registration order, locks, hidden data, rerun approval, deterministic verdicts and their vocabulary, evidence for every PASS, zero coverage as `INCONCLUSIVE`, "results are never tuned to pass a gate", "no earlier verdict changes", the advance rule with its 2026-11-21 amendment, the non-negotiable rules, and all safety, privacy, machine, deletion and dependency rules.

Placement: as proposed (items 1, 2, 4, 5 and 7 in CLAUDE.md; items 3 and 6 in ROADMAP.md, with the cross-reference), keeping the numbers 1 to 7 in both files.
