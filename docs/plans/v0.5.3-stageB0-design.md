# v0.5.3 stage B, B0: the design of D1's gate (EB1 and EB2)

**Approved by the maintainer on 2026-11-11** (#217, comment 6799266241). Stage B runs with caps of
150 CPU-h, 4.5 M review tokens and 8 agent-days. B0 comes first, with sub-caps of 30 CPU-h, 1.5 M
review tokens and 2 agent-days. B0's task, in the maintainer's words, is to "look for a new route
that separates EB1/EB2 from the planets, grazing PG planets included". The threshold is fixed and
committed before the measurement, on development data only. If no route reaches D1's target on
development data, B0 stops and brings the result, with the draft infeasibility record of D1 if the
bound shows a lack of information. D2, D4 and T2a do not start without a new approval, and D1's
target is not relaxed.

Written before any B0 measurement. Revised after Codex's round 1 (`WEAKENED`; the consensus record).
`method_status: not_validated`. Nothing in `src/` or in T2a's fingerprint changes. No confirmation
data is drawn or used.

## 1. The target

D1 for a family is the frozen rule `calib05.c1.decision_rules`. It has two conditions, on the
family's eligible set:

- **(i)** the Wilson 95 % upper end of the SURVIVED fraction is at most 0.10;
- **(ii)** the new battery's SURVIVED count is not above v0.4's.

At C1' size (about 194 eligible EB1 and 186 EB2, v0.5.1's projection), (i) allows at most 11 EB1
and 10 EB2 survivors. In v0.5, 35 of 190 EB1 and 40 of 186 EB2 survived. Of these, 33 EB1 had
been found at half their true period, and all 40 EB2 are folded by construction (v0.5.1 plan,
section 2, item 5). B0's bound concerns (i) only, a necessary condition: if (i) cannot be met, D1
fails whatever (ii) gives.

## 2. The routes: what information C1 holds

The literature's routes ([`sources/v0.5.3-stageB0-literature-2026-11-11.md`](sources/v0.5.3-stageB0-literature-2026-11-11.md))
fall into the channels below. For each one, the table records whether C1's frozen generator
(`calib05.c1.generate`) puts it into a binary's data and not into a planet's.

| Channel (literature) | In C1? | Existing code |
|---|---|---|
| Odd/even depths (Robovetter A.4.1.4; LEO-Vetter IV.4) | yes: a folded binary's two eclipses (EB1 ratio 0.2 to 1.0, EB2 0.5 to 0.9) | `odd_even` |
| Secondary eclipse at phase 0.5 (Robovetter MS Secondary; LEO-Vetter MS tests) | yes, for a binary found at its true period | the battery's phase-0.5 check |
| Duration, ingress and depth against the star's density (Seager and Mallen-Ornelas 2003; Morton 2012); the V shape (Robovetter A.4.3; LEO-Vetter IV.2) | yes: a folded binary's eclipse lasts as long as at its true period | `duration_excess` (version 3); stage A (d)'s geometric route |
| Candidate size: depth with the star's radius (Robovetter A.4.3 "deep"; LEO-Vetter IV.1) | partly; see below | none |
| Ellipsoidal variation, beaming, reflection (SWEET; Faigler and Mazeh 2011) | no: not generated (v0.5.1 feasibility README, (b)) | none |
| Centroid offset, halo or ghost pixels (Robovetter A.5; LEO-Vetter) | no: the binaries are on target, and their pixels come from the same `syn.make_aux(lc, scene=syn.Scene(), seed)` as a planet's, the light curve plus noise | `centroid` |
| Better stellar parameters (Gaia DR3) | no: C1's catalog star is the same for every family, radius and mass 1.0 with 5 % errors | none |

**The candidate size.** Binaries and planets get their depth from the same SNR rule (`solve_depth`),
but the distributions are not identical. EB2's rule uses the doubled period and divides by
`(1 + r) / 2` (`c1.py`, EB2 branch), so at equal SNR its eclipses are deeper by up to `2 / 1.5`. A
size route restricts the planets' depths, which is a different null from the oracle's (where the
planet's depth is free). The bound reports the binaries' depth quantiles, both the eclipse depth
and the folded mean, as information. Whether a size route could separate on C1 is not established
here. C1's planets draw their depths by the same SNR rule over the same noise levels, so a size cut that
removed binaries would face planets with overlapping depths. Equal SNR alone does not fix equal
depth.

**Consequence.** Every other channel C1 holds lies in the light curve, given the catalog star. A
test that compares the binary's light curve with the nearest planet's, under the full likelihood,
bounds every route that tests the planet hypothesis on the light curve, alone or combined:
Robovetter-style rule sets, likelihood-ratio tests and trained classifiers. That test is the WP1
and v0.5.1 oracle ([`d1_routes.py`](../validation/v0.5.1/feasibility/d1_routes.py), reviewed by
Codex in two rounds in v0.5.1). B0 measures this bound first, as a diagnostic, and builds a route
only if the bound leaves room (section 4).

## 3. The bound ([`d1_bound.py`](../validation/v0.5.3/stageB/d1_bound.py)), a diagnostic

- **Data.** 400 new development binaries per family: seeds `refute-v053-b0:<family>:<i>` for i
  from 0 to 399, never used before. They go through the frozen C1 generator and the frozen v0.4
  candidate generation. **Eligibility** is v0.5.1's forecast rule: the search SNR is at least the
  gate's `min_snr`. This approximates C1's eligibility, which is the v0.4 chain's gate after the
  candidate's finalization. Earlier counts were close: 388 of 400 EB1 and 372 of 400 EB2 in WP1,
  against 190 of 200 and 186 of 200 in v0.5's C1. The direction of the approximation's effect is
  not known.
- **The oracle**, imported unchanged:
  - It finds the planet at the found period nearest to the noiseless binary: free depth, impact
    parameter from 0 to 1.2 (grazing included), epoch shift, and the star's effective radius
    `R / M^(1/3)` within the 3-sigma bounds of the radius and mass errors.
  - It computes the Mahalanobis distance `d` under the generator's covariance (white noise plus
    each sector's AR(1)).
  - Both noiseless curves are multiplied by the star's known trend factor, as C1 applies it (Codex's
    round 1). The factor is redrawn from the generator's own first draws.
  - It runs with two stars: **`catalog`** (radius and mass errors of 5 % each) and **`star_known`**
    (no error).
- **Non-rejection.** At a planet false-failure level `alpha`, a binary's non-rejection probability
  is `Phi(z_alpha - d)`. For two Gaussian distributions with a common, known covariance, this is
  the smallest probability with which any level-`alpha` test of that planet fails to reject the
  binary (Neyman and Pearson). Any planet of the null gives such a lower bound, and the nearest one
  gives the strongest. "SURVIVED" stays the name of the aggregated verdict.
- **The level, `alpha = 0.0027`.** It is the project's existing convention for the false-failure
  rate a new test may impose on planets. The duration warning's acceptance (v0.5.1 plan, section
  3a) bounds its false warnings on 400 planets by Binomial(400, 0.0027), and stage A (d)'s
  threshold was the larger of the P and PG 99.73 % quantiles. The levels 0.05, 0.01 and 0.00135
  are reported as information. No other level is proposed.
- **The assumptions under which this is a bound**, stated as requirements, not shown:
  1. **The per-planet level.** A valid route must fail each planet of the null with probability at
     most `alpha`. Here the null is a planet at the found period around a star within its catalog
     errors. The project's checks already work this way: each one sets its FAIL threshold at a
     nominal level under the curve's own null (for example, `odd_even`'s 3 sigma on the curve's own
     errors). A route calibrated
     only on population quantiles of P and PG controls an average, and the bound does not apply to
     it. C1's planets all orbit the catalog's central star, but a test that used this would exploit
     the generator, not the data.
  2. **Selection.** Eligibility and the found period come from the same noisy curve as any test.
     The Gaussian formula is unconditional. Treating it as valid on the selected binaries assumes
     that the selection leaves the noise along the binary-to-planet difference (odd/even, duration,
     shape) unchanged. That is approximately so, because the selection acts on the box SNR, but it
     is not shown.
  3. **The star's null** is the whole ±3-sigma box. A test with a Gaussian prior on the star is a
     different problem. `star_known` is the most optimistic star, so section 4 bases its "no room"
     outcome on it, against a false infeasibility conclusion.
- **Directions.** Three approximations can only make `d` larger, and so the non-rejected binaries
  fewer, provided the fitted planet belongs to the null:
  - the noise covariance and the binary's exact shape are known;
  - the planet's period is fixed at the found period;
  - a fit that misses the minimum overstates `d`, and so does fitting in white weights before
    scoring under the covariance.

  The selection and the eligibility approximation have no known direction.
- **Per family**, the outputs are:
  - the attempted curves and the eligible and folded counts;
  - the C1' projection `n' = round(200 x eligible / attempted)`, with generation failures in the
    denominator, as in C1 (Codex's round 1);
  - D1's allowance at `n'`;
  - for each star and level: the expected non-rejected count at `n'`, and the Poisson-binomial
    probability of at most the allowance over 2,000 bootstrap cohorts of `n'`, with its median and
    95th percentile. This is a forecast over resampled cohorts, not a confidence interval;
  - the non-rejection among folded and unfolded binaries, and the depth quantiles.
- **What the bound does not cover.** D1 counts SURVIVED only. A binary held INCONCLUSIVE (or
  WEAKENED) does not survive under the frozen rule, so D1 could still be met through INCONCLUSIVE
  verdicts on binaries that no test separates. That is not separation, and so it is not a route.
  The report gives an illustrative quantity, the expected excess over the allowance as a fraction
  of `n'`. It is not a proven required share, and nothing about it is decided.
- **Disclosure.** Before this design, on 2026-11-11, the agent re-read v0.5.1's 760 recorded
  distances (`.cache/v051/d1routes/decompose_joint.jsonl`, other seeds) at several levels. At
  0.0027, the non-rejection fractions were 0.099 (EB1) and 0.19 (EB2) with the catalog star, and
  0.042 and 0.096 with the star known. The level was not chosen from these figures: it is the
  earlier convention. The measurement uses new seeds.

## 4. The decision rule (fixed before the run)

Each outcome is computed by `d1_bound.outcome`, at `alpha = 0.0027` and the projection `n'`.
Whatever the outcome, the bound remains a diagnostic under the assumptions of section 3.

- **(D) No conclusion.** Any record with status ERROR, a family with fewer than 150 eligible
  binaries, or a non-finite distance. B0 stops and reports the cause.
- **(A) The bound leaves no room for a family, even with the star known.** For EB1 or EB2,
  `star_known` gives an expected non-rejected count above the allowance, and the median cohort's
  probability of meeting the allowance below 0.05. Then, under section 3's assumptions, no route
  can be expected to meet D1 (i) by separation on development data. B0 stops and builds no route.
  It brings a **draft** infeasibility record of D1, which states those assumptions as the
  conditions of its claim and cites the bound as evidence under them, not as proof. D1 needs both
  families, so (A) for either one closes the gate.
- **(B) Room with the catalog star for both families**: the expected non-rejected count is at most
  the allowance. This allows a route to be investigated. It does not show that one can reach D1.
  B0 would continue with a combined likelihood-ratio route (geometry, odd/even and secondary), with
  its own design reviewed by Codex before any code runs. Its threshold would be fixed on
  development planets (P and PG, 99.73 % quantiles), and it would be measured on new development
  seeds.
- **(C) Otherwise.** The bound does not settle the question under the catalog star: room appears
  only with the star known, or the median pass probability is not below 0.05. B0 stops and brings
  the bound, with no infeasibility claim and no claim about what would solve it.

## 5. Caps and accounting

- **The CPU ledger.** [`ledger_b.py`](../validation/v0.5.3/stageB/ledger_b.py) gives stage B a
  ledger of 150 CPU-h, and B0's parts (`b0_*`) form a sub-line of 30 CPU-h:
  - `b0_bound` gets 4 CPU-h and `b0_reserve` 25.9;
  - `budgeted`'s line is 149.9, and 0.1 CPU-h is kept outside it for the charges, because its
    projection counts the charges on top of the whole line (Codex's round 1); a charge beyond that
    0.1 is a STOP;
  - B1's 120 stay unallocated until approved.

  A part's strict cap is what is left of B0 and of stage B, minus one batch's reserve, over 1.10,
  and at most 1.5 times its allocation. On a resumed part, its own consumption so far is subtracted
  from what is left and is also counted by the driver: a conservative double count.
- **Charges.** The smoke run of `bound_one`, on index 100000 of each family (outside the measured
  set), took 19.8 processor-seconds before this design and is charged as `b0_smoke_2026-11-11`.
  `summarize` checks the caps and the charges' room before it starts, and charges its own
  processor time even when it fails. A charge is always recorded, and one that takes the charges
  past their 0.1 CPU-h is a STOP. After a transfer, `declare` keeps the persisted allocations
  (Codex's round 2).
- **Cost estimate.** The smoke run took about 10 processor-seconds per binary, so 800 binaries need
  about 2.3 CPU-h, inside the 4 allocated.
- **Running.** The run goes through `scripts/launch_affinity.ps1`, with v0.5.2's driver unchanged:
  - 12 workers configured, at most 10 in business hours, pinned to the even processors 8 to 30;
  - BelowNormal priority;
  - batches of 30 minutes;
  - resumable from the stored shards.

  During a run only the CPU-h move, and the driver enforces them. No Codex call runs, and the
  run's wall time is reserved in agent-days by `check_caps`.
- **Review tokens** follow the maintainer's rule of 2026-11-11 (`caps.json`):
  - the count is each Codex conversation's uncached input plus output, from its last turn, since
    the CLI's figures are cumulative;
  - the caps are 1.5 M for B0 and 4.5 M for stage B;
  - a stop when 80 % of either is reached.
- **Agent-days** are counted since 2026-11-11T14:45Z, as wall clock over 8-hour days: 2 for B0
  and 8 for stage B.
- **The fingerprint** covers the code hash, the scripts, the imported oracle, v0.5.2's driver, the
  v0.4 claim and this design. The design and scripts are committed and pushed before the run.

## 6. The stop

After the bound, under (A), (C) or (D), B0 stops. The stop report,
`docs/validation/v0.5.3/stageB/README.md`, gives:

- the bound;
- the outcome;
- under (A), the draft infeasibility record;
- Codex's consumption and the other caps.

It goes through Codex's end-of-B0 review before it reaches the maintainer. Under (B), the route's
design goes to Codex before any of its code runs.
