## The common test (a new, fatal density test)

- **Inputs, per curve.** The candidate comes from v0.4's frozen pipeline (detrending, global
  prewhitening, search; `calib05.plans.pair` on v0.4's claim), as B0 builds it. The test reads
  the detrended flux near the events: the cadences within twice the found duration of
  `t0 + k·P/2`, every half period, so both the primary and a secondary at either alias are
  covered. The flux is turned into a deficit, `(level − f) / level`, with `level` the median out
  of transit. Each cadence's error is `per_point_sigma` out of transit, divided by the same
  level. Both families carry one free offset each.
- **The planet family.** `forecast.planet_model` at the found period: depth, impact parameter
  0 to 1.2, epoch shift ±0.1 d, and the star's effective radius within the catalog star's ±3σ
  box (`d1_routes.effective_bounds(0.05, 0.05)`, B0's convention).
  - It is fitted by weighted least squares from a grid of starts: impact 0, 0.3, 0.6 and 0.85;
    two radii in the box; the candidate's depth and 1.5 times it.
- **The binary family.** `forecast.binary_model` with its period at the found period or twice it
  (the alias):
  - the epoch shift ±0.1 d;
  - the primary depth;
  - the duration, between 0.5 and 2 times the found duration;
  - the ingress fraction, 0.01 to 0.5;
  - the secondary ratio, 0 to 1.

  It is fitted by weighted least squares from a fixed grid of starts (ratio 0, 0.5, 1; ingress
  0.1, 0.3). The better alias is kept.
- **The statistic.** `Λ = χ²_planet − χ²_binary`, white-weighted, a likelihood-ratio statistic
  under a white-noise approximation, which is stated. A large Λ flags a binary.
- **The three per-curve outcomes:**
  - **FAIL** (fatal, REFUTED) if Λ > τ;
  - **INCONCLUSIVE** if either fit fails (a least-squares error, no start converging, or a
    non-finite χ²), or fewer than 2 events have data;
  - **PASS** otherwise.

  No sensitivity block is modelled for the new test, so its INCONCLUSIVE comes only from failed
  fits and coverage. That understates `i_f`, which is not conservative for a route, and is
  disclosed. The strict budget's retention cost then rests mostly on `r_f`.

- **Eligibility** is B0's (v0.4's search SNR at least the gate's).

## The threshold and the bands (binomial, at a calibrated τ)

1. **Calibration.**
   - On the calibration planets, τ* is the smallest threshold at which every strict planet
     budget of step 2 holds at its point value: `r_P ≤ 0.025`, `r_P + i_P ≤ 0.05`,
     `r_f ≤ 0.05` for PG, PV and PA, `i_P(≥15) ≤ 0.05`.
   - The smallest such τ gives the most binary rejection, since rejection falls as τ rises.
   - τ* is chosen from the continuous range of the calibration Λ values, with no grid.
2. **Evaluation at τ*.**
   - Each limit gets its one-sided Clopper-Pearson end, with a Bonferroni split over the eight
     limits: P's REFUTED, retention and SNR ≥ 15 INCONCLUSIVE; PG, PV and PA; EB1 and EB2. Each
     is at 1 − 0.05/8.
   - Planets: the upper ends of `r_f`, of `r_P + i_P` and of `i_P(≥15)`.
   - Binaries: the upper end of each family's non-rejected fraction.
3. **D1 at the projection.** C1''s projection is B0's `n'` per family. D1's allowance is
   `k = forecast.max_count(n')`, the Wilson rule. The binary side holds if the binomial
   probability of at most k survivors among n', at the upper end of the non-rejected fraction,
   holds jointly for EB1 and EB2 with probability at least 0.95, by the conservative bound
   `1 − Σ (1 − p_f)` (the audit check's joint-probability condition).

## The outcome (step 2's)

- **Route** if, at τ*:
  - every planet upper end is within its strict budget (`r_P` ≤ 0.05, `r_P + i_P` ≤ 0.05,
    PG/PV/PA `r_f` ≤ 0.10, `i_P(≥15)` ≤ 0.05);
  - and both binary families meet D1 by this test alone.
- **`INCONCLUSIVE`** otherwise, or under any stop.
- **Reported apart:**
  - the warning class (Λ > τ* as WEAKENED);
  - the whole-allowance budgets;
  - the separation curves (the binary rejection against the planet rejection over τ).
- **Never a negative result or a draft** (step 2: the binary side assumes the other tests reject
  nothing).

## The run

- **The parts.** Two parts, each under `budgeted_v3`, launched through `launch_detached.py` (12
  workers, processors 8 to 31, BelowNormal), with no new dispatch after 2026-11-18 19:30:
  - `h2_step3_planets` holds the 1,200 planet curves, one per shard;
  - `h2_step3_binaries` holds the 754 binaries, one per shard.
  - One curve per shard gives the driver's checkpoint, a twentieth of the shards, many curves
    (60 and 38). Step 4 showed that a checkpoint of a few heterogeneous shards underestimates the
    rest.
- **The allocation** is set from a pilot of one curve of each kind, run pinned before the freeze
  with only its status and CPU printed (16.4 and 16.9 CPU-s), at 1.4 times the projection: 7.7
  CPU-h for the planets (1,200 × 16.4 s, about 5.5) and 5.0 for the binaries (754 × 16.9 s,
  about 3.5). The step is dispatched only
  if the projection fits the room left under the phase's 80 %; otherwise it is brought to the
  stop.
- **The caps** are checked for the phase only, with the rest of the plan's tokens reserved.
- **The summary** checks the charges' room, charges its own CPU under a unique name, and writes
  its record only after that charge.

**Stops before any record:** the caps; a population count other than the design's; a reader's
refusal; the driver's own stop.

**Stops in the record** (the outcome is then `INCONCLUSIVE (a stop)`, with no aggregate):
- more than 5 % of a family's curves raising an error;
- a family below C1's floor of eligible curves;
- a binary family not exactly B0's eligible count. (A binary not reproducing B0's ephemeris is
  no stop; it counts as not rejected.)

**Unknowns are counted conservatively.**
- A planet that raises an error counts as INCONCLUSIVE, a retention loss.
- A binary that raises an error, or does not reproduce B0's ephemeris, counts as not rejected,
  and stays in B0's frozen population of 381 and 373.

**The 80 % CPU barrier** is checked before each part: the phase's consumption, plus this part's
allocation, plus each other planned part's remaining allocation (planned, or allocated less
consumed), plus one interrupted batch's charge (12 workers x (300 s + 20 s), 1.07 CPU-h) and the
charges' room left, must stay within 40 CPU-h, or the run stops; this is read under the run's
lock. The part's hard cap is also bounded by that margin, over the driver's 10 % overshoot.
Each shard's limit is 300 s (one curve, about 17 CPU-s by the pilot).
