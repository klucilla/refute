## 1. The question

Can a battery with `discovery.version: 5` and a two-level output be **specified from the primary
papers and built in `src/`** so that:
- **level 1, the screen,** follows a frozen specification whose every equation and threshold
  has a recorded primary source, or is marked report-only; and
- **level 2, the verdict,** is version 2's verdict, byte for byte, on the same inputs, while
  versions 1 to 4 and every old lock stay as they are?

It answers with:
- a frozen specification;
- synthetic tests written before the code;
- the `src/` change;
- evidence of identity, lock compatibility by tag, full CI and an independent gate.

It measures nothing on real targets. It produces no verdict and no acceptance, and it creates no
lock. Version 5 is **not adopted** for confirmatory use here. Its adoption needs the blind review
of the rule change, which belongs to v0.5.12.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 The two levels

- **Level 1, `screen`** (a new field, never a verdict): `retained`, `rejected` or `unassessed`,
  by the draft specification's definitions and precedence
  ([docs/plans/battery-v5-spec.md](battery-v5-spec.md), lines 63 to 87). Step 0 freezes them.
  - "Not assessed" is never a PASS.
  - A `TestResult` with zero coverage stays INCONCLUSIVE (`src/refute/core/verdict.py`). In the
    screen it is `unassessed`, and it is counted.
  - The screen is a new, domain-agnostic function of `src/refute/core/verdict.py`.
- **Level 2, `verdict`:** `aggregate` and `aggregate_roles` are not edited. Version 5 runs the
  version-2 path with the version-2 roles.
- **Codex's blocking item 1 of the draft's review, as written** (verdict identity as an
  executable contract):

  > Level 2 executes the version-2 path with the same inputs, configuration, roles, stage, seeds
  > and error behavior. Compare canonical bytes of the legacy verdict and reason through every
  > supported entry point, and assert identical failure behavior. Keep version-5 guards,
  > target-keyed seeds and screen-only results in separate outputs. A4 requires every expected
  > decisive test to be assessed for `SURVIVED`, and every required screen-core test to be
  > assessed for `retained`. Exercise both invariants and the legacy error cases in synthetic
  > tests.

  - **Its consequences:**
    - a version-5 guard never intercepts the legacy centroid call;
    - `power.candidate_seed` is not changed;
    - screen-only results never enter the input of `aggregate_roles`.
  - **A4 (integrity) and A5 (identity)** are software invariants of this phase:
    - A4 is the invariant quoted above;
    - A5 is level 2's verdict and reason equal, as canonical bytes, to version 2's on the same
      inputs.

    Step 0 freezes both. The population targets A1 to A3 belong to v0.5.12.

### 2.2 The screen-only tests, reimplemented from the papers

The candidates are those of the draft's section 2.3:
- **F1:** odd and even;
- **F2:** a significant secondary, with the occultation exception;
- **F3:** size;
- **F4:** V shape;
- **U1:** model-shift uniqueness.

Their equations and thresholds are those of:
- Kunimoto et al. 2025 (K25), sections 3.9 and 4;
- Thompson et al. 2018 (T18), Appendix A;
- Seager and Mallén-Ornelas 2003 (SM03), Eq. 7.

The draft read them through a tool that summarizes pages, and Codex could not verify them
offline. **None is adopted until step 0 records its source.**

- **Codex's blocking item 4 of the draft's review, as written** (source verification as a
  condition of core membership):

  > Before coding, record the primary-paper version, equation, inequality and applicability for
  > every test. Keep a source manifest for the implementer and exclude GPL implementations.
  > Specify the phase grid and wraparound, masks, event separations, MES normalization, `F_red`,
  > `N_TCE`, error estimators, minimum coverage and invalid-input behavior. Freeze the exact
  > conjunction and occultation-exception logic. Use report-only status for unresolved rules and
  > for F4 in this phase. Synthetic fixtures verify implementation behavior. U1's empirical
  > thresholds come only from the frozen development calibration procedure.

  - **Codex's blocking item 1 of the round on this charter, as written:**

    > Step 0 freezes a nonempty screen core, its required coverage and its eligibility. A
    > report-only metric may be computed only from a frozen, traceable formula with declared
    > coverage and domain. An unresolved formula is recorded as unavailable. An unresolved
    > threshold has no effect on the screen. F4 and U1 remain report-only. If no core test
    > qualifies, stop before Step 1. Test that report-only outcomes cannot change the screen or
    > Level 2.

  - **What it means here:**
    - **Unchecked means no effect.** An equation that step 0 cannot check against the paper is
      recorded as unavailable and is not computed. A threshold that step 0 cannot check has no
      effect on the screen: it never gives a `rejected`.
    - **F4 is report-only** in this phase.
    - **U1 is report-only** in this phase. Its empirical thresholds come from a development
      calibration, which is v0.5.12's. Here U1 is computed only from a frozen, traceable
      formula, and has no threshold.
    - **An empty core stops the phase.** If no test qualifies for the core, the phase stops
      before step 1.
- **No GPL code.** LEO-Vetter's code is GPL-3.0 or later
  ([docs/sources/engines-licences-2026-11-24.md](../sources/engines-licences-2026-11-24.md)).
  So are the known limb-darkened transit models (batman, PyTransit).
  - None is copied, imported, linked, or read while the specification or the tests are written.
  - The source manifest lists every file and page the implementer read, with its URL or path
    and date.
  - The draft's transit-model form of F1 is left out: it needs a limb-darkened model.
- **Deterministic.** No random number enters F1 to F4 or U1. A later need is seeded by the
  target, never by fitted parameters.
- **Screen-only tests carry no sensitivity block.**
  - A screen-only test is assessed only:
    - with coverage above zero;
    - inside its declared domain;
    - with no guarded numerical failure.
  - A legacy test keeps the draft's rule: a sensitivity refusal is not assessed.
- **Pixel level.** The centroid keeps its role in the verdict. It is reported beside the screen
  and is not part of it.

### 2.3 Versions 1 to 4 and the old locks

- **Explicit version sets.** Today's `>=` comparisons would capture version 5 (`schema.py`,
  `params.py`). `pipeline.py` would write it as 2. `DiscoveryParams.version` is
  `Literal[1, 2, 3, 4]`. The change replaces each with an explicit set, and the synthetic tests
  prove each one.
- **Guards and seeds gated by version.** The centroid guards and any target-keyed seed apply
  only to version 5, on version 5's own path. `centroid_v5` serves versions 2 to 4 and is not
  edited unconditionally. `power.candidate_seed` stays as it is.
- **Four proofs:**
  - (i) **The golden snapshot.** A snapshot of versions 1 to 4 on the synthetic fixtures is
    generated at the parent commit and committed before the `src/` change. It must be equal,
    byte for byte, after the change.
  - (ii) **Claim hashes.** Every committed claim has the same resolved hash before and after.
  - (iii) **Tags.** `scripts/verify_locked_tags.py` gives joint PASS on every tag, each in its
    own environment.
  - (iv) **Full CI.** `scripts/ci_evidence.py <sha> --level full` holds on the final commit
    that changes `src/`.
- **`HISTORICAL_LOCKS`** (`src/refute/core/lock_compat.py`) is not extended.

## 3. Steps

Each step ends with a check that a record can run. A failed check returns the stop to the
maintainer.

**Step 0. Specification freeze.** The output is `docs/plans/v0.5.11-spec.md`; the draft
specification stays as history. Before any version-5 output exists, the step does five things.

1. **The primary sources, before any code** (Codex's item 4, section 2.2).
   - For every test, it records the paper version, equation, inequality and applicability, read
     from the paper itself.
   - It stores a `sources/` record with each URL, retrieval date and a short literal excerpt.
   - An unchecked formula is recorded as unavailable; an unchecked threshold has no effect on
     the screen.
   - It writes the source manifest.
2. **The core, the required coverage and eligibility.** The core must not be empty: if no test
   qualifies, the phase stops before step 1. Codex's blocking item 2 of the draft's review, the
   part that applies to step 0, as written:

   > Stage 0 freezes [...] the core, required coverage, eligibility [...] before any version-5
   > output. Recount joint assessability and retention for recorded tests by host and
   > population. Mark missing coverage and new-test assessability unknown.

   - **Left out, for v0.5.12:**
     - A1 to A3;
     - loss handling;
     - the calibration and futility rules;
     - the counts at 250 eligible cases;
     - the evaluation half.
   - **How the recount is done.**
     - It is done by a committed script.
     - It uses committed records only: the v0.5.6 part B records and the v0.5 C2 records.
     - It is a read-only recount (verification, not a rerun).
   - **The unknowns stay unknown.**
     - Where a record has no per-test coverage, the cell is `unknown`.
     - Every new test's assessability is `unknown`. Nothing is imputed.
   - **The warning.** Codex's recount found 24 of 100 part B planets decidable, and 82 retained,
     1 rejected and 17 unassessed when only the legacy odd/even, secondary and plausibility
     tests are replayed under the screen. This is a warning about carrying legacy refusals into
     the core, not a measure of the new core.
3. **The invariants A4 and A5,** as in section 2.1.
4. **Every rule of the screen.** Step 0 writes, before the code:
   - the domain bounds, `N_TCE`, the phase grid and the minimum events per parity;
   - every aggregation rule, from events to a test and from sectors to a target;
   - every invalid-value rule: NaN, infinite and out of range give `unassessed` with a reason,
     never a pass or a fail;
   - the availability rules and the tie rules;
   - the outcome vocabulary;
   - the `entry` field: `search` only, any other value refused.
5. **The appendix marked "not adopted"** (section 2.4).

- **Codex:** up to three rounds in one thread, since this is a rule change.
- **Check:**
  - the specification's commit and SHA-256 are in #218 before step 1;
  - the core is not empty;
  - `src/` is unchanged from the commit that approved this charter (`git diff --quiet <that
    commit> -- src/`).

**Step 1. Synthetic tests, written before the code.** Synthetic data only, no network.
- **The cases:**
  - planets;
  - binaries with unequal depths, with a secondary away from phase 0.5, and at half the period;
  - an occultation that meets the exception;
  - grazing binaries and high-impact giants (F4, report-only);
  - nulls;
  - every `unassessed` path;
  - every report-only path.
- **Sabotage.** These must be impossible:
  - a SURVIVED with an expected decisive test not assessed;
  - a `retained` with a required screen-core test not assessed.
- **Identity:**
  - the verdict with and without the screen;
  - level 2 against version 2 through every supported entry point, with identical failure
    behavior;
  - versions 1 to 4 against the golden snapshot.
- **Report-only outcomes** cannot change the screen or level 2.
- **The golden snapshot** is generated at the parent commit by a committed script and committed
  in this step.
- **Codex's blocking item 2 of the round on this charter, as written:**

  > Freeze the exact specification commit and SHA-256 in #218 before Step 1. On the pre-source
  > commit, record the expected test IDs and intended failure reasons. Apply strict
  > expected-failure markers only to tests that require the new implementation. Existing
  > regression checks must pass. After Step 2, verify marker removal in the diff and require
  > every specified ID to pass in JUnit. Require full CI on the final source-changing SHA and
  > verify that the gated SHA contains that exact source tree. Store each gate unchanged after
  > the reviewed SHA, with the model, thread ID, prompt and SHA. A normal gate passes with
  > `SURVIVED`, or `WEAKENED` with every finding tracked as a GitHub issue. A review after fixes
  > uses a fresh, separately approved session.

- **Order.**
  - The tests are committed and pushed before any `src/` change, together with a record of
    their expected test IDs and intended failure reasons.
  - Strict expected-failure markers go only on tests that need the new implementation. Existing
    regression checks, the identity tests against the golden snapshot included, must pass.
  - The `src/` commit removes the markers and adds none. The removal is checked in the diff.
- **Check:**
  - git order, and the push run's `created_at`;
  - `scripts/ci_evidence.py <sha> --level fast` on the test commit;
  - the record of expected test IDs is in the test commit.

**Step 2. The `src/` change.** **STOP before the first commit in `src/`,** with Codex's review of
the full diff attached (Process diet 2, item 2).
- **What the change contains:**
  - the explicit version sets;
  - one version-5 path for every caller;
  - `screen`;
  - the `_d5` test module;
  - the gated guard and seed.
- **What it does not contain:** a dependency change, a transform, or anything of section 2.4.
- **Check:**
  - no marker is left, checked in the diff;
  - every specified test ID passes, counted from a JUnit XML file;
  - the four proofs of section 2.3, with full CI on the final commit that changes `src/`.

**The gate** follows step 2 (section 7).

    apart.

  never written as met.

  - at most 10 workers and 2 downloads in business hours, 20 and 6 outside them;
  - BelowNormal priority;
  - batches of at most 30 minutes;
  - every execution resumable under rule (d).
- **Time box: seven calendar days from approval** (ROADMAP.md, "How phases work", item 3).

## 7. Reviews and the gate

- **Codex** (read-only, through `.cache/v04/tools/codex_affinity.ps1`):
  - up to three rounds at step 0, in one thread (a rule change);
  - the review of the full diff, attached to step 2's stop;
  - one review of all the phase's commits before the gate.

  Later rounds resume the same thread. Each answer is stored unchanged with the model, the
  effort, the CLI version and the tokens by the real sum of the events.
- **The gate.** The phase changes a rule in `src/`, so a fresh Codex session cannot be its gate
  (ROADMAP.md, "How phases work", item 6).
  - **The reviewer:** one independent, fresh, read-only Claude session on a clean checkout of a
    pushed SHA, given the gate-review protocol and the phase name only.
  - **Its approval:** the maintainer approves it first in #218, with its purpose, its number
    (one) and its cost. The estimate is of the order of 350 thousand tokens, the size of the
    v0.5.9 gate ([docs/gates/v0.5.9-review.md](../gates/v0.5.9-review.md)).
  - **Its record:** it is stored unchanged, in a commit after the reviewed SHA, with its model,
    thread ID, prompt and SHA.
  - **Its SHA:** the gated SHA contains exactly the source tree of the final commit that changed
    `src/`, which has full CI evidence.
  - **Its outcome:** it passes with `SURVIVED`, or with `WEAKENED` and every finding tracked as
    a GitHub issue. A `REFUTED` gate closes nothing. A review after fixes is a fresh session,
    approved separately.
- **The blind review of the rule change** comes before adoption, in v0.5.12. This phase adopts
  nothing.
