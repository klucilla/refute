## 2. What a verdict asserts (the frame for every argument)

The four discovery verdicts are built in a fixed order (`core/verdict.py`, `aggregate`): a gate
test that did not PASS gives INCONCLUSIVE; a fatal FAIL gives REFUTED; a missing expected test or
any INCONCLUSIVE gives INCONCLUSIVE; a warning FAIL gives WEAKENED; otherwise SURVIVED. Read as
assertions:

- **SURVIVED**: every expected attack ran on data that could have shown its failure mode, and none
  did. It is never "true".
- **WEAKENED**: every gate passed, no fatal attack succeeded and none was undecided, and a
  warning-class doubt was shown. **What counted as "shown" has changed between battery versions**:
  in versions 2 and 3 a flagged sinusoid alone was a warning FAIL (`battery_v5.py`,
  `battery_v51.py`), so WEAKENED then included "a sinusoid exists"; in version 4 a warning FAIL of
  `sinusoid` needs the sinusoid to explain the dip.
- **REFUTED**: a fatal attack succeeded.
- **INCONCLUSIVE**: Refute could not say which of the others holds.

A test's **severity** states the consequence of its failure. **The code distinguishes the
consequence of a failure from the inability to decide**; it does not establish a single ordering
in which an undecided test must weigh less than a failed one. Three principles frame the arguments:

1. **No PASS without evidence.** A test that examined nothing, or could not have detected its
   alternative, does not PASS (`enforce_coverage`; the sensitivity blocks).
2. **No statement stronger than its evidence.**
3. **Partial evidence is preserved in the dossier.** Whether unresolved warning attacks block the
   categorical verdict is a separate policy choice; INCONCLUSIVE does not erase the decided tests.

## 3. B1: does a warning test's INCONCLUSIVE block the verdict?

**The rule today.** Any INCONCLUSIVE gives INCONCLUSIVE (rule 3 of `aggregate`); a warning FAIL
gives WEAKENED (rule 4).

- **(a) Keep blocking.** No SURVIVED or WEAKENED verdict is issued while an expected decisive attack
  is undecided; gate and fatal-FAIL precedence remain unchanged. It respects principle 2.
  *Against:* a warning attack then weighs more on the categorical verdict when undecided than when
  decided against the claim; whether that is a defect is the policy question.
- **(a′) (added after the blind review) (a), plus an INCONCLUSIVE split for reporting only:** every
  INCONCLUSIVE verdict is labelled in the record, over **the claim's decisive set**, as
  **"warning-only gaps"** (every gate PASSes, every expected decisive test is present, no test that
  can emit FATAL is INCONCLUSIVE or failed, and the undecided tests are warning tests) or **"fatal
  or gate gaps"** (otherwise; **a missing expected test is a "fatal or gate gap"**). The label
  changes no verdict and no target's count; it delivers principle 3 in the record.
- **(b) A warning INCONCLUSIVE does not block; the undecided tests are disclosed.** It can yield
  SURVIVED while a warning attack did not run on data able to show its failure. **Rejected on
  principle 2.**
- **(c) It blocks only under a frozen coverage threshold.** Coverage is already required; a coverage
  threshold says nothing about power at the alternative.
- **(d) It blocks for some families and not others.** A real candidate has no family label.
  **Rejected on principle.**
- **(e) An undecided warning test caps the verdict at WEAKENED** (a registered variant only).
  Definition, corrected after the blind review: **every expected decisive test is present; every
  gate PASSes; every test that can emit FATAL (by its definition, whatever severity a given result
  emits) PASSes; no undecided test is deferred to a later stage or has zero coverage (those keep
  blocking)**; then an undecided warning test gives WEAKENED (never SURVIVED), with the undecided
  tests named in the reason. It lowers the evidential requirement for a retained verdict while
  preserving SURVIVED's. It **mixes three things under WEAKENED**: a test without power, a test
  that examined too little, and the ambiguous sinusoid (a doubt partly shown). **Before (e) could
  ever be adopted it needs a frozen limit on the spurious families' SURVIVED + WEAKENED: section
  7's paired rule applies to (e) with SURVIVED + WEAKENED as the outcome in every spurious family,
  at the same margin**, and an independent justification, which these principles do not supply.

**Proposed primary: (a′).** Reason: it keeps every verdict's meaning and delivers principle 3 by
reporting. (e) is registered beside it as a variant, reported only.

**The metrics audited under (e)** (section 8): the spurious families' SURVIVED and SURVIVED +
WEAKENED (D1's rule counts SURVIVED only), T2b, T2c, D2, D4 and T2a. The paired transitions of
B1 (e) and B3.3 are reported together, applying each rule's guards.

## 4. B2: the background sub-check's alternative

**The rule today.** `systematics` (a warning test) has a background sub-check that FAILs when the
in-transit background rise is both significant (a number of its errors) and at least a fraction of
the SAP depth (the FAIL fraction). Its sensitivity block requires a rise at the FAIL fraction plus
a declared increment (the alternative) to reach the rejection region with the declared probability;
**the depth and the rise are drawn independently** (`battery_v51_diff.py`, `background_block`),
unlike the aperture and SAP blocks, which use a measured correlation.

**What the sub-check measures.** A positive in-transit background rise relative to the measured SAP
dip. Its connection to a false-positive mechanism depends on a specified measurement model (the
sign, the scale against the aperture, a contaminant's coupling into the background estimate, and
the model's uncertainty).

- **(a) Keep the alternative.** It was declared before results. **The "no derivation" objection
  applies equally to all three increments** (aperture, SAP and background; `params.py` calls them
  conventions): none has a derivation on record. **R2 proposes no change to any of the three**;
  singling out the background's would need a reason specific to it, which is not argued here.
- **(b) An alternative derived from a contamination scenario.** **Ineligible for adoption until
  controls exist that couple a contaminating process to SAP and to the background estimate,
  including partial contamination.** If it is ever adopted, the block's draw must follow the
  contamination model (depth and rise correlated as the model says), not independent draws. Its
  position against the current alternative is not argued here. **A PASS certifies the declared
  sensitivity under the stated model, not the absence of background contamination.**
- **(c) A different statistic** (an interval on the background's share of the dip): a new test,
  with its own acceptance and threshold to argue.

**Proposed primary: (a)**; (b) is registered as ineligible until its controls exist.

## 5. B3: the sinusoid's small-residual requirement, and the ambiguous-cause branch

**The rule today.** `sinusoid` (a warning test) flags a significant sinusoid at P/2, P or 2P. With
`r` the box depth after the sinusoid model, `sigma_r` its error, D the candidate's depth and `f`,
`k` the frozen constants: a flagged sinusoid with `r + k·sigma_r < f·D`, valid coverage and FAIL
allowed is a **FAIL**; **a flagged sinusoid otherwise is INCONCLUSIVE** (`AMBIGUOUS_SINUSOID`; no
sensitivity blocks). The sensitivity block bounds the probability of "flag and small residual" by
Fréchet, `max(0, p + q − 1)`, only when coverage is valid and FAIL is allowed (otherwise zero).

**B3.1 Keep the small-residual requirement.** **Proposed primary: (a) keep.** Without it, the
alternative becomes "a sinusoid exists", which does not attack the claim ((c), dropping it, was the
frozen rule of versions 2 and 3; it is not proposed).

**B3.2 How the sensitivity's joint probability is computed.** **Proposed primary: Fréchet, at the
freeze.** A joint probability cannot exceed `min(p, q)`, so a tighter certificate could only change
cases where both marginals already reach the required probability. The fitted coefficients and `r`
are linear in the same flux, so under the white-noise model scaled by beta their covariance has a
closed form; beta, a scalar inflation, does not represent how red noise correlates them. **A future
joint model is a variant that needs its own blind review before any number of it.**

**B3.3 The ambiguous-cause branch: a proposed decision-rule change** (it turns some INCONCLUSIVEs
into PASS). The evidence split, with the conditions the blind review asked for, **acting only on
today's ambiguous branch**:

1. **What is preserved.** The flag, the FAIL (with the candidate's depth D, as today) and the
   frozen Fréchet blocks are unchanged. The new reading is evaluated **only where the current rule
   gives the ambiguous INCONCLUSIVE** (a flagged sinusoid without a small residual).
2. **D's provenance and uncertainty.** The branch uses its own depth, **`D_r`**: the same box
   contrast as `r`, on the same series, cadences, baseline and weights, **on the flux before the
   sinusoid model is subtracted**; and `sigma_D`, its error under the declared error model (the
   same white-noise model scaled by beta that gives `sigma_r`). A trend shared by both cannot
   inflate `r` against a depth measured elsewhere.
3. **The rule.** In the ambiguous branch, **shown large**,
   `r − k·sigma_r > f·(D_r + k·sigma_D)`: the sinusoid does not explain the dip, a PASS for this
   hypothesis. Otherwise the branch stays INCONCLUSIVE.
4. **Guards.** It requires `r` finite, `D_r` finite and positive, `sigma_r` and `sigma_D` finite
   and strictly positive, and a valid domain, coverage and detectability (the frozen sensitivity
   blocks); otherwise INCONCLUSIVE.
5. **A distinct record.** The new PASS is recorded as **"flagged and shown large"**, apart from "not
   flagged", with its `r`, `sigma_r`, `D_r` and `sigma_D`, so its transitions can be counted.
6. **A new adversarial family, VN**, frozen before any number (its design is in the targets
   document: shapes, period ranges, amplitudes, harmonic content, seeds, eligibility and
   comparator, the minimum number of ambiguous cases), counted under D1's rule and in section 7's
   paired rule; **the record states how many VN curves reach the ambiguous branch under the frozen
   rule, and below the frozen minimum of eligible ambiguous cases the VN safeguard is NOT
   DECIDABLE** (which prevents B3.3's adoption).

**Proposed primary: the evidence split, only if conditions 1 to 6 are accepted in the second blind
review; otherwise the current branch stays decisive and the split is a reported-only variant** (if
Codex's last round does not accept VN's definition, that conservative registration applies before
the bundle is built).
The symmetry with the FAIL side is a convention, **not a proof that variability outside the fitted
model is excluded**. An implementation note: the `q` marginal hard-codes `3.0` where the outcome
uses `ATTENUATION_SIGMAS` (`battery_v52.py`); a new version would use the constant in both.

## 7. The paired non-inferiority rule (for every change)

For **every departure of the new version from the frozen battery** (in the decisive combination:
B3.3 if adopted; and every reported variant), on **the same curves** under the old and the new
rules:

- **Where:** each spurious family of D1 (SE, V1, V2, EB1, EB2, ER, NW, NR), the new family VN, and
  T2b (the inverted and the rotated groups kept separate). **For T2b's paired safeguard, one rotation
  per star is pre-specified** (so that the pairs are independent, the star being the unit); T2b's
  frozen metric keeps all its rotations.
- **The outcome:** "retained" is SURVIVED for the D1 families and VN, and SURVIVED or WEAKENED for
  T2b; **for B1 (e), SURVIVED + WEAKENED in every spurious family as well**.
- **The paired population** is fixed before the draw: each pair is one curve under both rules;
  **a pair invalid under either rule** (not eligible, an ERROR) is excluded from both and counted;
  **if more than 5 % of the pairs are invalid, the reading is NOT DECIDABLE**. Each valid pair
  worsens (retained only under the new rule), improves (retained only under the old) or agrees.
- **The statistic and the bound:** `d = p_worse − p_better`; **the one-sided 95 % upper bound of `d`
  is obtained by inverting an exact unconditional trinomial test, with a pre-specified ordering
  and maximization over the discordance parameter**. The rule: that bound is at most the margin.
- **The margin, frozen now, before any number: `delta = 0.02`** for every family and for T2b.
- **Power:** each reading must have at least 80 % power at `d = 0` over the whole design range of
  discordance, **0 to 0.10** (the share of pairs that worsen or improve). A normal approximation
  (`n ≥ (1.645 + 0.842)² · 0.10 / 0.02²`) gives about **1,550 valid pairs per synthetic family:
  an initial estimate only**. **Before the freeze, the test's ordering and the smallest valid size
  that demonstrates at least 80 % power at `d = 0` for the whole design range are fixed, by an exact
  enumeration of the complete rule or a conservative analytic guarantee** (a design computation on
  no data, no number of the new version). No cut on the observed discordance is used; if one were
  added, it would be fixed separately and included in that computation. **For T2b**, whose size is
  fixed by the real stars, the same computation decides whether its paired reading has the power;
  **if it does not, the reading is NOT DECIDABLE**.
- **A NOT DECIDABLE or a bound above the margin prevents the change's adoption** (a STOP of the
  change, not a trade), on top of each target's frozen rule.
