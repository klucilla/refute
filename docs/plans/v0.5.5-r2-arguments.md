## 2. What a verdict asserts (the frame for every argument)

The four discovery verdicts are statements about a claim, built in a fixed order
(`core/verdict.py`, `aggregate`): a gate test that did not PASS gives INCONCLUSIVE; a fatal FAIL
gives REFUTED; a missing expected test or any INCONCLUSIVE gives INCONCLUSIVE; a warning FAIL gives
WEAKENED; otherwise SURVIVED. Read as assertions:

- **SURVIVED**: every expected attack ran on data that could have shown its failure mode, and none
  did. It is the strongest statement Refute makes about a candidate, and it is never "true".
- **WEAKENED**: every fatal attack was answered, and a warning-class doubt was **shown by
  evidence**.
- **REFUTED**: a fatal attack succeeded.
- **INCONCLUSIVE**: Refute could not say which of the others holds.

A test's **severity** states the **consequence** of its failure: a fatal failure means the claim
cannot stand; a warning failure means it stands with a doubt. Severity does not state how much
evidence a test needs; that is the sensitivity rule (a PASS needs demonstrated power at a declared
alternative; zero coverage is INCONCLUSIVE). Three principles follow, and every argument below is
judged against them:

1. **No PASS without evidence.** A test that examined nothing, or could not have detected its
   alternative, does not PASS (`enforce_coverage`; the sensitivity blocks).
2. **No statement stronger than its evidence.** A verdict may never assert more than the tests
   showed.
3. **No statement weaker than its evidence, without a reason.** A verdict that discards what was
   shown (for example, calling a claim undecidable when every fatal attack was answered and only a
   warning-class question is open) understates the evidence; that is a loss of information, not
   caution.

Principle 3 is the one a change can be argued from; principle 2 is the one a change must never
break.

## 3. B1: does a warning test's INCONCLUSIVE block the verdict?

**The rule today.** Any INCONCLUSIVE, warning or fatal, gives INCONCLUSIVE (rule 3 of
`aggregate`), while a warning FAIL gives only WEAKENED (rule 4). A fatal test's INCONCLUSIVE and a
gate's are **not** in question under any option below: they keep blocking.

**The options and their arguments:**

- **(a) Keep blocking.** *For:* principle 1 read at the verdict level: SURVIVED needs every check
  decided, and so does any verdict short of INCONCLUSIVE; a reader who does not open the dossier is
  never told "weakened" when nothing was shown. *Against:* principle 3: when every fatal attack was
  answered and the only open item is a warning-class question, "nothing can be said" discards the
  fatal attacks' evidence. It also inverts the severity order: a warning check has more effect on
  the verdict when it cannot decide than when it decides against the claim.
- **(b) A warning INCONCLUSIVE does not block; the undecided tests are disclosed.** *For:* the
  severity's consequence is respected. *Against:* principle 2: the verdict could read SURVIVED
  ("every expected attack ran on data that could have shown its failure mode") while a warning
  attack did not; the disclosure would contradict the verdict's own meaning. **Rejected on
  principle.**
- **(c) It blocks only when the test's coverage falls under a threshold frozen in advance.**
  *For:* separates "no data" from "data without power". *Against:* coverage is already required
  (zero coverage is INCONCLUSIVE); a coverage threshold says nothing about power at the
  alternative, so it would let an underpowered PASS-like reading through. It moves the question
  without answering it.
- **(d) It blocks for some families and not others.** *Against:* the verdict is about a claim and
  cannot depend on which family the claim came from (the families are a calibration device; a real
  candidate has no family label). **Rejected on principle.**
- **(e) (added here) An undecided warning test caps the verdict at WEAKENED**: if every gate and
  fatal test is decided and no fatal test FAILs, a warning INCONCLUSIVE gives WEAKENED (never
  SURVIVED), with the undecided tests named in the reason, beside any warning FAIL. *For:*
  principle 3 (the fatal attacks' evidence is kept); principle 2 (it can never produce SURVIVED,
  so it never asserts more than was shown); the severity order (an undecided warning check has at
  most the effect of a warning FAIL). *Against:* WEAKENED then means "a warning-class doubt was
  shown **or** could not be excluded": a wider meaning than today's, which a reader must be told;
  it changes the meaning of a word the historical records use.

**Proposed primary: (e)**, with the word's widened meaning written into the new version's verdict
documentation and every reason string (the undecided tests named). **Reason:** it is the only
option that keeps principle 2 untouched (no new SURVIVED can arise from it) while correcting the
violation of principle 3 and of the severity order. **(a) is reported beside it** as the current
rule (charter (d)4; amendment 1 (f): both aggregations for D2, D4 and T2a).

**What (e) can make worse** (the dependency table, section 8):
- **D1 (spurious families)**: D1 counts SURVIVED only; (e) cannot raise a SURVIVED count, so D1's
  frozen rule is untouched; the reported SURVIVED + WEAKENED count of the spurious families can
  rise, and that must be shown beside D1 (no target, but a visible cost).
- **D4 (decidability)** and **D2, T2a**: INCONCLUSIVE planets with only warning-class gaps become
  WEAKENED; that is the intended effect, and it is exactly what the adversarial checks must bound.
- **T2b**: if T2b counts detections that "survive" as SURVIVED or WEAKENED (to be confirmed from the
  frozen protocol before R3; **unconfirmed here**), (e) can raise T2b's count; T2b is then a direct
  adversarial check of (e).
- **D3 (REFUTED in grazing, variable and artifact hosts)**: unchanged (no REFUTED arises).

**How the spurious families test it:** in SE, V1, V2, ER and NR, every curve whose verdict moves
from INCONCLUSIVE to WEAKENED is a spurious signal (by construction) that the warning-class tests
could not exclude; their share among the families' WEAKENED is the measure of what (e) admits.
T2b measures the same on real noise. R3 reports both, under both aggregations.

## 4. B2: the background sub-check's alternative

**The rule today.** `systematics` (a warning test) has a background sub-check that FAILs when the
in-transit rise of the background is both significant (a number of its errors) and a large enough
fraction of the transit depth (the FAIL fraction). Its sensitivity block requires the rise at a
larger fraction (the FAIL fraction plus a declared increment: the alternative) to be detected with
the declared probability, by a simulation of both the depth and the rise with their errors
(`battery_v51_diff.py`, `background_block`).

**What the sub-check attacks.** The claim "the dip is the target's" against the alternative "the
dip is (mostly) background light": a contaminating source whose signal enters the background
estimate. A PASS should mean: had the dip been background, the sub-check would have seen it.

**The options and their arguments:**

- **(a) Keep the alternative.** *For:* it was declared before results; a larger alternative certifies
  sensitivity only against larger contamination. *Against:* the increment between the FAIL fraction
  and the alternative is a convention with no derivation on record; a requirement without a
  derivation is an accident of the rules, and an accident of the rules should be correctable when
  the correction is argued before its numbers and confirmed on new draws.
- **(b) An alternative derived from the contamination scenario**, written before any number of the
  new version: **the alternative is the background rise at which the whole dip would be background
  light** (the scenario that turns the candidate into a false positive), under the stated
  measurement model of how a contaminant's light enters the background estimate relative to the
  aperture. *For:* the PASS then certifies exactly the disposition-changing scenario; partial
  contamination below it changes the depth (a radius), not the candidate's existence, and stays
  covered by the FAIL threshold, which is unchanged. *Against:* **the measurement model is an
  assumption** (how the pipeline's background estimate scales with a contaminant's flux in the
  aperture; **unconfirmed here**, to be sourced before the freeze); if the model is wrong, the
  derived alternative is wrong. A derivation that lands above the current alternative also certifies
  less, which must be argued from the scenario, never from its effect.
- **(c) A different statistic with no fixed alternative** (for example, an interval on the
  background's share of the dip). *For:* reports what the data say instead of a binary at one
  point. *Against:* it is a new test, not a parameter; it needs its own acceptance, and its decision
  rule would still need a threshold argued the same way.

**Proposed primary: (b)**, **conditional** on the measurement model being sourced and accepted by
the blind review; if it is not, **(a)** stays the primary. **Reason:** an alternative should be the
point of the hypothesis space where a PASS changes a disposition; (b) is the only option with that
derivation, and (a) has none on record. The FAIL rule (significance and FAIL fraction) is not
touched by either.

**What (b) can make worse:** T2a and D2 gain only where the sub-check was INCONCLUSIVE for lack of
power; the cost is on **spurious signals that are partial background contamination**: V1 and V2
(variability can enter the background), NR (correlated noise), and T2b's transformed curves.
**D1 (EB1, EB2)**: a background eclipsing binary is the scenario (b) targets; D1's frozen rule
tests it on the EB families. **D3 (artifact hosts, PA)**: unchanged rule for REFUTED (a warning
test cannot refute).

**How the spurious families test it:** the background sub-check's PASS rate in V1, V2 and NR under
(b) against (a), on the same curves; T2b on real curves. A rise in those families' SURVIVED is a
direct cost; R3 reports it beside D1's frozen rule.

## 5. B3: the sinusoid's small-residual requirement, and the ambiguous-cause branch

**The rule today.** `sinusoid` (a warning test) flags a significant sinusoid at P/2, P or 2P. It
**FAILs** only when a flagged sinusoid also leaves a **small** residual dip (the box depth after the
sinusoid model, plus a number of its errors, below a fraction of the depth), with valid coverage.
Its sensitivity block bounds the probability of "flag and small residual" from below by the
Fréchet bound, max(0, p + q − 1), and that bound must reach the declared probability at the
declared alternative, or a PASS becomes INCONCLUSIVE. **A flagged sinusoid without a small residual
gives INCONCLUSIVE** ("it does not establish the dip's cause"; `battery_v52.py`,
`AMBIGUOUS_SINUSOID`).

**What the test attacks.** The claim "the dip is a transit" against "the dip is stellar
variability": a sinusoid that **exists and explains the dip**. Both conditions define the
alternative; neither alone does.

**Three separate questions**, kept apart so that a method change is never confused with a rule
change:

**B3.1 Keep the small-residual requirement?**
- **(a) Keep it.** *For:* without it, a genuine transit beside variability would be flagged as
  explained by the variability; the requirement is part of the alternative's definition, not a
  convention. *Against:* none on principle; the objections in the record are about how its
  probability is computed (B3.2) and what happens when it is not met (B3.3).
- **(c) Drop it.** *Against:* it changes the alternative into "a sinusoid exists", which does not
  attack the claim; **rejected on principle.**
- **Proposed primary: (a) keep.**

**B3.2 How the sensitivity's joint probability is computed** (a method question).
- **Fréchet (today).** The sharp lower bound for unknown dependence; it needs no model of the
  dependence. *Against:* the flag and the residual are computed from the same data under a stated
  Gaussian model, so their dependence is **not** unknown: the bound discards information the model
  gives.
- **A simulated joint probability** (the flag and the small residual drawn together under the
  model's covariance, with the simulation's uncertainty bounded by a declared band). *For:* it
  computes the same quantity the rule defines, instead of bounding it; it is a method improvement,
  not a relaxation, **provided** the simulated marginals agree with the battery's own computation
  of each marginal within a declared tolerance (a check that is part of the method) and the
  simulation's band is declared.
  *Against:* it rests on the Gaussian model's dependence being right; Fréchet does not.
- **Proposed primary: the simulated joint probability, with Fréchet reported beside it.** Reason:
  the rule asks for a probability, and the model the battery already uses determines it; a bound
  that ignores known structure answers a different question. If the review finds the model's
  dependence unsupported, Fréchet stays.

**B3.3 The ambiguous-cause branch** (an evidence question).
- **Today.** Flagged and not small: INCONCLUSIVE, whatever the data say about the residual.
- **The evidence split.** Three cases: the residual is **shown small**:
  FAIL (unchanged); the residual is **shown large** (the dip survives the sinusoid model by a
  declared margin, at a declared probability): the sinusoid does not explain the dip, so the
  alternative is excluded and the test PASSes for this hypothesis; **neither shown**: INCONCLUSIVE.
  *For:* principle 3: when the data show the dip survives the sinusoid model, "it does not
  establish the dip's cause" discards that evidence; an abstention needs a reason in the data.
  *Against:* "shown large" needs its own threshold and probability, argued like the FAIL side; a
  real planet on a variable star then passes this test where today it is undecided, which is the
  purpose, and also the risk (C7 below).
- **Proposed primary: the evidence split**, with the "shown large" threshold set symmetric to the
  FAIL side (the same multiple of the residual's error, around the same fraction of the depth),
  argued before any number of the new version.

**What B3's proposals can make worse:** V1 and V2 (variability) and T2b are the direct adversarial
checks: a spurious dip made by variability that leaves a large residual under the sinusoid model
would now PASS this test. **D3, PV (variable hosts, REFUTED)**: unchanged (a warning test cannot
refute). **D1**: the eclipsing-binary families are not attacked by `sinusoid`; no effect expected,
reported anyway.
