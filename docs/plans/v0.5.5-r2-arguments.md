## 2. What a verdict asserts (the frame for every argument)

The four discovery verdicts are built in a fixed order (`core/verdict.py`, `aggregate`): a gate
test that did not PASS gives INCONCLUSIVE; a fatal FAIL gives REFUTED; a missing expected test or
any INCONCLUSIVE gives INCONCLUSIVE; a warning FAIL gives WEAKENED; otherwise SURVIVED. Read as
assertions:

- **SURVIVED**: every expected attack ran on data that could have shown its failure mode, and none
  did. It is never "true".
- **WEAKENED**: every gate passed, no fatal attack succeeded and none was undecided, and a
  warning-class doubt was **shown by evidence**.
- **REFUTED**: a fatal attack succeeded.
- **INCONCLUSIVE**: Refute could not say which of the others holds.

A test's **severity** states the **consequence** of its failure. **The code distinguishes the
consequence of a failure from the inability to decide**; it does not establish a single ordering
in which an undecided test must weigh less than a failed one. Three principles frame the
arguments:

1. **No PASS without evidence.** A test that examined nothing, or could not have detected its
   alternative, does not PASS (`enforce_coverage`; the sensitivity blocks).
2. **No statement stronger than its evidence.** A verdict may never assert more than the tests
   showed.
3. **Partial evidence is preserved in the dossier.** Whether unresolved warning attacks block the
   categorical verdict is a separate policy choice; INCONCLUSIVE does not erase the decided tests.

Principle 2 is the one a change must never break. Principle 3 does not by itself require any
change: it makes the B1 question a policy choice that needs its own justification.

## 3. B1: does a warning test's INCONCLUSIVE block the verdict?

**The rule today.** Any INCONCLUSIVE, warning or fatal, gives INCONCLUSIVE (rule 3 of
`aggregate`), while a warning FAIL gives WEAKENED (rule 4). A gate's or a fatal test's
INCONCLUSIVE, and a missing expected test, are **not** in question under any option below.

**The options and their arguments:**

- **(a) Keep blocking.** *For:* principle 1 read at the verdict level: no SURVIVED or WEAKENED
  verdict is issued while an expected decisive attack is undecided; gate and fatal-FAIL precedence
  remain unchanged; WEAKENED keeps its narrow meaning
  (a doubt shown by evidence), which every historical record uses. It respects principle 2.
  *Against:* a warning attack then has more effect on the categorical verdict when it cannot
  decide than when it decides against the claim; whether that asymmetry is a defect is the policy
  question.
- **(b) A warning INCONCLUSIVE does not block; the undecided tests are disclosed.** *Against:*
  principle 2: the verdict could read SURVIVED while a warning attack did not run on data able to
  show its failure mode. **Rejected on principle.**
- **(c) It blocks only when the test's coverage falls under a threshold frozen in advance.**
  *Against:* coverage is already required; a coverage threshold says nothing about power at the
  alternative. It moves the question without answering it.
- **(d) It blocks for some families and not others.** *Against:* the verdict is about a claim and
  cannot depend on a calibration family label (a real candidate has none). **Rejected on
  principle.**
- **(e) (added here) An undecided warning test caps the verdict at WEAKENED.** Definition: **all
  expected decisive tests are present, every gate PASSes and every fatal test PASSes; missing
  expected tests stay blocking; the precedence of gates and of fatal FAILs is unchanged**; then a
  warning INCONCLUSIVE gives WEAKENED (never SURVIVED), with the undecided tests named in the
  reason, beside any warning FAIL. **What it is:** option (e) changes the aggregation and broadens
  WEAKENED to include unresolved warning attacks. **It lowers the evidential requirement for a
  retained and decidable verdict, while preserving the requirement for SURVIVED.** This trade-off
  requires an independent justification, which the principles above do not supply.

**Proposed primary: (a), the current rule. (e) is registered beside it as the variant** (charter
(d)4), reported side by side for every number of D2, D4 and T2a (amendment 1 (f)). **Reason:** (a)
and (e) both respect principle 2; (e) is a policy change that lowers the evidence a retained
verdict needs, and no independent justification for it is argued here. If one is argued before the
freeze (for example, from how verdicts are used downstream, written without reference to any
target), the primary can be reconsidered at the decision stop.

**What (e) can make worse** (section 8):
- **D1 (spurious families)**: D1 counts SURVIVED only; (e) cannot raise a SURVIVED count, so
  **D1's frozen rule does not limit (e)'s cost**; the spurious families' SURVIVED + WEAKENED can
  rise.
- **T2b** counts gated detections whose verdict is SURVIVED or WEAKENED, separately for inverted
  and rotated curves (`calib05/c2.py`); (e) can increase that count and **is directly tested by
  T2b's frozen comparison**.
- **D4 (decidability), D2, T2a**: INCONCLUSIVE planets with only warning-class gaps become
  WEAKENED.
- **D3 (REFUTED in grazing, variable and artifact hosts)**: unchanged (no REFUTED arises).

**How the adversarial checks measure it:** in SE, V1, V2, ER and NR, and in T2b, **the paired
INCONCLUSIVE-to-WEAKENED transitions over the fixed eligible denominator**, reported alongside the
SURVIVED and SURVIVED + WEAKENED rates under both aggregations.

## 4. B2: the background sub-check's alternative

**The rule today.** `systematics` (a warning test) has a background sub-check that FAILs when the
in-transit rise of the background is both significant (a number of its errors) and at least a
fraction of the SAP depth (the FAIL fraction). Its sensitivity block requires a rise at a larger
fraction (the FAIL fraction plus a declared increment: the alternative) to reach the rejection
region with the declared probability, by a simulation of both the depth and the rise with their
errors (`battery_v51_diff.py`, `background_block`).

**What the sub-check measures.** **A positive in-transit background rise relative to the measured
SAP dip. Its connection to a false-positive mechanism depends on a specified measurement model**
(the sign of the rise, its scale relative to the aperture, the coupling of a contaminant's light
into the background estimate, and that model's uncertainty).

**The options and their arguments:**

- **(a) Keep the alternative.** *For:* it was declared before results; every PASS so far was
  certified against it. *Against:* the increment between the FAIL fraction and the alternative is
  a convention with no derivation on record; a requirement without a derivation is an accident of
  the rules, and such an accident should be correctable when the correction is argued before its
  numbers and confirmed on new draws.
- **(b) An alternative derived from a contamination scenario**, written before any number of the
  new version: the rise at which, **under a specified measurement model**, the whole dip would be
  background light. *For:* the PASS would certify sensitivity to the disposition-changing
  scenario. *Against:* **the unchanged FAIL threshold still flags sufficiently strong observed
  effects, but it does not certify sensitivity to partial contamination below the proposed
  alternative**; raising the alternative eases the power certificate and **narrows the set of
  effects against which a PASS is certified**. Without a supplied derivation of the sign, scale,
  aperture coupling and model uncertainty, (b) has no basis.
- **(c) A different statistic with no fixed alternative** (an interval on the background's share
  of the dip). *Against:* a new test, with its own acceptance and its own threshold to argue.

**Proposed primary: (a). (b) is registered as a variant that exists only with its derivation**,
supplied and sourced before the freeze; **without that derivation, (a) remains the primary**. In
every case, **a PASS certifies the declared sensitivity under the stated model, not the absence of
background contamination.** Reason: (b) narrows what a PASS certifies, and its justification
depends on a measurement model not yet specified.

**What (b) can make worse:** spurious signals that are partial background contamination;
variability that enters the background (V1, V2); correlated noise (NR); T2b's transformed curves;
**D4** (decidability changes both ways); and, wherever a case moves to SURVIVED, **D1 and T2c**.
**C1's EB1 and EB2 test eclipsing-binary survival, not background-binary coupling; they do not
validate the proposed background measurement model.** D3: unchanged rule for REFUTED (a warning
test cannot refute).

**How the checks measure it:** the background sub-check's PASS rate in V1, V2 and NR under (b)
against (a), on the same curves, and T2b on real curves. **The frozen families remain mandatory
non-regression checks. Validation of B2 additionally requires controls that explicitly couple the
contaminating process to SAP and to the background estimate, including partial contamination**
(a future obligation; nothing is run now).

## 5. B3: the sinusoid's small-residual requirement, and the ambiguous-cause branch

**The rule today.** `sinusoid` (a warning test) flags a significant sinusoid at P/2, P or 2P. It
**FAILs** only when a flagged sinusoid also leaves a **small** residual dip, with valid coverage
and FAIL allowed: with `r` the box depth after the sinusoid model, `sigma_r` its error and `f`,
`k` the frozen constants, small means `r + k·sigma_r < f·D` (D the depth). Its sensitivity block
bounds the probability of "flag and small residual" from below by the Fréchet bound,
`max(0, p + q − 1)`, which must reach the declared probability at the declared alternative, or a
PASS becomes INCONCLUSIVE. **A flagged sinusoid without a small residual gives INCONCLUSIVE** ("it
does not establish the dip's cause"; `battery_v52.py`, `AMBIGUOUS_SINUSOID`), and that branch
carries no sensitivity blocks.

**What the test attacks.** "The dip is a transit" against "the dip is stellar variability": a
sinusoid that **exists and explains the dip**. Both conditions define the alternative.

**B3.1 Keep the small-residual requirement?**
- **(a) Keep it.** Without it, a genuine transit beside variability would be flagged as explained
  by the variability; the requirement is part of the alternative's definition.
- **(c) Drop it.** It changes the alternative into "a sinusoid exists", which does not attack the
  claim. **Rejected on principle.**
- **Proposed primary: (a) keep.** The separate questions are how the joint probability is computed
  (B3.2) and how an unresolved residual is classified (B3.3).

**B3.2 How the sensitivity's joint probability is computed** (a method question).
- **Fréchet (today):** the sharp lower bound for unknown dependence; it needs no model of the
  dependence.
- **A simulated joint probability:** the flag and the small residual drawn together. **A joint
  probability is determined only after specifying a common generative model for the fitted
  coefficients and the residual statistic. Agreement of the marginal probabilities is necessary
  but does not validate their dependence.** The proposal keeps the alternative, the phase handling
  and the rejection region unchanged; **the decision uses the lower limit of the simulation's
  declared band**, and a band that crosses the requirement counts as not met.
- **Proposed primary: the simulated joint probability is proposed only with a common generative
  model specified, supported by a derivation and accepted in the independent review; otherwise
  Fréchet remains primary**, and it is reported beside the joint probability in every case.
  Reason: Fréchet supplies a conservative certificate for the same joint probability; a supported
  dependence model may permit a tighter certificate, but an unsupported one is not knowledge.

**B3.3 The ambiguous-cause branch: a proposed decision-rule change.**
- **Today:** flagged and not small gives INCONCLUSIVE, whatever the data say about the residual.
- **The evidence split** (a rule change: it turns some INCONCLUSIVEs into PASS). With `r`,
  `sigma_r`, `f`, `k` and D as above: **shown small** means `r + k·sigma_r < f·D` (FAIL, unchanged);
  **shown large** means `r − k·sigma_r > f·D` (the sinusoid does not explain the dip: PASS for this
  hypothesis); **otherwise INCONCLUSIVE**. **Any new PASS keeps the applicable coverage, domain and
  sensitivity requirements and carries valid sensitivity blocks.** *For:* an abstention then needs
  a reason in the data. *Against:* the symmetry is a defensible convention, **not a proof that
  variability outside the fitted model is excluded**; a real planet on a variable star passes this
  test where today it is undecided, which is the purpose and also the risk.
- **Proposed primary: the evidence split, declared as a rule change**, with the current branch
  registered beside it.

**What B3's proposals can make worse:** spurious variability that leaves a large residual under
the sinusoid model, or whose joint probability now passes (B3.2: **additional spurious survivors
may be admitted when sensitivity-blocked cases become PASS**); V1, V2 and T2b are direct checks.
**D4** changes; wherever a case moves to SURVIVED, **D1 and T2c**. **The sinusoid test does not
inspect family labels; changes may affect eclipsing-binary verdicts (EB1, EB2) and must be
measured.** **D3's PV counts REFUTED** and so cannot by itself detect a warning INCONCLUSIVE-to-PASS
change: PV's transitions are reported separately.
