# v0.5.3: three design questions, argued both ways before stage A's numbers

**For the maintainer's decision; nothing is decided here.** The maintainer's decision of 2026-11-11
(#217, item 2): for each design question, the principled argument on both sides, **written before
looking at any new number of stage A** and validated by Codex. This draft must be committed after
its review and before stage A's first run, so that git shows the order. No argument below may rest on what a change would do to a
target. It is already known that D2 would pass, 98 kept against 97 needed, if every non-sinusoid
INCONCLUSIVE blocker were removed (`docs/validation/v0.5.2/closing-record.md`). That result is
therefore excluded as a reason for or against any change: a change needs a justification that would
stand with that number unknown. Any change adopted later is prospective, applies only to a new
battery version and is accepted on data outside development (section 4). `method_status:
not_validated`.

## 1. Should a warning-severity test's INCONCLUSIVE block the verdict?

**Today.** `core.verdict.aggregate` applies its rules in order: a gate test that did not PASS gives
INCONCLUSIVE; a fatal FAIL gives REFUTED; a missing expected test or **any INCONCLUSIVE** gives
INCONCLUSIVE; a warning FAIL gives WEAKENED; else SURVIVED. So a warning test that cannot decide
blocks the verdict, while the same test failing only weakens it.

**For keeping the rule (blocking):**
- *Evidence-only.* Refute's principle is that nothing is PASSed without evidence that the data were
  examined and could have shown the problem (zero coverage is INCONCLUSIVE; #185 extended it to
  power). SURVIVED asserts that every expected check found nothing; with a check undecided, that
  assertion is not earned, whatever the check's severity.
- *Severity grades consequence, not evidence.* "Warning" says what a failure means (a doubt,
  WEAKENED), not that the check is optional. Dropping undecided warnings would make the verdict
  silently mean "the checks that could run found nothing", a different and weaker claim.
- *Asymmetry is intended.* An undecided check is ignorance; a warning FAIL is evidence of a doubt,
  already recorded as WEAKENED. Ignorance should not be rewarded with a clean verdict, and keeping
  it visible is safer for a framework whose output is read by people who may not open the dossier.
- *Precedent.* The rules were fixed and locked before results (v0.1 onwards); changing them after a
  round failed its targets invites the suspicion of moving the goalposts.

**For changing the rule (a warning INCONCLUSIVE not blocking, or giving a distinct verdict):**
- *Monotonicity.* If verdicts govern an operational decision (which candidates go forward),
  incomplete warning evidence arguably should not restrict that decision more than an observed
  warning failure does: a check that can at most lower the verdict to WEAKENED has, under today's
  order, more effect when it cannot decide than when it decides against the candidate. The
  opposing point stands: `aggregate` deliberately distinguishes missing knowledge from adverse
  evidence (`core/verdict.py`, the rules in `aggregate`), and INCONCLUSIVE is not a stronger
  negative verdict than WEAKENED, only a different statement; the framework already separates
  decisive tests from diagnostics that never reach `aggregate` (`aggregate_roles`).
- *Proportionality.* The severity was chosen because a failure of that check would not justify
  REFUTED; by the same reasoning its absence of a decision should not cost more than its failure.
  The consistent alternative is that an undecided warning check yields at most WEAKENED (or a
  labelled "SURVIVED with undecided warnings"), never a blanket INCONCLUSIVE.
- *Transparency is kept without blocking.* The undecided check can stay listed in the verdict reason
  and the dossier, so no reader is misled; what changes is only which of the four words is used.
- *Literature practice.* Vetting pipelines distinguish disposition-changing flags from informational
  flags. The NASA Exoplanet Archive's page on the Kepler DR25 Robovetter's minor flags says that
  "some minor flags are informational only and do not necessarily indicate the KOI is a FP"
  (https://exoplanetarchive.ipac.caltech.edu/docs/koi_comment_flags.html, read 2026-11-11). That is
  a precedent for reporting a flag without letting it decide, not evidence that Refute's warning
  tests should work that way.

**What any change would need.** A rule change is a change to `core/verdict.py`, which every pack and
every historical claim uses: it applies only to a new battery version, with the historical locks
and claims reproducing their recorded verdicts unchanged, an acceptance of its own, and the final
evidence on data outside development.

## 2. The background sub-check's alternative

**Today.** `systematics`' background sub-check FAILs when the in-transit background rise is at least
3 of A0's errors and at least 0.5 of the SAP depth; its sensitivity block requires the rise at 0.8 of
the depth (0.5 plus an increment of 0.3) to be detectable with probability 0.95, by a simulation
that draws both the SAP depth and the background rise from independent Gaussians with their A0
errors (`battery_v51_diff.py`, `background_block`).

**For keeping the alternative:**
- *Physical meaning.* A background rise comparable to the transit depth is exactly the
  contamination scenario the sub-check exists to catch. Changing the alternative leaves the FAIL
  thresholds unchanged, but a larger alternative can certify sensitivity only to larger
  contamination, permitting a PASS without demonstrated power against smaller, relevant effects.
- *Fixed before results.* The increment and the probability were declared in v0.5 before any
  outcome, as for every test; the sensitivity rule's force comes from not adjusting them per round.
- *A declared policy.* Statistical sub-checks require power at registered alternatives, normally
  0.95 (`period_alias` uses 0.90, and categorical checks have no power requirement;
  `packs/tess/power.py`). That supports preserving a declared policy; it does not mean exceptions do
  not exist.

**For changing it:**
- *Power at the declared alternative.* At the declared alternative, the available data and the
  current estimator may lack power. That supports abstention unless the alternative's physical
  relevance is independently challenged; changing it requires a contamination-based rationale, not
  merely insufficient precision. (A noise-scaled alternative risks making adequacy
  self-fulfilling; any change should keep an independently justified physical effect.)
- *Role.* The sub-check is part of a warning test; requiring the same power as a fatal test may be
  disproportionate to its consequence (a failure only weakens).
- *The FAIL and power thresholds are separate choices.* The 0.3 increment between the FAIL
  threshold (0.5) and the alternative (0.8) is a convention; a principled derivation (from the
  contamination fraction that would change a disposition) is not on record.

**What any change would need.** A derivation of the alternative from the physics of contamination,
written before measuring its effect, then the same acceptance and confirmation path as any test.

## 3. The sinusoid's small-residual requirement

**Today.** The sinusoid test FAILs only when a sinusoid at P/2, P or 2P is flagged and the residual
box depth after the model is small (box after the model plus 3 of its errors below 0.5 of the
depth); a FAIL additionally requires valid coverage and `fail_allowed`. Its sensitivity block reports
the Fréchet lower bound max(0, p + small − 1) of the joint probability of the flag and the small
residual; it must reach 0.95 at the declared alternative, and low sensitivity turns a PASS into
INCONCLUSIVE, never into a FAIL. The residual's box averages its in-transit and out-of-transit
cadences with uniform weights, while the model behind it is fitted with inverse-variance weights
(`battery_v52.py`, `box_after_model`).

**For keeping it:**
- *Both conditions are needed.* A sinusoid is an alternative explanation only if it both exists and
  explains the dip. Without the residual condition, a genuine planet coexisting with variability
  that meets the harmonic flag thresholds could receive a warning FAIL, even though the variability
  does not explain the transit.
- *No independence assumption.* The Fréchet bound is the sharp universal lower bound given valid
  marginals and unknown dependence (the marginals themselves rest on modelling assumptions);
  replacing it by a product of the marginals would add an independence assumption that must itself
  be validated.
- *Abstention is legitimate.* When the residual cannot be measured precisely enough, declining to
  certify is the correct output, not a defect.
- *Declared before results*, as for every test.

**For changing it:**
- *The requirement may exceed what this estimator can show.* Under this estimator and residual
  model, insufficient residual signal-to-noise prevents a sensitivity-qualified PASS; it does not
  prove that every estimator fails, nor does it prevent an observed FAIL. Its INCONCLUSIVE may then
  measure the star's noise more than the sinusoid hypothesis.
- *The residual estimator.* The residual's box uses uniform weights; a requirement stated on one
  estimator rather than on the information in the data couples the rule to an implementation
  choice.
- *Joint versus marginal.* A joint simulation of the flag and the residual need not assume
  independence, and may provide a sharper bound than Fréchet's if the full procedure and the
  simulation's uncertainty are calibrated; then it is a method improvement, not a relaxation.
- *A caution for either side:* any alternative residual threshold requires false-warning control
  when real transits coexist with stellar variability.

**What any change would need.** A frozen, calibrated estimator or a measured joint probability, an
acceptance of its own and the final evidence outside development; the requirement itself (0.5 of
the depth, 3 errors, 0.95) changes only with a derivation written before measuring its effect.

## 4. The data outside development

The plan of v0.5.1 (`docs/plans/v0.5.1-plan.md`, section 5) defines the confirmation sets, never
used in development: **C1'** (C1's generator with seeds from a future drand round, fixed by a lock's
commit B), **C2'** (new real stars drawn by drand from a pool built by the v0.4 funnel's selection
rule, excluding every target of v0.1 to v0.5) and **C3'** (new real controls drawn by drand from
pools that exclude every earlier target). **None has been drawn**: v0.5.1 and v0.5.2 drew no drand
round and selected no real target (their closing records). The v0.5 calibration records (C1, C2,
C3) and every forecast of v0.5.2 are development data. **No confirmation sample exists yet: none was
drawn or used, and no confirmation light curve was read.** Pool metadata, classification notes and
reviewer annotations were examined for T3b's planning in v0.5.2
(`docs/validation/v0.5.2/stage2/t3b-status.md`); subsequent population amendments are therefore
prospective, not blinded. C2' keeps its exclusion of the v0.4 funnel's 100 stars and of every target
of v0.1 to v0.5, development targets and reserves included (`v0.5.1-plan.md`, section 5). Drawing
any of them is a non-delegated stop (protocol, drand, selection of real targets).
