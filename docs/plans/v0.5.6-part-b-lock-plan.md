# v0.5.6 part B: the lock plan

**For the maintainer's "pode travar"** (the decisions of 2026-11-22, 13:00, items 5 and 6, #218).
Nothing below runs before it. The design is [amendment 1](v0.5.6-plan-amendment-1.md); the claim
is `calibration/v0.5.6/claim.yaml`. All commands run from the project root, and every Python run
goes through the project's virtual environment, pinned to CPUs 8 to 31 at BelowNormal
(`.cache/v055/tools/pinrun.py`).

## What the lock covers

`refute lock` hashes three things:
- **the resolved claim:** the test plan with every default written out, and
  `test_plan.discovery.inputs`. These inputs are the SHA-256 of part A's frame, frame record, frame
  code, draw, records and runner; of `part-b-prep.json` and its code; of the plan; of amendment 1;
  and of the v0.5 claim.
- **the attachments:**
  - `pool-targets.yaml`, the pool: `planet` 288 and `fp` 606;
  - `part_b_claim.py`, `part_b_draw.py`, `part_b_run.py`, `part_b_run.diff` and
    `part_b_evaluate.py`.
- **the code (`refute-code-1`):** `src/`, `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock`, unchanged since part A.
  There is no dependency change and no change to `src/`: the two known defects of #59 are locked
  in as they are.

## Steps after "pode travar"

1. **Before the lock.** The tree must be clean, `HEAD` must equal `origin/main`, and CI must be
   green on `HEAD`. The part B tests must pass (`tests/v056/test_part_b.py`). They check, among
   other things, that the claim's input hashes are current and that the runner's battery chain
   is part A's.
2. **Lock:** `.venv/Scripts/refute.exe lock calibration/v0.5.6/claim.yaml`, then
   `.venv/Scripts/refute.exe verify calibration/v0.5.6/claim.yaml`, which must print `PASS`.
3. **The lock commit:** `claim.lock.json` and `LOCK_HISTORY.md` only. It is **pushed alone**, as
   the head of its own push, so it has its own CI push run. Its creation time fixes the round.
4. **The round:** `part_b_draw.py round <lock commit>` writes `part-b-round.json`, holding the
   first drand round published at least 10 minutes after the creation time of that CI push run.
   The record is committed. No push run, or more than one, stops the step for the maintainer.
5. **The draw**, once the round is published: `part_b_draw.py draw`.
   - It refuses unless the lock verifies.
   - It verifies the beacon's BLS signature, orders each stratum, and walks MAST metadata only
     until 150 TICs are eligible per stratum.
   - It checks disjointness from part A by TIC and by product.
   - It writes `part-b-draw.json`, which is committed and pushed **before any light curve is
     read**.
6. **The run, once:** `part_b_run.py run`.
   - It refuses unless the lock verifies, the draw names this lock, and the draw is `complete` or
     `short`.
   - Caps: 15 CPU-h, and a 20 GB disk peak (admission under 18 GB, a STOP over 20 GB).
   - Pixel files are deleted per target after the record and its manifest.
   - Workers and network: Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 18:00, at most 10 workers and 2 downloads;
     otherwise up to 20 and 6.
   - Batches of up to 28 minutes, resumable target by target.
   - The estimated download is about 100 GB. It is recorded per target and in total, as in
     part A.
7. **The verdict:** `part_b_evaluate.py` writes `part-b-verdict.json` and `.md`, as it comes, with
   the limits label. The records are bundled unchanged with their SHA-256, as in part A, and the
   result is posted on #218.

A STOP of any step, a failed check or a cap reached goes to the maintainer. A rerun needs the
maintainer's approval, recorded first on an issue with its reason and procedure.
