# v0.5.6 part B: the lock plan

**For the maintainer's "pode travar"** (the decisions of 2026-11-22, 13:00, items 5 and 6, #218).
Nothing below runs before it. The design is [amendment 1](v0.5.6-plan-amendment-1.md); the claim
is `calibration/v0.5.6/claim.yaml`. All commands run from the project root, and every Python run
goes through the project's virtual environment, pinned to CPUs 8 to 31 at BelowNormal
(`.cache/v055/tools/pinrun.py`).

## What the lock covers

`refute lock` hashes three things:
- **the resolved claim:** the test plan with every default written out, and
  `test_plan.discovery.inputs`. These inputs are the SHA-256 of part A's frame, frame record, frame
  code, draw, records and runner; of `part-b-prep.json` and `part-b-exposure.json` with their
  code; of the plan; of amendment 1; and of the v0.5 claim.
- **the attachments:**
  - `pool-targets.yaml`, the pool: `planet` 197 and `fp` 411;
  - `part_b_claim.py`, `part_b_draw.py`, `part_b_run.py`, `part_b_run.diff` and
    `part_b_evaluate.py`.
- **the code (`refute-code-1`):** `src/`, `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock`, unchanged since part A.
  There is no dependency change and no change to `src/`: the two known defects of #59 are locked
  in as they are.

## Steps after "pode travar"

1. **Before the lock.** The tree must be clean, `HEAD` must equal `origin/main`, and CI must be
   green on `HEAD`. The part B tests must pass (`tests/v056/test_part_b.py`). They check, among
   other things, that the claim's input hashes are current and that the runner's battery chain
   is part A's.
2. **The round, then the lock** (the decision of 2026-11-22, 14:30, item 2):
   - `part_b_claim.py --drand-round auto` writes into the claim's `definitions` the drand
     network, the chain hash, and the first round published at least 30 minutes later, with its
     time (genesis + period x (round - 1)). The test checks the claim is current, and the claim is
     committed.
   - `.venv/Scripts/refute.exe lock calibration/v0.5.6/claim.yaml`, then
     `.venv/Scripts/refute.exe verify calibration/v0.5.6/claim.yaml`, which must print `PASS`.
3. **The lock commit:** `claim.lock.json` and `LOCK_HISTORY.md` only, pushed at once with the claim
   commit, and as the head of the push, so it has its own CI push run. That run's creation time,
   read from GitHub, is the lock's time on GitHub.
4. **The check:** `part_b_draw.py check` reads the lock's time on GitHub and tells whether the
   claim's round is after it. If it is not, the lock is discarded without a draw, and step 2 is
   redone with a new number, as a recorded relock (`refute lock --force --reason`). No push run,
   or more than one, stops the step for the maintainer.
5. **The draw**, once the round is published: `part_b_draw.py draw`.
   - It refuses unless the lock verifies, and unless the claim's round is after the lock's
     time on GitHub, read again. It uses that round and no other.
   - It verifies the beacon's BLS signature, orders each stratum, and walks MAST metadata only
     (in a cache folder of this round) until 150 TICs are eligible per stratum. Every listing used
     is kept in the record.
   - It validates itself offline before writing: the replayed order, the recounted reasons, the
     strata, and disjointness from part A by TIC and by product.
   - It writes `part-b-draw.json`, which is committed and pushed **before any light curve is
     read**.
6. **The run, once:** `part_b_run.py run`.
   - It refuses unless the lock verifies, the draw validates offline (the BLS signature, the
     round derived from the CI run, the rebuilt entries) and is published (committed, equal to
     `HEAD`, on `origin/main`), and the draw is `complete` or `short`.
   - Every record and batch carries the SHA-256 of the lock and the draw. A resumed run refuses
     records of another lock, draw or target.
   - After the last batch drains, it writes the run's terminal state to the ledger (`complete`,
     or a stop of kind `cap_cpu`, `cap_disk`, `window` or `other`).
   - Caps: 15 CPU-h, and a 20 GB disk peak (admission under 18 GB, a STOP over 20 GB). A STOP
     lets the targets in flight finish, and the ledger records any overrun; the run then ends.
   - Pixel files are deleted per target after the record and its manifest.
   - Workers and network: Monday to Friday, 08:00 to 18:00, at most 10 workers and 2 downloads;
     otherwise up to 20 and 6.
   - Batches of up to 28 minutes, resumable target by target.
   - The estimated download is about 100 GB. It is recorded per target and in total, as in
     part A.
7. **The verdict:** `part_b_evaluate.py` writes `part-b-verdict.json` and `.md`, as it comes, with
   the limits label. It refuses unless the lock verifies, the draw validates and is published, and
   the ledger's terminal state is `complete`, `cap_cpu`, `cap_disk` or `window` (a stop of kind
   `other` goes to the maintainer). It checks every record against the claim, the lock and the
   draw, and every batch against the claim, the runner, the lock and the draw. The records are bundled unchanged with their SHA-256, as in part A, and the
   result is posted on #218.

A STOP of any step, a failed check or a cap reached goes to the maintainer. A rerun needs the
maintainer's approval, recorded first on an issue with its reason and procedure.
