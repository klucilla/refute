## Conventions

- **Seeds.** Development: `int(SHA-256("refute-v051-dev:<case>:<i>")[:16], 16)`. Acceptance
  (unseen while building): `int(SHA-256("refute-v051-accept:<case>:<i>")[:16], 16)`; the second
  set of the combined gates: `refute-v051-accept2`. A test that reads an acceptance seed before its
  WP's code is frozen is a deviation, recorded in #208.
- **Binomial bands** (as v0.5): "FAILs at least at rate p" over N trials means a FAIL count at or
  above the lower 2.5 % quantile of Binomial(N, p); "false-failure rate at most a" means a count at
  or below the upper 97.5 % quantile of Binomial(N, a). N = 200 per case unless stated.
- **Noise models** (as v0.5): white Gaussian; correlated, the AR(1) of `synthetic.ar1_series` with
  `red_noise_ppm` half the white level and `tau` one hour; and a stellar trend (a sinusoid of
  amplitude uniform in [0, 2000] ppm, period uniform in [3, 10] d, as the C1 generator), alone and
  with the correlated noise. Injections enter before detrending.
- **Claim schema.** Every new parameter and test exists only with `discovery.version: 3`, in a new
  `v051` section of the test plan (defaults = the values below). Claims of v0.1 to v0.5 give
  exactly their recorded outputs; the v0.5 battery (`discovery.version: 2`) is unchanged.
- A case passes when its "Must" holds. A **sabotage** check names a patched version of the code
  (applied inside the test, never committed) and the case it must make fail.

## A0: the empirical error of an event measurement (revision 2, corrected)

**Windows.** For a candidate of duration `T`, an event's window is its in-event span with its
baseline bands, of half-width `h = T / 2 + baseline_outer_durations x T` (2 T with the claims'
parameters). In each sector, **pseudo-event windows** are centred on a regular grid of spacing
`2 h`, so they are disjoint (no shared cadence); a pseudo-window is kept when it shares no cadence
with the window of a real event or of the phase-0.5 window (centre at phase 0.5, the same
half-width). Each kept pseudo-window is measured by the same estimator as a real event (the
per-event depth, with its coverage rule) on the same series. Its coverage `c` (in-event cadences
over a full event's) puts it in one **coverage stratum**: `c >= 0.9`; `0.7 <= c < 0.9`;
`min_coverage <= c < 0.7`.

**Per-event error, with no scaling.** A real event `i` of sector `s` takes the pseudo-windows of the
same sector and the same coverage stratum (`n` values). Their **ordinary sample variance**
`s_i^2` (the estimator for which the correction holds; no robust substitute) is corrected for the
dependence between neighbouring windows by the estimator `sigma_i^2 = s_i^2 (n - 1) / (n - n /
n_eff)`, with the **effective count** (finite-sample, positive-part autocorrelations)
`n_eff = n / (1 + 2 sum_{k=1..min(5, n - 1)} (1 - k / n) max(0, r_k))`, `r_k` the lag-`k`
autocorrelation of the
pseudo-window values in their time order within each segment of the sector (pooled over the
segments; lags that cross a gap are not used). The event's error is `max(model error, sigma_i)`.
With `n_eff < 10` in its sector and stratum, the event has no empirical error and is not measured
(counted, by reason); a test left with too few measured events is INCONCLUSIVE (class
`not_enough_data`), as before.

**Group statistics** (a group mean, the odd-even difference, the large-minus-core or
SAP-minus-expected difference over events): the weighted combination `sum_i w_i d_i` has the
variance `sum_ij w_i w_j sigma_i sigma_j rho_ij`, `rho_ii = 1`. For two events of the same segment
at grid lag `k = round(|t_i - t_j| / 2 h)` with `k <= 5`, of coverage strata `a` and `b`,
`rho_ij = r_{k,a,b}`, `a` the stratum of the earlier event and `b` of the later one; otherwise
`rho_ij = 0`. **The rule (amendment 3, Codex's proposal):** in each sector, every pseudo-window
value is standardized by the mean and standard deviation of its coverage stratum **over the whole
sector**; `r_{k,a,b}` is the pooled Pearson correlation of the pairs (an earlier window of stratum
`a`, the window `k` grid steps later, of stratum `b`) in every segment of the sector, the `m_{k,a,b}`
pairs of all segments taken together: the Pearson correlation of those paired values (the
paired-sample means subtracted, the centred cross-product sum divided by the square root of the
product of the two centred sums of squares). The pooled `r_k` of the shortcut is the same estimator
on the pairs at lag `k` of all strata together, and both are recomputed inside each bootstrap
replicate. It is
directional (`r_{k,a,b}` and `r_{k,b,a}` are estimated apart, never assumed equal) and its sign is
kept (no positive part). A pair `(k, a, b)` needed by an event pair is **estimable** with
`m_{k,a,b} >= 20`; a group statistic that needs a non-estimable `r_{k,a,b}`, or whose covariance
matrix `[sigma_i sigma_j rho_ij]` is not positive semidefinite (its smallest eigenvalue below
`-1e-12` of its largest), or whose variance is not positive, has no calibrated error, and its
decisive test is INCONCLUSIVE (class `insufficient_sensitivity`). **The verified shortcut (Claude's
proposal), an equivalence rule:** the strata of a sector are pooled, every `r_{k,a,b}` replaced by
the pooled `r_k` of all its standardized pseudo-windows, only when, for every lag `k` from 1 to 5 and
every directional pair of strata `(a, b)` with `m_{k,a,b} >= 20`, the 95 % interval of
`r_{k,a,b} - r_k` (both recomputed in each replicate) lies inside the **margin** `[-0.1, 0.1]`, and at
least one such term exists. The
intervals come from a block bootstrap over the sector's segments (200 replicates, segments drawn
with replacement, a seed derived from the test name and the candidate; the 2.5 and 97.5 %
percentiles), so that the shared windows and their dependence are carried into the uncertainty;
with fewer than 3 segments in the sector, the shortcut is not taken. Otherwise each pair uses its
own `r_{k,a,b}` (the rule). The margin, the bootstrap and the rule are fixed here before code;
whether the shortcut preserves calibrated errors is shown by A0.7, never assumed.
That zero beyond lag 5 (and across segments and sectors) is the independence assumption of A0; it
is not assumed blindly: row A0.6 must show it on synthetic data with known truth, correlated noise
included. **Where A0.6, or a pull row (A0.1, A0.2, A0.4, A0.5, A0.7) fails in a stratum, the errors of that
stratum are not accepted: every test result whose error relies on them is reported, but stays
diagnostic there, and a decisive test whose only usable error is such an error is INCONCLUSIVE
(class `insufficient_sensitivity`); never decisive on an uncalibrated error.** The code knows the
stratum of each measurement (noise model as declared by the acceptance run; at run time, the
coverage stratum and the sector count), and the acceptance records which strata passed.

**Strata of the calibration.** Noise model (white; correlated, AR(1) with `tau` one hour; correlated
with a long time scale, AR(1) with `tau` 0.5 day; a stellar trend; correlated with a trend) x
coverage stratum (the three above) x, for group statistics, the number of sectors (2, 3, 4): 5 x 3
strata for per-event rows, 5 x 3 x 3 for group rows. Every row below is required and reported **in
each stratum**, never only in aggregate.

**The unequal-event patterns of A0.5** (within each group stratum), fixed now: (p1) one group one
event short of the other (an event lost in a gap); (p2) few events, 2 against 3; (p3) one sector
holding half the events, at the minimal coverage stratum; each at event spacings P = 1, 3 and 10 d,
and with equal sector noise and with a trend in one sector.

**Why.** v0.5 row 1.10 (per-event pulls sd 1.348 in correlated noise) and the WP3 diagnosis (two
measurements of the same photons treated as independent) are both errors of an assumed model;
pseudo-event windows measure the noise of the actual estimator at the actual scale, and their
autocorrelation measures how far the windows, and so the events, depend on each other.

## WP3 (a): `aperture_depth` and `systematics` with the error of the difference

The rejection regions and the alternatives of v0.5 are kept. What changes is the error of the
difference, measured with A0 on the **difference series** itself:

- `aperture_depth`: the depth of the annulus (the large aperture's pixels minus the core's), and
  the large-minus-core depth difference, measured on the flux series of each; the difference's
  error is the A0 error of the large-minus-core depth difference measured at the pseudo-events.
  FAIL as v0.5 (difference at `max_sigma` with that error, and ratio at least `min_depth_ratio`).
  Sensitivity: the v0.5 simulation (alternative ratio `min_depth_ratio + 0.3`), with the core depth
  and the difference drawn from their measured errors and their measured correlation (from the
  pseudo-events).
- `systematics` (SAP): the series `SAP / median(SAP) - CROWDSAP x PDCSAP / median(PDCSAP)` per
  sector; its event depth is the SAP-minus-expected difference, its error the A0 error of that
  difference. FAIL as v0.5 (difference at `max_sap_sigma` and relative difference at least
  `max_sap_rel_diff`). **When SAP and PDCSAP are identical up to a constant factor in a sector**
  (no correction was applied there, as in synthetic data), there is no corrected flux to compare
  with, so the sub-check is **not applicable** in that sector (reported, never a PASS, never
  "detectable"); with no sector where it applies, the SAP sub-check is not applicable and the test
  rests on its other sub-checks (the registry makes SAP conditional; the dumps and background
  sub-checks stay required). A zero scatter over the pseudo-windows with a non-identical series is not
  zero error: the model error floor applies, and if both are zero or not estimable the sub-check
  is INCONCLUSIVE ("error not measurable").
- `systematics` (background): unchanged from v0.5 except its error, measured with A0 on the
  background series.

## WP3 (b): `sinusoid` per harmonic, with the star's other variability modelled (#203)

## WP3 (d): `odd_even` with an efficient estimator (plan amendment 1)

The forecast: at the Cramer-Rao error, `odd_even` against the full depth is detectable for 98.9 %
of eligible development planets; the diagnosis: v0.5's group errors are about 20 % above it (on a
white-noise planet at SNR 10.3, 57 and 63 ppm per group against about 50), and `odd_even` alone
refused a PASS on 15 of 75 development planets (median SNR 11.3). The rule and its alternative
(#185) are kept; the estimator changes:

- each group's depth is the weighted least-squares amplitude of the candidate's trapezoid (the
  shape fitted once on all events; the group amplitudes the only free parameters) against the
  flattened curve's baseline (the transit-masked detrending), instead of a box mean against local
  baseline bands; the errors are A0's, on the same estimator at the pseudo-events.

## The eclipsing-binary route: `duration_excess` (warning, #10)

*Pending, recorded on 2026-11-06 (not a revision): A0's revision 2 removes the shifted ephemerides
this section uses for `sigma_T`, and the sensitivity rule below can rarely be detectable with TIC
radius and mass errors of 5 % (it needs `sigma_T` at most about 1.9 % of `T_max`), so as a decisive
warning the test would leave most planets INCONCLUSIVE. Both go to the maintainer with a proposal
agreed with Codex (#208); no code of this section is written before that decision.*

- **Measurement.** The total duration `T` of the candidate's trapezoid (the fit of WP3 (d)), and
  its error `sigma_T` from 20 injection-recoveries of the same trapezoid (the measured parameters,
  the same estimator) on A0's shifted ephemerides; at least 10 valid recoveries, else INCONCLUSIVE
  (`not_enough_data`).
- **Bound.** The circular-orbit maximum for a central transit (b = 0) at the found period,
  `T_max(P) = (P / pi) asin(min(1, (1 + k) / (a / R*)))`, `a / R*` from the TIC mass and radius by
  Kepler's third law, `k` the square root of the fitted depth. Its uncertainty from 2000 seeded
  draws of the radius, the mass (TIC errors, independent Gaussians truncated at positive values)
  and `k` (from the depth's A0 error); `sigma_Tmax` is their standard deviation. Missing radius,
  mass or their errors: INCONCLUSIVE (`missing_input`).
- **Decision.** Warning when `T - T_max(P) >= 3 sqrt(sigma_T^2 + sigma_Tmax^2)`: "duration X h is Y
  sigma above the circular-orbit maximum for the TIC star; an eccentric orbit, a wrong stellar
  radius or a binary at twice the period can do this". Never fatal; the report says a binary it
  flags is WEAKENED, not refuted.
- **Sensitivity rule.** Alternative `T_alt = T_max(2P)` (the same expression at twice the found
  period, with the same draws); detectable when `(T_alt - T_max(P)) / sqrt(sigma_T^2 +
  sigma_Tmax^2) >= 3 + 1.645` (the Gaussian closed form with the full uncertainty above).

| # | Case | Must |
|---|---|---|
| D.1 | pulls of `T` on 400 circular planets per noise model | A0's bands |
| D.2 | 400 circular planets, families P and PG of the C1 generator, acceptance seeds | false-warning count within the upper 97.5 % quantile of Binomial(400, 0.0027) |
| D.3 | binaries folded at half their period (C1 EB1 geometry, impact parameter 0 to 0.8), depth ratios 1.0, 0.9 and 0.7, target SNR 10, 20, 30, 200 each | per case, among the binaries whose `duration_excess` rule is detectable (the full uncertainty above): the warning at least at the lower 2.5 % quantile of Binomial(n, 0.95); the test expected to catch each case reported (`odd_even` for 0.7, `duration_excess` for 1.0 and 0.9) |
| D.4 | a star without a mass error; without a radius | INCONCLUSIVE, class "missing input" |
| D.5 | 1.8 (a), (b), (c) of v0.5 with the rule above | (a) warning at least 0.95; (b) never PASS; (c) false-warning at most 0.0027 |

**Sabotage:** without `sigma_Tmax`, D.2 fails; with the model duration error of the v0.4 trapezoid
(not accepted: coverage 0.54), D.1 fails.
