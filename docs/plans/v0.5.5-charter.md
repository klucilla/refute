## A statement that applies to every decision below

**Every decision of this charter was taken with the results of v0.5 to v0.5.4 known.** That
includes v0.5's confirmation counts, round 3's forecasts (D2 keeps 141 of 178; T2a's `K_B` is
331 of 559), B0's and B0b's bounds, and step 4's numbers at every level. None of them can be
"blind". This is why each decision below is argued **from a principle that does not depend on
any of those numbers**, and why none of the values was chosen by what a target needs. A reader
should check exactly that: whether any value below would have been different had the results been
different.

## What a charter decision is, and what it is not

- It fixes **conventions** that the records never fixed and that a check needs before it can
  return a number: the price on the planet side, the use of the catalog star, the standard of
  proof for an infeasibility record, whether the prospective route may open, and how work is run.
- It changes **no target, no frozen rule and no verdict**: D1 to D5, T2a, T3a and T3b keep their
  frozen rules (`src/refute/packs/tess/calib05/c1.py:633-702`; the v0.5 acceptance document). v0.5
  stays `WEAKENED`.
- It decides **no design question** (whether a warning test's INCONCLUSIVE blocks, the background
  alternative, the sinusoid's small-residual requirement). Those belong to R2, after the gate G1,
  and are a stop that is not delegated.

## (a) The planet-side price, per exit

A test that denies SURVIVED to a binary also denies it, at some rate, to true planets. A binary
leaves SURVIVED by one of three exits, and each exit has a different cost for the planets
(`src/refute/core/verdict.py:260-276`; `c1.py:641-690`). The price is fixed **per exit**:

| Exit | The price on the planet side | Where the unit comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **REFUTED** (a fatal FAIL) | The frozen budgets, unchanged, **for the whole battery**: P ≤ 0.05; PG, PV and PA ≤ 0.10 (`c1.py:664-669, 682`). A new test uses **only the room measured on the development data**: the budget less the battery's own REFUTED fraction there. | The frozen rules D2 and D3 |
| **INCONCLUSIVE** | At most **0.05 more per new test** on P at target SNR ≥ 15 (the criterion of v0.5.4's step 2, [`v0.5.4-h2-step2-i3-increment.md`](v0.5.4-h2-step2-i3-increment.md)), **and** D2 and D4 held in full | Step 2's increment; the frozen rules D2 and D4 |
| **WEAKENED** (a warning FAIL) | At most **0.10 of the true planets of each family** demoted by the new test | The tolerance unit the project already used: D2's margin (kept ≥ v0.4's − 0.10) and the REFUTED limit of PG, PV and PA (0.10) |

**The frozen Wilson upper limits also remain mandatory:** 0.10 for P and 0.20 for PG, PV and PA
(`c1.py:664-669, 682`). Subtracting observed fractions does not establish compliance with the
joint battery rules.

**The argument of principle.**
- **REFUTED** is already priced by frozen rules. Taking the budget as given, and leaving a new
  test only the room the battery has not used, is the only reading that changes no rule. The
  room is measured, on the battery and the population the new test would join, never assumed.
- **INCONCLUSIVE** costs retention and decidability, which D2 and D4 already guard. The per-test
  increment of 0.05 is the one the project fixed for a new test before this charter (step 2), so
  it is reused, not chosen.
- **WEAKENED** has no frozen cap: D1 counts SURVIVED only, and D2 counts WEAKENED as kept
  (`c1.py:641-656`). Without a price, a battery that weakens every curve would meet D1, D2, D3 and
  D4 as written, and "SURVIVED" would lose its meaning. 0.10 is the unit the project already uses
  for "a tolerable loss of the planets' standing" in two frozen rules. It is not derived from D1.
- **Never chosen by what D1 needs.** None of the three values comes from D1's records: the
  REFUTED budgets and step 2's increment predate this charter, and 0.10 is D2's and D3's unit. If
  a check shows D1 needs a higher price, the price does not move: that is a finding, reported as
  such.

**What this fixes for the checks:** D1-P (R1) reads its curve at these prices. The INCONCLUSIVE
exit has no room while D2 fails its rule (round 3's forecast keeps 141 of 178 against 156); D1-P
reports it as **conditional on D2 and D4 being met in full**, never as available room. The
REFUTED exit's room is not measured by D1-P (it draws no planets); D1-P reports it as scenarios
only.

## (b) The catalog star

**The decision.**
- **A test uses the catalog star with its declared uncertainty**, as a Gaussian prior on the
  radius and the mass (5 % each in C1, `synthetic.make_star`), never as an exact value.
- **For new draws, a new version of the generator draws the true star within that uncertainty**,
  instead of placing it at the catalog's centre:
  [`docs/validation/v0.5.5/generator_v2.py`](../validation/v0.5.5/generator_v2.py) (version
  `c1-v2`).
  - R and M are drawn from N(1, 0.05), the declared errors, **with no artificial truncation**
    (only the physical domain is kept), from a stream of their own. Every other draw of C1 is
    unchanged for the same seed.
  - **Independent R and M errors are an additional modelling convention**: the catalog declares
    two errors and no correlation. The effective radius `R / M^(1/3)`, which is what the
    durations depend on, is not exactly Gaussian under this law; its 0.0527 is a first-order
    approximation.
  - The durations use that star, and so does everything C1 derives from them (the depth fixed
    for the target SNR, the flat time, the ingress fraction); the catalog star the data carries
    stays 1.0 ± 0.05.
  - `src/` does not change. **Old records and old draws do not change**; version 1 stays
    `c1.generate`.
- **The box (B0's ±3σ on the effective radius) and the exact value are published beside the
  primary reading, as reference readings** (no ordering around the primary is established).

**The argument of principle.**
- A catalog value is a measurement with an error, so a test that treats it as exact claims
  precision the data does not have. A real target's true star is not at its catalog's centre.
- In C1 version 1, every true star is exactly at the centre (`c1.py:77-80` with `c1.py:366`).
  A test that uses the catalog value exactly is then right by construction on synthetic data and
  wrong on real stars. The generator must not reward a convention the real data contradicts.
- The prior is the declared error, unchanged. Drawing the true star from the same distribution
  as the prior makes the prior **match the distribution actually drawn**, and no more than that:
  it does not by itself establish the calibration of any test built on it (that needs its own
  demonstration). The box and the exact value are published beside it **as reference oracle
  readings under their respective stellar assumptions; no ordering around the primary surrogate
  is established** (the drawn star can fall outside the box), so that a reader sees how much a
  result depends on the convention.

## (c) The standard of proof for an infeasibility record

An infeasibility record says that a target cannot be met. **Writing one is a stop that is not
delegated**; this section fixes only what such a record must contain before it can be proposed.

1. **The scope, declared**: either "a battery under the current test rules" or "any admissible
   battery". A record never claims more than its scope.
2. **A population bound**: a statement about the family's rate (the frozen rules are family
   rates), not a per-planet level.
3. **Every exit, each at its price** of section (a). A record that closes one exit leaves D1
   open.
4. **Both sides of the trade-off**: the binaries and the planets, on the same draws.
5. **Two new, disjoint seed sets**, and **the sampling of the confirmation draw included** (the
   record holds for the draw that would decide, not only for the development sets).
6. **Assumptions conservative toward feasibility**: wherever a choice exists, the one that makes
   the target easier to meet.
7. **A statement a reviewer can check without the code**: the inequality, its inputs and its
   assumptions, written out.
8. **Reviews**: Codex's review, an independent review, and the maintainer's approval.

**The argument of principle.** A claim of impossibility must survive the same attack as any
claim. Each element closes a way a false "infeasible" could pass: a scope larger than shown (1); a
per-planet level read as a family rate (2, B0's limit); one exit closed while another stays open
(3); the planets forgotten (4); a development set read as the decision (5); assumptions chosen
against the target (6); an argument only the code can follow (7).

## (d) The prospective route stays open

**The decision.** A prospective route (a new battery version whose test rules may differ from the
current ones) **stays open**, with **every** safeguard of section 5 of the proposal:
1. a **new battery version**, with the **historical locks reproducing unchanged** (the nine locks
   verify at their tags);
2. the **arguments frozen before any number of that version** is computed;
3. an **independent review** that receives the design questions and the code's semantics, but
   **not the targets' forecasts**;
4. **both variants registered and published** (the current rule and the new one), with the
   principled one as the **primary**;
5. **confirmation on new draws**, never drawn before;
6. **the disclosure that the results were known** when the arguments were written;
7. **the targets of the spurious families** (D1 for SE, V1, V2, ER and NR; T2b) as **mandatory
   adversarial checks**. Passing them does not by itself prove that no relaxation occurred
   elsewhere.

**Opening the route decides no design question.** Whether a warning test's INCONCLUSIVE blocks,
the background alternative, and the sinusoid's small-residual requirement are decided only in R2,
after G1, and that decision is a stop that is not delegated.

**The argument of principle.** A rule that is wrong in principle should be correctable, or the
project tests its rules' accidents instead of the claims. A correction made with the results known
cannot be told apart from moving the goalposts, unless it is argued before its numbers, reviewed
blind to the targets, published beside the old rule, and confirmed on draws no one has seen. The
spurious families' targets are one check of that among the seven, necessary and not sufficient.

## (e) The process changes, adopted

The nine changes of section 6 of the proposal are adopted for v0.5.5:

1. **A decision-value test before any run.** A check runs only if **at least two of its outcomes
   are plausible and each changes the next action**. The design states each outcome's prior and
   next action.
2. **A futility rule.** Each check states when it stops early, because its remaining part can no
   longer change its outcome.
3. **The sample sized from the band first.** The design shows, before the run, that the sample
   can reach each decisive outcome (the band's width at the expected counts).
4. **A fixed cap and one pre-approved resume**, with the same code and the same rows. A second
   resume, or any change of code or rows, needs the maintainer.
5. **The price before the freeze.** Each design states its CPU, token and agent-time price from
   measured unit costs, before it is frozen.
6. **One Codex round is enough for mechanical completions** (a resume, a completion script with
   no new rule). Designs and rules keep up to three.
7. **One ledger of floors and margins per target, with units** (what is counted, of what, against
   which allowance), so that two numbers are compared only in the same unit.
8. **The acceptance's sizing is redesigned before it is paid for** (the inherited 2,824 to 3,446
   CPU-h; R4's concern, recorded here).
9. **Messages and inputs are kept per case**, so that no diagnosis needs a rerun to recover them.

**The argument of principle.** Each change removes a waste the records show (the proposal's
section 6, with its citations): runs whose outcome was fixed by design, pilots that foretold the
result, samples too small for their band, repeated completion scripts, prices set after the
freeze, review effort on mechanical steps, numbers compared in different units, and diagnoses that
had to rerun to recover their inputs.
