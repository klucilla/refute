# v0.5.5: R2's design questions (a DRAFT: questions, options and costs only)

**Status: a draft.** Written under the maintainer's decisions of 2026-11-21 at G1, item 4 (#218):
"a draft of R2's design questions (the prospective route of the plan's section 7 and of the
charter (d), amendment 1 (f)), only as a list of questions, options and costs. Nothing is decided,
frozen or locked." **Nothing here is decided, frozen or locked; no option is recommended; R2's
design decisions are a stop that is not delegated.** Whether R2 opens at all is decided at G1.

**Written with results known.** This list was written after v0.5 to v0.5.4's records and R1's
D1-P set A, before T2a-E's and D2-F's results. Charter (d)6 asks R2 to disclose that the results
were known when its arguments were written; this draft is part of what that disclosure covers.

**Sources:** the plan, [`v0.5.5-fable-core-plan.md`](v0.5.5-fable-core-plan.md), sections 4, 5
and 7; the charter, [`v0.5.5-charter.md`](v0.5.5-charter.md), sections (c), (d) and (e);
amendment 1, [`v0.5.5-charter-amendment-1.md`](v0.5.5-charter-amendment-1.md), sections (c), (e)
and (f); the three design questions, [`v0.5.3-design-questions.md`](v0.5.3-design-questions.md),
sections 1 to 4.

**The cost frame** (the plan, section 7, an opinion with ranges, not a measurement): R2 on paper is
0 CPU-h, 0.5 to 1 M review tokens and 3 to 5 agent-days, and stops on "a review that does not
accept". R3 (build and joint forecast) is 150 to 250 CPU-h, 3 to 4 M tokens and 8 to 12
agent-days, and stops if the joint pass probability stays under 0.8 at its cap. R4 (acceptance
and certification) is 1,500 to 4,000 CPU-h. The costs below are the extra each option adds to
that frame, where one can be told; "unpriced" marks what no record prices.

## A. Entry

**A1. Does R2 open?** The plan's pause criteria at G1 (section 7): D1-P closes every exit at its
price and no class-scoped record is accepted; D2-F puts the floor at the allowance under every
admissible variant; T2a-E frees under 40 % and the prospective route stays closed.
- Options: (a) R2 opens for every target whose route needs a rule change (D2, T2a, possibly D1);
  (b) R2 opens only for the targets R1 left on "route"; (c) records first, R2 later; (d) a pause.
- Cost: (a) the full frame above; (b) a smaller paper phase, but R3's joint forecast still needs
  every target and non-regression target on the same fresh curves (the plan, section 7), so R3's
  cost is not reduced in proportion; (c) and (d) cost no CPU.

## B. The three design questions (v0.5.3-design-questions.md, sections 1 to 3)

**B1. Does a warning-severity test's INCONCLUSIVE block the verdict?** (section 1; today it
does, `core/verdict.py`).
- Options: (a) keep blocking; (b) a warning INCONCLUSIVE does not block, with its count disclosed
  in the verdict; (c) it blocks only when the test's coverage falls below a threshold frozen in
  advance; (d) it blocks for some families (those whose spurious targets would pass more) and not
  for others.
- Effects known from the records: most of T2a's losses are warning INCONCLUSIVEs, and D2 would
  pass 98 against 97 without non-sinusoid INCONCLUSIVE blockers (the plan, section 5). That
  knowledge is exactly what charter (d) asks R2 to disclose.
- Cost: on paper, review tokens only; (b) to (d) change every target that counts INCONCLUSIVE, so
  amendment 1 (f) asks both aggregations for D2, D4 and T2a (C4 below).

**B2. The background sub-check's alternative** (section 2; today 0.8 at 0.95).
- Options: (a) keep; (b) an alternative derived from a population argument written before any
  number of the new version; (c) a test of the background with no fixed alternative (a
  different statistic).
- Cost: (b) needs the argument's inputs sourced (a population fact); unpriced in CPU until R3.

**B3. The sinusoid's small-residual requirement** (section 3).
- Options: (a) keep it, with step 7's Fréchet certificate; (b) keep it, with the simulated joint
  probability of the flag and the small residual (D2-F's SIN_J); (c) drop the requirement; (d)
  keep it only where the box's evidence is ambiguous (D2-F's RULE_3 reading).
- The plan (section 5) calls (b) "closer to method than to rule"; whether it is a method change
  (a better computation of the same rule) or a rule change is itself a question for the review.
- Cost: (b) is about 20,000 draws per phase per planet (D2-F's implementation); its CPU in a
  battery run is unpriced.

**B4. The data outside development** (section 4): which sets R2 may read, and which stay unseen
for R3 and R5. Options and their cost belong with C5.

## C. The safeguards of charter (d), made operational

**C1. The new battery version and the nine historical locks.** Charter (d)1: the locks must
reproduce unchanged at their tags.
- Questions: which command verifies all nine; on which environment (the current `uv.lock` or the
  lock of each tag); what a lock that no longer reproduces does to R2 (a stop, a record).
- Cost: unpriced; a full reproduction of nine dossiers may cost CPU-hours, to be priced before R3.

**C2. "The arguments frozen before any number of that version."** Charter (d)2.
- Questions: what counts as "a number of that version" (a unit test's output, a synthetic smoke,
  a pilot); the commit order (argument, review, then code); whether a pilot of the new code's
  cost (process change 5, "the price before the freeze") is allowed before the freeze, and on
  what data.
- Options: (a) no number at all before the freeze, prices from the current version's unit costs;
  (b) cost-only pilots on synthetic data with no verdict output, disclosed.
- Cost: (a) risks a mispriced R3; (b) adds a small pilot (unpriced).

**C3. The blind reviewer** (charter (d)3; amendment 1 (f)).
- Questions: who reviews (a fresh session of which model, or a person); the allowlist of the
  bundle (the design questions, the code's semantics, the frozen rules, the proposed rules); how
  "the code's semantics" is written without forecasts; the bundle's SHA-256 manifest recorded
  before the review; what happens when a bundle is found to hold a forecast (that review is void,
  a new reviewer).
- Cost: one independent review is the main token cost of R2 (inside the 0.5 to 1 M frame);
  a voided review doubles it. A Claude reviewer session needs the maintainer's approval.

**C4. Both variants, both aggregations.** Charter (d)4 (the current rule and the new one, the
principled one as primary); amendment 1 (f) (D2, D4 and T2a under both aggregations).
- Questions: the criterion for "principled" (an argument from the claim's semantics, written
  before numbers); whether the primary can differ per question; how the report shows both.
- Cost: a second aggregation is a recount of the same runs (small CPU); a second rule variant in
  R3 may need its own runs where the variants change what is computed (unpriced).

**C5. Confirmation on new draws** (charter (d)5; amendment 1 (c): generator version 1 for a
confirmation).
- Questions: the seed source (drand rounds are a stop that is not delegated); the set sizes from
  the band first (process change 3); which earlier seeds are excluded; generator version 1 or
  version 2 for R3's joint forecast (amendment 1 (c) keeps version 2 for development only).
- Cost: R5's 50 to 100 CPU-h (the plan); a larger set for a narrower band raises it.

**C6. The disclosure that the results were known** (charter (d)6).
- Questions: its text, where it sits (the version's README, each record), and what it lists (at
  least v0.5 to v0.5.5's records and this draft).
- Cost: none in CPU.

**C7. The spurious families as adversarial checks** (charter (d)7: D1 for SE, V1, V2, ER and NR;
T2b).
- Questions: their thresholds under the new rules (unchanged from the frozen ones, or restated);
  whether a failure of one stops R3; how the record says that passing them "does not by itself
  prove that no relaxation occurred elsewhere".
- Cost: they are part of R3's joint forecast; unpriced apart.

## D. Order, interactions and later phases

**D1. The building order** (the plan, section 4: the `odd_even` estimator first, then the
sinusoid, then D1's test, then the background, then certification) and the dependency table (a
change for one target can hurt another: D1 against D2, D4 and T2a through INCONCLUSIVE blocks;
D2 and T2a against D1 and T2b through any sinusoid change).
- Options: (a) the plan's order; (b) one target at a time, each with its own non-regression set;
  (c) every change at once in one version.
- Cost: (b) repeats R3's joint forecast per step; (c) one forecast, but a failure is harder to
  attribute.

**D2. D1's route.** The plan's overall assessment: D1 by a priced warning-class PASS or a
class-scoped record. R1's D1-P set A was futile, both decisive exits "unresolved" (`d1p-A.json`).
- Options: (a) D1 enters R2 with a new test (priced by charter (a) and amendment 1 (a), 0.05 per
  new test); (b) D1 goes to a class-scoped record (its standard of proof: charter (c) and
  amendment 1 (e), a stop that is not delegated); (c) D1 waits for more evidence (D1-P set B,
  which needs funding).
- Cost: (a) a share of R3; (b) a record's own runs on two new seed sets (unpriced); (c) about
  14.2 CPU-h (D1-P set B's allocation).

**D3. T3b.** Its recount was deferred by the decision-value test; the plan sees it closed by an
availability record or an amended frame.
- Options: (a) an availability record; (b) an amended frame argued in R2; (c) the recount first.
- Cost: (a) and (b) on paper; (c) the recount's CPU (unpriced in R1's designs).

**D4. R3's caps and stop rule, and R4's sizing.** The plan: R3 at 150 to 250 CPU-h, stopping
under a joint pass probability of 0.8 at the cap; process change 8 asks the acceptance sizing
(the inherited 2,824 to 3,446 CPU-h) to be redesigned before R4 is paid for.
- Questions: whether 0.8 stays as R3's threshold; whether the acceptance redesign is part of R2
  or of R3.
- Cost: the redesign is on paper; R4's CPU is the largest cost of the line.

## What this draft does not do

It decides no question, recommends no option, freezes and locks nothing, selects no target and
draws no seed. It changes no rule, no target and no record. The maintainer decides at G1 whether
R2 opens; R2's design decisions are a stop that is not delegated.
